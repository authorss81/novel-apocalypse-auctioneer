# Chapter 903: The ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SEVENTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Makes Stone Said Out Loud At Those Boards That A Piece Of Paper Is Not The Same Thing Twelve Mornings Apart And About Nine People Agreed With The Sentence And Nobody Asked The Clerk A Thing About It And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Seven Lines And Did Not Carry A Thirty-Eighth And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and forty-two days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and one. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and seventeen days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and thirty-one days. Seven hundred and ninety-two days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and eleven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and eighty-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and eighty-four days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and twenty days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and sixty marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and forty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and it is not said out loud again on any morning of this stretch, and that page did not take a line this morning.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and seventeen mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and sixteen. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and sixty-seven mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and eighty-seven, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and twenty-eighth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and forty-second night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty-one of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and thirty-second afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the forty-third morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and two days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank and stood about four feet off the near end of the first table in about four hundred yards of air and did not put a question into any of it, and about four of them have said since that a yard can spend a morning on a thing it has already spent a morning on and still have nothing to show for the morning.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other, and about four of them said it while looking at the form under the clean page and about four of them said it while not, and about four of them have said since that the man who makes the stone was not listening to either lot.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is thirty, and it moved on the one hundred and eightieth morning from twenty-nine, and he said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the boy has now said no figure out loud on more mornings running than he has said one, and that about four of them said he says it louder on the mornings he says it.

---

At about half past ten the man who makes the stone put his chisel down on the low wall, which he does not do while he is working, and stood with his hand flat on the stone the way the man of fifty-six stands with his hand flat on the stone, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at him and nobody said anything.

---

Then he said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, in about nineteen people's hearing, and he said it to the form and not to the clerk.

---

"I have been at the foot of this wall twelve mornings," he said, "and a piece of paper is not the same thing twelve mornings apart. That is not a complaint about anybody. It is only that a change in the hand is not a figure."

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet agreed with the sentence, one after another, out loud, in about nineteen people's hearing, and about four of them have said since that it is the first morning of this stretch on which about nine people in that yard agreed out loud with a sentence about a page, and that not one person has asked the clerk about it, and that about four of them said agreeing with a thing is not the same as asking a person whether it is so and that about four of them have not said which of those two they would rather have done.

---

And the clerk was standing at the boards with her hand on the clean page and she heard all of that and she said nothing, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clerk who has said no four times without a reason in that yard has not said one word this morning to a thing about nine people agreed with, and that about four of them said she was not asked, and that about four of them have said since that nobody in that yard has yet worked out a way of asking her a thing that is not a question.

---

The man at the end of the second table said one thing out loud about it, in the ordinary voice, and nobody answered him, and he has been at that table long enough that nobody in that yard counts it.

---

"Stone does not change either," he said. "You would not hold a thing to a man for that."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man at the end of a table answered a sentence about paper with a sentence about stone, and that about four of them said both of them are true and that about four of them said two true things put next to each other do not make a third thing, and that about four of them have said since that the yard has now got a thing it agrees with and a thing it agrees with and nothing whatever in between them.

---

Then the man of about thirty-four said nothing at all, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who knows three people and would not name them was in that yard for the whole of the agreeing and said nothing, and that about four of them said the three of them would have been in it, and that about four of them have not said why they said that about three people nobody has named.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack of something at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and forty-second night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty-one of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the seventy-sixth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched a clerk do that on a morning when about nine people in that yard had agreed out loud with a sentence about a page, and about four of them have said since that a thing agreed with and not asked at is the ordinary shape of that yard now, and that about four of them said the page at the back has never taken a line for anything anybody agreed with.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning and not one of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a thing agreed with out loud is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a person agreeing with a sentence about paper does not move it. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and two days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and two days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet agreed with a sentence this morning and not one of them asked the clerk about it.
