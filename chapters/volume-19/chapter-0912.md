# Chapter 912: The ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-SIXTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Makes Things Round Said Three Figures Out Loud In One Mouth At About Two In About Nineteen People's Hearing And Did Not Join Them And About Four People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Tried To Join Them And About Four Of Them Have Said Since That Not Joining Them Is Not The Same As Not Seeing The Join And About Four Of Them Have Not Said Which Of Those Two It Is And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Seven Lines And It Did Not Carry A Thirty-Eighth And It Is Not Going To And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and fifty-one days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and ten. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and twenty-six days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and forty days. Eight hundred and one days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and fifteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and twenty days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and ninety days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and ninety-three days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and twenty-nine days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and sixty-nine marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and fifty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and it is not said out loud again on any morning of this stretch, and that page did not take a line this morning.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and twenty-six mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and twenty-five. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and seventy-six mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and ninety-six, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and thirty-seventh of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fifty-first night of that run, having slept on five hundred and fifty of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and forty-first afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the fifty-second morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and eleven days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards went up after the light found the eleven feet, and the light lay along the top of the second table for a minute before it reached the boards, and the man who makes things round carried his own bench out from under the lean-to and set it down where he sets it and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not speak to him. The form was still under the clean page at about four feet off the near end of the first table with the space on it empty, and about four of them have said since that nobody in that yard has looked at the back of that form this morning and that about four of them said the front of it is the part people look at.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other, and the man of about thirty-four was at the near end of the second table with his hands in his pockets and did not say anything to anybody.

---

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then he said no figure, and said so out loud, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard the word *no* come out of that yard at about ten to eight and about four of them have said since that he has said no figure out loud on a good many mornings and that on those mornings nobody has asked him for one. The morning went by the way it goes. The man who digs loam stayed at the north end with his barrow and did not come down to the boards. The woman who keeps a scale brought her weights out and stacked them and went back inside and came out again, which is what she does.

---

At about eleven a woman of about fifty-two came down that bank and stood at about four feet off the near end of the first table and looked at the form under the clean page for longer than she looked at it on the morning she asked about it, and she asked nothing this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a person who asked a question out loud in that yard and was refused has come down that bank twice since and has not asked a second thing, and that about four of them have not said whether she is waiting for something. And she went back up that bank at about half past eleven and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her go up it and nobody said anything while she was going.

---

At about two the man who makes things round took the tool to a piece of ash that was standing on end in the clamps, and the shaving came off it in one long ribbon and curled on the ground at his feet, and about four people at the top of eleven feet stopped what they were doing for the length of it, which is about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall. And then he stopped the wheel and stood with his hand on the piece and said four things out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing, and he said them one after another and he did not put anything between them.

---

"There are four places in this district where those three lines can be read," he said.

---

"The number of documents this district does not own is four," he said.

---

"And the number of conditions with no end on it is four," he said.

---

"And a figure has stood at four for as long as anybody in this yard has been counting," he said.

---

And he put his hand back on the ash and started the wheel again and the ribbon came off it and curled on the ground, and about nineteen people in that yard had heard all four of those and not one of them had heard him say a thing that joined them.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who makes things round has now said out loud the three fours and the three fours and the four, and that about four of them said not joining them is not the same as not seeing the join, and that about four of them have not said which of those two it is. And about four people at the top of eleven feet tried, and about four of them have said since that the man of about thirty-four got as far as saying *so that would be one for each of* and then stopped with the rest of it in his mouth and shut it, and that the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said *if you put them end to end* and did not finish that either, and that about four of them said the turner did not help either of them and did not stop them either.

---

And the man at the end of the second table said nothing at all about any of it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who said out loud on the one hundred and ninety-fourth morning that a figure is a morning and not an answer has had nothing to say this morning about a man saying four numbers in about nineteen people's hearing, and that about four of them said he was at the end of the table with his back to it.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a bar of iron at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fifty-first night of that run, having slept on five hundred and fifty of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the eighty-fifth time running, and nobody asked him anything, and nobody in that yard said out loud that he was said to have said something else. At about four the man who makes things round swept his shavings into his hand and carried them to the edge of that bank and put them over it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who said three fours out loud at about two has swept his own floor at about four, and that about four of them said nobody has asked him whether the four he said at about two are the four on that wall.

---

Then the clerk wrote the morning on the page at the back of that lot book in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man put three figures in one mouth at about two and a page at the back of a book took nothing at about four, and that about four of them said the page has only ever been for one kind of asking and that this was not it. At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up, and about four of them have said since that nothing went on that page this morning and that the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it is not going to carry a thirty-eighth because nobody in that yard has asked the man of fifty-six anything.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning and not one of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and three fours said out loud in about nineteen people's hearing is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and saying a figure out loud is not a page coming down that lane. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and eleven days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and eleven days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and at about two this morning a man who makes things round said three fours out loud in about nineteen people's hearing and did not join them, and nothing went on that page for it.
