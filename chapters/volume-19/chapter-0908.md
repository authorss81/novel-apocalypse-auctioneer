# Chapter 908: The ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-SECOND Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month About Nineteen People Stood At The Foot Of That Bank And About Nineteen Of Them Could Have Asked One Person Something In About Four Hundred Yards Of Air And Not One Of Them Did And A Man At The End Of The Second Table Came Close To It And Did Not And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Seven Lines And Did Not Carry A Thirty-Eighth And Nobody Thanked Anybody For It

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and forty-seven days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and six. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and twenty-two days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and thirty-six days. Seven hundred and ninety-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and eleven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and sixteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and eighty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and eighty-nine days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and twenty-five days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and sixty-five marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and fifty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and it is not said out loud again on any morning of this stretch, and that page did not take a line this morning.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and twenty-two mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and twenty-one. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and seventy-two mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and ninety-two, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and thirty-third of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and forty-seventh night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty-six of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and thirty-seventh afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the forty-eighth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and seven days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down, and about four of them have said since that about nineteen people came down that bank this morning with a thing they could have asked, and that about four of them have not said who told them there was one.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other, and the man who digs loam went out off the north end with his barrow and stayed out there, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who asked that yard two things out loud in ten mornings put the north end of that yard between himself and the boards this morning and stayed there all day, and that about four of them said a man can be in a yard all day and not once be in the middle of it.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is thirty, and it moved on the one hundred and eightieth morning from twenty-nine, and he said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a boy of seventeen is the only person in that yard who puts a number into about four hundred yards of air every morning without being asked to and that nobody has ever asked him what it is about.

---

At about half past ten about nine people at the top of eleven feet were standing at about four feet off the near end of the second table within reach of a person in about four hundred yards of air, and not one of them said anything to her, and about four of them have said since that a yard that has spent mornings learning how to put a question into the air can spend one morning not putting one, and that about four of them said nobody in that yard has worked out yet whether those are the same kind of thing.

---

And then the man at the end of the second table put his hand on the table and got up, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at him, and he stood there at about four feet off the near end of that table with his hand on it for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and he did not ask her anything, and he sat down again.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that he came close to it and did not, and that about four of them said the close is the part they will keep, and that about four of them have said since that a man at the end of a table who has never once been asked a question in that yard got up this morning on the strength of a clerk saying a thing out loud yesterday and then sat down again, and that about four of them said nothing about it happened.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a harder thing happened in that yard on the one hundred and seventy-eighth morning, when the man who digs loam asked out loud whether anybody had read that page, and that about four of them said the asking that morning took the whole yard about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall before anybody in that yard would come near it, and that about four of them said the not-asking on this morning took the whole morning and nobody in that yard cost anything by it.

---

And then a man of thirty-eight said one thing out loud, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, in about nineteen people's hearing, and it was not about the page and it was not about what he went to look at.

---

"Nobody has asked me what I went up that lane to see," he said, "and I am not going to wait to be asked and then say it. A man who has said he has not got it yet has said the whole of it, and that yard can go on this morning without another word out of me about it."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that about nine people in that yard stood in about four hundred yards of air this morning with a thing they could have said and did not say it, and that about four of them said the hardest of them was a man who has come back down that hill with something in him and a clerk who has said a thing out loud that nobody can use.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack of something at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and forty-seventh night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty-six of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the eighty-first time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and that page carries thirty-seven lines and none of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched a clerk do that on a morning when not one of them had asked her for anything at all, and about four of them have said since that nobody in that yard thanked anybody for anything this morning, and that about four of them said the man who got up and sat down again did not do it in front of anybody so that they would thank him, and that about four of them said he is not a man who is thanked.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning and not one of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and nineteen people not asking one person something is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a morning on which nobody asks anybody anything is not a page coming down that lane. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and seven days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and seven days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and about nine people in that yard have said since that nobody thanked anybody for this morning and that about four of them said nobody should have to be thanked for a morning like this one and that about four of them have not said why they think that.
