# Chapter 896: The ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTIETH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Page Came Down That Lane At About A Quarter To Ten In The Own Hand Of A Young Woman Who Came Down With Nothing Else And She Gave It To Nobody In Particular And The Clerk Read It Out Loud At Those Boards And Entered Nothing And It Has A Heading And A Rule And One Space In It For A Name And The Lane Count Moved From Twenty-Nine To Thirty And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and thirty-five days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and ninety-four. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and ten days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and twenty-four days. Seven hundred and eighty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and ninety-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and seventy-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and seventy-seven days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and thirteen days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and fifty-three marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and thirty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move on the morning a page came down that lane and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and ten mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and nine. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and sixty mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and eighty, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it moved this morning from twenty-nine and it has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and twenty-first of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-fifth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-four of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and twenty-fifth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the thirty-sixth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood ninety-five days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said out loud at those boards, before he said a figure, that a page came down that lane this morning, and the lane count is thirty and it moved this morning from twenty-nine, and then he said a figure, and he said it before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down, and about four of them have said since that a yard that has spent three mornings finding out who would carry a thing up a hill spends the fourth morning in the ordinary way, and about four of them said it has been a while since that happened.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment, and the man who mends fencing said nothing and went on with the wire in his hand, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that there has been wire in that yard every morning of this for as long as that bank has been standing, and that about four of them said a yard that reads four figures off a wall has never once counted the things it is holding.

---

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is twenty-nine, and it has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and he said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the count of that lane has been the same figure for so long that a boy of seventeen says it out loud without looking at anything.

---

And then at about a quarter to ten a young woman came the rest of the way down that lane with a page folded once in her own hand and nothing else on her, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet saw her come over the top of that bank before anybody in that yard had said anything about her, and about four of them have said since that nobody in that yard has seen anybody come down that lane with one thing and no second thing since the last time the count moved.

---

She folded it once and gave it to nobody in particular and stood at the foot of that bank with her hands showing afterwards, and about four people at the top of eleven feet asked her whether she wanted it put somewhere and she said no out loud, and about four of them have said since that a young woman who has come down nine miles with a page in her own hand said no to four people at once and did not wait to be asked twice.

---

Then Orren Pike said out loud, at those boards, before he said a figure, that the page had come down that lane, and the lane count is thirty and it moved this morning from twenty-nine, and then he said a figure, and he said it before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

"Two hundred and one," he said, at the near end of the first table.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet said that after him out of step with each other and about four of them have said since that a boy of seventeen says a figure out loud on the morning the lane count moves and that about four of them said he has never done both on one morning before and about four of them have not said what the two figures have to do with each other.

---

Then the clerk read it out loud at those boards, in about nineteen people's hearing, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, and she read all of it, and she entered nothing.

---

"It is a form," she said. "There is a heading and there is a rule and there is one space on it. That is the whole of what is on it and I have read all of it and I have put nothing on anything."

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at it and about four of them said out loud that the space on it is for a name, and about four of them said the heading over it is not a heading anybody in that yard has seen, and about four of them said the rule under it is straight and ruled the same way as the column on that form, and about four of them have said since that the yard has two pieces of paper on its own boards this morning and that about four of them said the second one arrived at about a quarter to ten and nobody knows whose hand it was in before it was in hers.

---

Nobody filled it, and nobody was asked to fill it, and the clerk did not ask and the boy did not ask, and a man of thirty-eight was not asked, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the easiest thing in that yard this morning would have been for one person to say a name out loud and read it back, and that about four of them said that is not the same as filling a space and that about four of them have said they do not know what it is.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that this district has made fourteen things and did not make a fifteenth this morning on the day a page came down that lane, and that about four of them said a form is not a thing anybody in this district made.

---

About half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and at about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-fifth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-four of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the sixty-ninth time running, and nobody asked him what any of them is for.

---

Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it, and about four of them have said since that a page came down that lane this morning and the page at the back of that lot book did not move, and that about four of them said that is the second time in this stretch that a thing has arrived and nothing has been entered for it, and that about four of them said the first time was on the one hundred and seventy-fifth morning when a man went up that lane with nothing in his hand.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and about four people out of about nineteen said the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up, and the form was still lying at about four feet off the near end of the first table when the light stopped being able to make it out.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning and not one of the thirty-six is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move this morning, and a page coming down that lane is not a fifteenth of it. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a form with one space on it is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it moved this morning from twenty-nine, because somebody came down that lane with a page in her own hand, and it is not going to move again for anything this yard does at those boards. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty too. The record has stood ninety-five days and nobody in that yard has asked what it is ninety-five days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
