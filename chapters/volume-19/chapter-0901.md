# Chapter 901: The ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FIFTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Record On Those Boards Stood One Hundred Days And Nobody In That Yard Asked What It Is One Hundred Days Of And About Four People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Noticed That Figure And Did Not Ask And The Form Was Still Under The Clean Page With A Space On It Nobody Is Going To Fill And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Seven Lines And Did Not Carry A Thirty-Eighth And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and forty days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and ninety-nine. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and fifteen days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and twenty-nine days. Seven hundred and ninety days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and seventy-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and eighty-two days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and eighteen days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and fifty-eight marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and forty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and it is not said out loud again on any morning of this stretch, and that page did not take a line this morning.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and fifteen mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and fourteen. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and sixty-five mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and eighty-five, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and twenty-sixth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fortieth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-nine of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and thirtieth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the forty-first morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to eight the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and the form was lying where it has lain for six mornings, under the clean page, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, and the space on it is empty, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down and not one of them said anything about a piece of paper.

---

About four of them have said since that a page which came down a lane in a young woman's own hand on the first of those six mornings is not the same object as a page which has lain on a board for six, and that about four of them said the first one had a person in it and the sixth one does not.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other, and the woman who keeps a scale was still packing her weights into the corner of that bank and did not turn round, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a morning on which nobody is going to ask that man anything is a morning he gets his four out earlier, and that about four of them said he does not know that.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is thirty, and it moved on the one hundred and eightieth morning from twenty-nine, and he said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the boy has said no figure on more mornings of this stretch than he has said one, and that about four of them said nobody in that yard has asked him to say it and that about four of them said nobody has told him not to.

---

And then the man of about forty-one came the rest of the way down that bank, which he has done twice in this stretch, and went and stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table with his back to the boards and did not say anything at all, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that he has come down that hill more in this stretch than in any ten mornings before it and that nobody in that yard has asked him about that either.

---

At about eleven the yard was as full as it gets, with about nine people at the top of eleven feet standing in about four hundred yards of air between the second table and the boards, and about four of them said the figure out loud, one after another, out of step with each other, the way they say a figure, and nobody said anything else about it.

---

Nobody asked what it is one hundred days of, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that they noticed it and did not ask, and that is the whole of it, and that about four of them have said since that they were the same on this morning as they were on the one before it, and that about four of them said they do not think that is a coincidence, and that about four of them have not said why they do not think it.

---

The man at the end of the second table heard all four of those figures said out loud and said nothing about them, and the man of about thirty-four heard them at the near end of that table and said nothing either, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that two men in that yard have said nothing out loud on the same morning for eleven mornings running and that nobody is keeping that.

---

And then the man who digs loam came up off the north end with his barrow and put it down at about four feet off the near end of the second table, the way he puts it down, and did not ask anything, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who asked that yard two things out loud in ten mornings and got nine refusals to the second of them has not asked a third.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack of something at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that nobody in that yard has asked her what is in the sack and that about four of them said nobody in that yard is going to.

---

About half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fortieth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-nine of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the seventy-fourth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

And then a man of thirty-eight said one thing out loud at about ten past four, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, in about nineteen people's hearing, and he said it before the light went off the boards.

---

"I said a thing out loud in this yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning," he said, "and I am not going to say it a second time, and nobody thanked me for saying it the first time and nobody is going to be thanked for it. A man who says a thing out loud twice in the same stretch is a man who has started wanting something for it, and I have not started wanting anything."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a fact said out loud in that yard is a morning and not a page, and that about four of them have said since that the thing a man of thirty-eight said three mornings ago is still in his mouth and is not on anything anybody has written, and that about four of them have not said what it is instead.

---

And then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it, and about four of them have said since that a clerk wrote a morning down in her own hand on the morning a figure in that yard stood on a number it had never stood on, and that about four of them said she did not say the figure out loud and did not look at the boards while she wrote it.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning and not one of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a form lying under a clean page for six mornings is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and it is not moved by a decision. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and about four people at the top of eleven feet noticed the figure and did not ask, and that is the whole of the hundredth morning.
