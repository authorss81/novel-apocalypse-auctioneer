# Chapter 909: The ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-THIRD Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Yard Asked The Clerk Out Loud At About Four Feet Off The Near End Of The Second Table In About Nineteen People's Hearing To Read The Page At The Back Of That Lot Book And She Said Out Loud That She Is A Clerk And Not A Person Who Finds Out And That Was The Whole Of What She Said And Nobody In That Yard Decided Anything About It And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Seven Lines And Did Not Carry A Thirty-Eighth And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and forty-eight days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and seven. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and twenty-three days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and thirty-seven days. Seven hundred and ninety-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and twelve days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and seventeen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and eighty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and ninety days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and twenty-six days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and sixty-six marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and fifty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and it is not said out loud again on any morning of this stretch, and that page did not take a line this morning.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and twenty-three mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and twenty-two. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and seventy-three mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and ninety-three, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and thirty-fourth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and forty-eighth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty-seven of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and thirty-eighth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the forty-ninth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and eight days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said a figure out loud before the clerk's hand had moved, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and the man who digs loam came up off the north end with his barrow and did not go back down to the north end when he got to the boards, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who put a corner of that yard between himself and those boards for the whole of a morning came back into the middle of it this morning, and that about four of them have not said what he came back for.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet then stood about four feet off the near end of the second table in about four hundred yards of air and waited, and about four of them have said since that about nine people in that yard had been getting ready to say something for two mornings and that about four of them said nobody would admit to being the one it was ready for.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is thirty, and it moved on the one hundred and eightieth morning from twenty-nine, and then he said a figure, and he said it before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

"Two hundred and seventeen," he said, at the near end of the first table.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and about four of them have said since that the boy said a figure on the same morning that about nine people in that yard were standing about four feet off the near end of the second table not saying anything, and that about four of them said a number is a thing a boy can say while a yard is deciding what it is going to do, and that about four of them have said since that a yard deciding what it is going to do is not a decision and that nobody in that yard has decided anything this morning.

---

Then at about eleven the man who digs loam put his barrow down at about four feet off the near end of the second table and asked the clerk a question out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard every word of it and nobody said anything while he asked it.

---

"Read the page at the back of that book," he said.

---

And the clerk said one thing back to him, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and that was the whole of what she said.

---

"I am a clerk and not a person who finds out," she said.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who asked that yard on the one hundred and seventy-eighth morning whether anybody had read that page came back to that table this morning and asked the person who keeps it to read it, and that about four of them said the asking took about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and that about four of them said that is the whole of what he did.

---

And nobody asked her a second thing, and nobody in that yard said out loud that she could be asked again, and nobody in that yard said out loud that she could not, and about four people at the top of eleven feet got as far as opening their mouths on that and shut them, and about four of them have said since that nobody in that yard has said whether that question will be put again in that yard, and that about four of them said not saying it is not a rule against it and that about four of them said not saying it is not a rule for it either.

---

And she said nothing else this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clerk who has said no four times out loud without a reason and who said a thing out loud about that page two mornings ago has now said one sentence to a request made out loud to her and has not said a word since, and that about four of them said the sentence was the same sentence she gave that yard on the one hundred and seventy-seventh morning with two words of it different, and that about four of them have said since that is the whole of what happened in that yard this morning and that about four of them have not said what they make of it.

---

And the man of about thirty-four was at the near end of the second table for all of it and said nothing at all, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who knows three people and would not say which three heard a request made out loud and a flat sentence back and said nothing, and that about four of them said the three of them would have said something and that about four of them have not said how they know that about three people nobody has named.

---

The man of about forty-one came down that bank at about half past eleven and stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table and looked at the boards, and he said nothing, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who said out loud on the one hundred and eighty-eighth morning that a page goes down that lane said nothing this morning at about the time a person was asked to read one, and that about four of them have said since that a direction and a request are not the same kind of thing and that nobody has put them in the same place yet.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a bar of iron at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and forty-eighth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty-seven of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the eighty-second time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and that page carries thirty-seven lines and none of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clerk who was asked out loud this morning to read that page wrote the morning on it in her own hand at about four o'clock and took no line for the asking either, and that about four of them said a line on that page has only ever been for an asking of the man of fifty-six and that this was not that.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning and not one of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a request made out loud and refused out loud is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and being asked to read a page is not a page coming down a lane. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and eight days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and eight days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and a request was made out loud at about four feet off the near end of the second table this morning and a clerk said one sentence back to it and nobody in that yard decided anything about whether it can be made again.
