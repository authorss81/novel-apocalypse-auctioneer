# Chapter 891: The ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FIFTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of Thirty-Eight Went Up That Lane Before There Was Light With Nothing In His Hand And Knocked At About Ten At A Time When Somebody Was In And Was Given Nothing For It And Nothing Came Down That Lane And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and thirty days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and eighty-nine. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and five days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and nineteen days. Seven hundred and eighty days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and ninety-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and ninety-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and sixty-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and seventy-two days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and eight days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and forty-eight marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and thirty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and five mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and four. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and fifty-five mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and seventy-five, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since, and a person went up that lane this morning with nothing in his hand and nothing came down it. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and sixteenth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirtieth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty-nine of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and twentieth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the thirty-first morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood ninety days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said a figure out loud before the clerk's hand had moved, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and the corner of that yard where a man of thirty-eight stands was empty, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet noticed it inside the first half hour, and about four of them have said since that he had said on the evening before at what time he would knock and that he knocked at about the time he said, and that about four of them said he did not say which time he would knock and about four of them have not said what made the difference.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment, and the yard was about as full as it gets, and nine miles from that bank a man of thirty-eight was on a step with his hands showing.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is twenty-nine, and it has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and he said a figure after it, and he said it before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

"One hundred and ninety-six," he said, at the near end of the first table.

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet said that after him out of step with each other and about four of them have said since that a boy of seventeen has said that figure out loud on four mornings running and that nobody in that yard has said out loud what any of them is about, and that about four of them said it is about a man who was not in that yard yesterday and about four of them said it is about nothing in particular, and that those are the same three people.

---

Nine miles up that lane there is a step and a rail and a door with a frame round it, and at about ten a man of thirty-eight knocked on that frame and somebody was in, which is what he said he would do, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who says out loud that he will come back down and say so if nobody is in has put himself in a place where the coming back down is the part that gets watched, and the door was answered by a woman, and she did not ask him his name and he did not give her one, and there was a table in that room with one thing on it that was not a book, and nobody offered him a chair and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who is not offered a chair says a great deal less than a man who is.

---

"You have come down that bank every morning," she said. "I have the count of it. I want the page at the back of that lot book."

---

And he said no out loud, in the ordinary voice, at about the door, and he gave no reason, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who has stood in a yard for one hundred and seventy-five mornings has now said no out loud twice in three days, and that about four of them said the first one was about a question and this one was about a piece of paper.

---

"Who writes on it," she said.

---

And he said no out loud a second time, in the same words and at the same speed, and she did not ask him a third thing and said nothing about his coming again, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a refusal of two things in one morning takes about as long as reading four figures off a wall, and then he came back over that bank at about eleven and he was neither early nor late, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched a man of thirty-eight come down eleven feet of bank with his hands showing, and the woman who keeps a scale looked at his hands and went back to her own morning, and then he said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, in about nineteen people's hearing, and he said it once and after that he said nothing more about that hill all day.

---

"A woman at a table nine miles up that lane asked me for the page at the back of that lot book and I said no, and she asked me who writes on it and I said no, and that is the whole of it and I am not going to be thanked for it," he said.

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet wanted to know what else she said, and about four of them said it out loud, and he said he was not going to say what she said after the second no, and about four of them asked what her face was doing and he said he was not going to say that either, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man of thirty-eight has come down that bank with a fact in it for the first time, and that the fact is two refusals, and that about four of them said two refusals is not a thing anybody in that yard has known what to do with.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a bar of iron at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and at about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirtieth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty-nine of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the sixty-fourth time running, and nobody asked him what any of them is for.

---

Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it and about four of them have said since that a clerk wrote down a morning on which a man said no to two people who were not in that yard, and that about four of them said the morning is named and not counted, and that about four of them said the yard has now got a thing it cannot put anywhere, and that about four of them said the page at the back takes a line when somebody asks in that yard and this was not asked in that yard, and that nobody at the top of eleven feet has said one word about what should be done with it, and that nobody in that yard has a reason.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said it this morning and did not go up.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning and not one of the thirty-six is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move, and a person went up that lane this morning with nothing in his hand and nothing came down it, and it is not going to move for anything this yard does at those boards. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled. The record has stood ninety days and nobody in that yard has asked what it is ninety days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
