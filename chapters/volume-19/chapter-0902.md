# Chapter 902: The ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SIXTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Boards Carry Eight Hundred And Nobody In That Yard Was Looking At Them When They Did And A Woman Of About Fifty-Two Asked The Clerk Out Loud What Would Have To Be True For That Space On That Form To Be Filled In And She Said No Out Loud And Gave No Reason And About Four People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Have Said Since That A No Without A Reason Has Stopped Being An Answer And Started Being A Wall And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Seven Lines And Did Not Carry A Thirty-Eighth And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and forty-one days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and sixteen days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and thirty days. Seven hundred and ninety-one days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and ten days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and eighty days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and eighty-three days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and nineteen days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and fifty-nine marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and forty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and it is not said out loud again on any morning of this stretch, and that page did not take a line this morning.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and sixteen mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and fifteen. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and sixty-six mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and eighty-six, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and twenty-seventh of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and forty-first night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and thirty-first afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the forty-second morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and one days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said a figure out loud before the clerk's hand had moved, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and the man who makes the stone was already at the near end of that wall with a chisel and a piece of stone and no particular hurry, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart came down the bank with the shafts up and stood about four feet off the near end of the second table until somebody took the horse away, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down at the hour they come down. Nobody in that yard read the figure off those boards out loud this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a board has gone over a round number without anybody looking at it, and that about four of them said that is what a board is for, and that about four of them have not said what a board is for.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other, and the woman who keeps a scale said good morning out loud to nobody in particular, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that she says good morning to that yard and not to the yard, and that about four of them said that is not a complaint.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is thirty, and it moved on the one hundred and eightieth morning from twenty-nine, and then he said a figure, and he said it before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

"Two hundred and thirteen," he said, at the near end of the first table.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and about four of them have said since that the boy says a figure on about three mornings in ten and says no figure out loud on the others, and that about four of them said the seven mornings he says no figure are the mornings anybody in that yard would want to see a figure on, and that nobody has ever asked him to say one and nobody has ever told him not to.

---

Then at about ten a woman of about fifty-two came the rest of the way down that bank, which she has done once before in this stretch of mornings, and that about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that nobody in that yard has said out loud why she comes down that hill.

---

She went to the form, which is lying under the clean page at about four feet off the near end of the first table, and looked at the space on it for a while, and then she went to where the clerk was standing and asked her a question out loud, in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, in about nineteen people's hearing, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet went quiet while she asked it.

---

"What would have to be true," she said, "for that space to be filled in."

---

And the clerk said no out loud, in the ordinary voice, and she gave no reason, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard both of those things, and the woman of about fifty-two did not ask again and stood where she was for a while. And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clerk who has said no three times without a reason has this morning said no a fourth time to a question nobody in that yard had thought of putting, and that about four of them said the question was a good one and a good question with a no without a reason is the plainest thing in that yard this month.

---

And then about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a no without a reason has stopped being an answer and started being a wall, and that about four of them have not said which of those two words is worse, and that about four of them said the wall is the one you can put a hand on and that about four of them said the answer is the one that was there before the wall went up.

---

And a man of thirty-eight said one thing out loud about that word, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, in about nineteen people's hearing, and he said it before the light had come off the boards, and nobody agreed with him.

---

"You have it the wrong way round," he said. "A wall is what is left when the last person who could answer has gone up a hill for the winter, and this morning there were about nineteen people at the foot of that bank and every one of them could have answered her and not one of them did, and that is not a wall. That is a yard."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man of thirty-eight told a yard of about nineteen people that it was not a wall by telling it what it was instead, and that about four of them said he was not agreed with, and that about four of them have not said by whom, and that about four of them said a thing said in that yard on a morning like this does not get agreed with before the light goes off the boards. And the man of about thirty-four was at the near end of the second table when the woman of about fifty-two asked her question and heard the answer, and he said nothing, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who said out loud on the one hundred and seventy-ninth morning that he knows three people and would not say which three was in about nineteen people's hearing this morning and said nothing at all, and that about four of them have said since that whatever those three would have said is still not said.

---

The woman of about fifty-two went up that bank at about half past eleven and did not stay to hear anything else, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that she came down that hill once this stretch and asked one question and went back up it, and that she is the only person who has ever gone back up that lane the same morning she came down it.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a bar of iron at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and forty-first night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the seventy-fifth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and that page carries thirty-seven lines and none of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it, and about four of them have said since that a clerk was asked a fourth question out loud this morning, gave the same answer and took no line, and that about four of them said the line on that page has only ever been for an asking and that this morning had two things in it and neither of them was that.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning and not one of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a no said out loud a fourth time is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a question put to a clerk at about four feet off the near end of the second table does not move it. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well. The record has stood one hundred and one days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and one days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and the boards carried eight hundred this morning and about nine people in that yard were working.
