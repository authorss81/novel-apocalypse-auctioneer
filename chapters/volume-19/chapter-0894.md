# Chapter 894: The ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-EIGHTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Man Who Digs Loam Asked Out Loud In About Nine People's Hearing Whether Anybody In That Yard Has Read The Page At The Back Of That Book And About Four People Said That They Have Not And He Said He Has Not Either And Is Not Going To And Nobody Asked Him Why And The Page At The Back Did Not Move And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and thirty-three days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and ninety-two. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and eight days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and twenty-two days. Seven hundred and eighty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and ninety-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and seventy-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and seventy-five days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and eleven days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and fifty-one marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and thirty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and eight mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and seven. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and fifty-eight mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and seventy-eight, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and nineteenth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-third night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-two of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and twenty-third afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the thirty-fourth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood ninety-three days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said a figure out loud before the clerk's hand had moved, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down and not one of them was early, and that is the first morning in this stretch of ten on which about nine people in that yard have come down eleven feet of bank with nothing in front of them.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other and did not look at the boards.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is twenty-nine, and it has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and then he said a figure, and he said it before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

"One hundred and ninety-nine," he said, at the near end of the first table.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other and about four of them have said since that the man who reads figures off a wall twice a morning has not been asked about any of them this month and that about four of them said the boy is being asked about a thing nobody has asked the man about, and that about four of them have not said what that is.

---

The man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart said out loud at about ten that he had been down to the siding before there was light and that the train on that siding had not moved and had not been moved, and the man who makes the stone said nothing and went on with the chisel, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a morning with a cart on it and a chisel on it and a figure off a wall is the shape of every morning in that yard and that about four of them said it is the first one this stretch that nothing else happened on the boards at all.

---

Then at about half past ten the man who digs loam came up off the north end with his barrow and put it down at about four feet off the near end of the second table and asked a question out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about nine people's hearing.

---

"Has anybody in this yard read the page at the back of that book," he said.

---

And about four people said that they have not, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said that they have not heard of anybody who has, and the man who digs loam said that he has not either and that he is not going to.

---

And then nobody said anything, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who has stood at the foot of that bank every morning of this and has never once asked anything out loud asked something out loud this morning and put his barrow down before he asked it, and that about four of them said he is a man who has been in that yard longer than the second table, and that about four of them said nobody has asked him why he asked that one.

---

About half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and about four of them have said since that on a morning with a question in it the number got said exactly as it gets said on a morning with nothing in it, and that about four of them said that is the plainest thing anybody has said in that yard for a while.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and at about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-third night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-two of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nine people heard it this morning for the sixty-seventh time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched a clerk do that on a morning when somebody asked a question out loud in that yard, and about four of them have said since that the morning was asked about a page and not about the man at the foot of that wall, and that about four of them have said since that the page at the back of that book takes a line when the man of fifty-six is asked something and that a man who digs loam asking a question about a page is not that, and that about four of them said the clerk did not say that and about four of them said nobody asked her.

---

And then a man of thirty-eight said one thing out loud at about ten past four, in about nineteen people's hearing, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, and he said it before the light went off the boards.

---

"I have not read that page either," he said. "And I am not going to stand up here and say I have, and I am not going to stand up here and say I have not, and a man who digs loam has asked that yard out loud this morning and about four of them said no and I have not got a reason to say a fifth thing about it."

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said it this morning and did not go up.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning and not one of the thirty-six is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move, and a question about that page does not move it. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled. The record has stood ninety-three days and nobody in that yard has asked what it is ninety-three days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
