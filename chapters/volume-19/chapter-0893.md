# Chapter 893: The ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SEVENTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month About Nine People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Said Out Loud One At A Time That The Page At The Back Of That Lot Book Is Not The Lot Book And A Clerk Said Out Loud That She Is A Clerk Of The Lot Book And Not Of Anything Behind It And The Man At The End Of The Second Table Said One Thing And Nobody Answered Him And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and thirty-two days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and ninety-one. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and seven days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and twenty-one days. Seven hundred and eighty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and ninety-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and seventy-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and seventy-four days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and ten days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and fifty marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and thirty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and seven mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and six. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and fifty-seven mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and seventy-seven, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and eighteenth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-second night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-one of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and twenty-second afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the thirty-third morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood ninety-two days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet were standing at the near end of the second table at about half past six, which is earlier than they stood yesterday, and about four of them have said since that a yard does not come down eleven feet of bank before light to do nothing and that about four of them said there is a thing they want to be able to say out loud about a door nine miles away.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment, and nobody at the foot of that bank turned round, and about four people have said since that a man can be at the foot of that wall saying four figures while nine people behind him are saying something else out loud.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is twenty-nine, and it has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and he said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the lane count and the figure are two different things a boy of seventeen says and that about four of them have stopped waiting for the second one, and then Mavis Dorr asked, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing, whether the page at the back of that lot book is the lot book.

---

"It is not," the clerk said.

---

And the turner asked whether it is part of that book, and the clerk said that it is not, out loud, in the same words, and the man who mends fencing asked whether the five lines on the boards are on that page, and the clerk said that they are not, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard all three of those and about four of them said the second and third after the first out of step with each other, and about four of them have said since that three people in that yard said out loud one at a time this morning that the page is not the lot book, and that about four of them said none of the three asked why, and that about four of them said all three wanted the same answer and got it.

---

Then the clerk said one thing out loud to about nineteen people, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, and she said it once.

---

"That page is not the lot book," she said. "I am a clerk of the lot book and not of anything behind it, and I have never been asked what is behind it and I am not going to go and find out on a morning like this."

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard that and about four of them have said since that a clerk has now told about nineteen people in a yard that there is a behind to that page and that she is not going to look at it, and that about four of them said that is the first time anybody has said out loud that there is a behind, and then the man at the end of the second table said one thing about the difference between a page and a book, and he said it in the ordinary voice and not to anybody in particular, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not answer him.

---

"A book is asked for by its name," he said. "A page is not asked for. A page is sent, and it comes down, and nobody has said out loud what it is for."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man at the end of a table has now said out loud in that yard that the page is a thing that is sent, and that about four of them said that is the closest anybody has come to the top of that hill this stretch of mornings, and that about four of them said nobody answered him and that about four of them said he was not waiting to be answered and that about four of them have not said what he was waiting for.

---

And then a man of thirty-eight said one thing out loud at about four feet off the near end of the first table, in about nineteen people's hearing, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it.

---

"She asked me for a page and I said no," he said. "I have not told her what a page is for either and I have had a morning and a half to think of it and I have not thought of one, and I am not going to go up there and ask her what she wants it for, because that would be asking, and I am not asking."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who said out loud on the one hundred and seventy-fourth morning that he was not going to be thanked has now said out loud that he is not asking, and that about four of them said the not-asking is not for the yard and that about four of them said it is for him, and that about four of them said the two of those are the same thing said two ways again.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a bar of iron at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-second night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-one of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and at about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the sixty-sixth time running, and nobody asked him what any of them is for.

---

Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and that page carries thirty-six lines and none of the thirty-six is in the lot book, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it and about four of them have said since that three people asked out loud what a page is for this morning and about nine people in that yard got an answer about a book, and that nobody in that yard has a way of asking the question so that the answer would be about the page.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning and not one of the thirty-six is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move, and a question about a page does not move it. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled. The record has stood ninety-two days and nobody in that yard has asked what it is ninety-two days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
