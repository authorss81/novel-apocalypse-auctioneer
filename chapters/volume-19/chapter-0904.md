# Chapter 904: The ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-EIGHTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Turner Said Out Loud At About Four Feet Off The Near End Of The Second Table In About Nineteen People's Hearing That A Page Goes Down That Lane And That The Carrying Of It Is A Person And He Would Not Name The Person And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move For Any Of It And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Seven Lines And Did Not Carry A Thirty-Eighth

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and forty-three days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and two. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and eighteen days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and thirty-two days. Seven hundred and ninety-three days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and twelve days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and eighty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and eighty-five days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and twenty-one days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and sixty-one marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and forty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and it is not said out loud again on any morning of this stretch, and that page did not take a line this morning.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and eighteen mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and seventeen. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and sixty-eight mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and eighty-eight, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and twenty-ninth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and forty-third night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty-two of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and thirty-third afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the forty-fourth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and three days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down, and above all of them the lane went up the hill in about four hundred yards of air with nothing on it, and about four of them have said since that about nineteen people in that yard have looked up that lane every morning of this stretch and that it is the one thing in that yard that nobody can do anything with on the spot.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other, and the man of about forty-one came down that bank behind them with his apron over his arm and stopped about four feet off the near end of the second table and did not say anything for a while.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is thirty, and it moved on the one hundred and eightieth morning from twenty-nine, and he said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a lane count is a count of pages in a carrier's own hand and not a count of people who go up a hill, and that about four of them said the boy says the figure the same either way, which is the correct way to say a figure that came down a lane.

---

Then at about eleven the man of about forty-one put his apron down on the second table and said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, in about nineteen people's hearing, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet went quiet while he said it.

---

"A page goes down that lane," he said. "That is the only direction anybody has put out loud in that yard about that hill and I have turned things round long enough to know a direction when I am holding one. Down. Everything else about that lane is a person holding a thing, and the holding is a person, and I am not going to name the person."

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard the direction and about four of them have said since that a man who makes things round has said out loud the only thing anybody in that yard has said about that lane in ten mornings, and that about four of them said the direction is the part that was not in doubt, and that about four of them said everything that has come down that lane has come down it.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet got as far as opening their mouths, and about four of them have said since that a question was not put this morning, and that about four of them said the whole of that yard had heard how it would sound, and that about four of them said a man who has said the direction out loud and stopped there has not been asked anything and is not owed an answer by anybody.

---

And the man of about thirty-four heard all of it from the near end of the second table and said nothing, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the yard has heard the direction and has still got no name, and that about four of them said those are not the same thing, and that a man who said out loud that he knows three people and would not say which three was standing about nine feet from a man who has just said out loud that the carrying of a page is a person and has not said who.

---

And then a man of thirty-eight said one thing out loud, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, in about nineteen people's hearing.

---

"I have been up that lane and down it," he said, "and I did not see who carries anything, because there was nothing to carry, and I am not going to stand in that yard and guess at a person out loud on a morning when a man has just told you the only thing he knows about it."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who has been to the top of that lane said out loud this morning that he has not got it, and that about four of them said that is the second morning running of not being asked, and that about four of them said nobody in that yard has offered him anything for going and nobody is going to.

---

The clerk said nothing at all about the direction, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who makes things round said out loud in that yard a thing on a morning a clerk has said no four times without a reason, and that about four of them said nobody asked her about it, and that about four of them said a thing said out loud by a man at a table is not a thing a clerk is answerable for and that is not the same as it being a thing she has no answer to.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a bar of iron at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and forty-third night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty-two of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the seventy-seventh time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and that page carries thirty-seven lines and none of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a direction was said out loud in that yard this morning and that page did not take a line for a direction either, and that about four of them said a line on that page has only ever been for an asking.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said it and did not go up, and about four of them have said since that a man who said a direction out loud in that yard this morning went up that bank without saying goodnight to anybody, which is what he does.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning and not one of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a direction said out loud is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a direction is not a page coming down a lane. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and three days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and three days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and a man who makes things round said out loud this morning that a page goes down that lane and did not name the person who holds it and nobody asked him for one.
