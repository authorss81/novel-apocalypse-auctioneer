# Chapter 898: The ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SECOND Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of Thirty-Four Asked A Man Of Thirty-Eight Out Loud In About Nineteen People's Hearing Whether He Would Carry That Form Back Up That Lane And He Said He Would Not And He Gave A Reason And The Page Did Not Move And He Was Not Asked Twice And The Lane Count Is Thirty And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and thirty-seven days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and ninety-six. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and twelve days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and twenty-six days. Seven hundred and eighty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and seventy-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and seventy-nine days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and fifteen days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and fifty-five marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and forty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and twelve mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and eleven. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and sixty-two mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and eighty-two, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and twenty-third of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-seventh night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-six of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and twenty-seventh afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the thirty-eighth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood ninety-seven days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and the form was still lying at about four feet off the near end of the first table where it had been put down, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down, and about four of them have said since that a piece of paper with one space on it has now been on those boards for three mornings and nobody has put anything in the space and nobody has said out loud that it is anybody's job to.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment, and about four of them said it and about four of them said it a moment after, which is what happens when about nine people in that yard are listening to two things.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is thirty, and it moved on the one hundred and eightieth morning from twenty-nine, and he said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of them have said since that the lane count moved on a morning when a young woman came down that lane on foot and that about four of them said nobody in that yard has asked her how far it is and that about four of them said she was not asked anything at all.

---

Then at about eleven the man of about thirty-four asked a man of thirty-eight a question out loud, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, in about nineteen people's hearing, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet went quiet while he asked it.

---

"Are you going to take that back up that lane," the man of about thirty-four said.

---

And the man of thirty-eight said he would not.

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet waited, and about four of them have said since that they waited because a man said no in that yard on a morning before and gave nothing else, and that about four of them said they waited to find out whether this one was that again, and about four of them have said that the silence after a no is the loudest thing that yard has.

---

And then he gave a reason, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard every word of it.

---

"A woman at a table nine miles up that lane asked me for the page at the back of that lot book and I said no," he said. "That was on the one hundred and seventy-fifth morning and about nine people in this yard heard me say it. If I carry a different page up that hill this week in my own hand then I am a man who said no to one thing on the one hundred and seventy-fifth morning and did another inside three days of it, and to whoever is at the top of that hill those are the same man. And there is a space on that page for a name, and I am not going to carry nine miles of lane with an empty space on it and no way of saying what is going in it."

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard that and about four of them have said since that a reason is a different thing from a no, and that about four of them said a no has got nothing on it to carry and about four of them said a reason has got a person attached to it and about four of them said that is the first time in this stretch of mornings that anybody in that yard has given one and about four of them said the reason was about the yard and not about the lane.

---

And nobody asked him a second time about it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the man of about thirty-four did not ask him again either, and that about four of them said the man of about thirty-four asked one question this morning and got more than he asked for, and that about four of them said he has not asked anybody else anything since.

---

The clerk did not write the reason on anything, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that nobody asked her to and that about four of them said the page at the back takes a line when somebody asks the man of fifty-six something and that about four of them said this morning was not that and about four of them said the yard keeps finding out where a thing cannot go and this is a new place.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-seventh night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-six of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the seventy-first time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it and about four of them have said since that a reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the page at the back did not move, and that about four of them said the clerk named the morning instead, and that about four of them have said since that the reason is in a mouth somewhere at the foot of that bank and is not on anything, and about four of them have said that a man who has not been thanked for a reason is in an unusual position in that yard and about four of them said nobody thanked him.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning and not one of the thirty-six is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a reason said out loud is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a man declining to carry a thing up that lane does not move it. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled. The record has stood ninety-seven days and nobody in that yard has asked what it is ninety-seven days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and nobody thanked him for a reason either.
