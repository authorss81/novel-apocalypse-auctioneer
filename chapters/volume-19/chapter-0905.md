# Chapter 905: The ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-NINTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of Thirty-Eight Went Out Of That Yard In The Afternoon And Up That Lane And Off It To Look At One Of The Four Places Where Those Three Lines Can Be Read And Came Back Down That Lane In The Evening With Nothing In His Hand And He Said Out Loud That He Has Not Got It Yet And Nobody Asked Him And The Lane Count Is A Count Of Pages And It Is Thirty And It Did Not Move And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Seven Lines And Did Not Carry A Thirty-Eighth

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and forty-four days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and three. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and nineteen days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and thirty-three days. Seven hundred and ninety-four days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and thirteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and eighty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and eighty-six days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and twenty-two days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and sixty-two marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and forty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and it is not said out loud again on any morning of this stretch, and that page did not take a line this morning.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and nineteen mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and eighteen. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and sixty-nine mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and eighty-nine, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it. A man went up that lane in the afternoon and came back down it with nothing in his hand and nothing came down it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and thirtieth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and forty-fourth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty-three of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and thirty-fourth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the forty-fifth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and four days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and a man of thirty-eight was standing at the top of that bank with his hands showing, which is what he does, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down behind him and about four of them have said since that a man who stands at the top of that bank in the morning has not gone anywhere yet and that a man who stands there in the afternoon has.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other, and the man of thirty-eight was still up on the bank, and about four of them have said since that he was not listening and that about four of them said he is never listening to that wall and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what he is listening to.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is thirty, and it moved on the one hundred and eightieth morning from twenty-nine, and he said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a count of pages is not a count of people and that a person can go up a lane and come back down it and the count will not move, and that about four of them said that is correct and that about four of them said it is not correct and that neither of them said why.

---

At about eleven the yard did what it does, and the man of thirty-eight came down that bank and stood at about four feet off the near end of the first table with his hands showing and said one thing out loud to about nine people in that yard, and he said it in the ordinary voice and he said it once.

---

"I am going up that lane this afternoon," he said, "and I am going off it to look at one of the four places, and I am not taking anything, and nobody has asked me to go and nobody has given me anything for going, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to say I am a good man for it."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man said out loud in that yard what he was not going to be thanked for before he had done it, and that about four of them said that is the way to say it, and that about four of them have said since that a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and that nobody in that yard is going to give him any of the three.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack of something at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

And then a man of thirty-eight went up that bank and up that lane with nothing in either hand, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched him go and about four of them have said since that he has gone out of that yard for the first time in a hundred and eighty-nine mornings, and that about four of them said nobody has said out loud that he will be back this evening and that about four of them said he said he was going to look at a place and that is not the same as saying he will come back down it.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and forty-fourth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty-three of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the seventy-eighth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and that page carries thirty-seven lines and none of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man went out of that yard this afternoon and the page at the back did not take a line for that either, and that about four of them said the page is not for a man going out of a yard.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

---

And then at about six, with the light off those boards and about nine people in that yard gone up that bank, a man of thirty-eight came the rest of the way down it with nothing in his hand, and about four people at the top of eleven feet were still there and heard him come down, and he did not go to the boards and he did not go to the second table and he stood about four feet off the near end of the first table in the dark with his hands showing.

---

"I have not got it yet," he said, out loud, to about four people, in the ordinary voice, and nobody asked him what he went to look at, and nobody asked him twice, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man came back down that hill in the evening with a thing he is not going to say and said two words about it out loud before he had been asked anything at all.

---

And about four of them said that a man who says he has not got it yet has told that yard he got something, and that about four of them said he did not have to, and that about four of them have said since that nobody in that yard has said out loud that he went nine miles up that lane to look at a thing and came nine miles back down it with nothing, and that about four of them said the count of pages coming down that lane will not move for it, and that about four of them said they do not know what the count is for.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning and not one of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a man saying he has not got it yet is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a person going up a lane and coming back down it with nothing in his hand is not a page coming down it. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and four days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and four days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight went up that lane in the afternoon and came back down it in the evening with nothing in his hand and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and the lane count is a count of pages and it is thirty and it did not move.
