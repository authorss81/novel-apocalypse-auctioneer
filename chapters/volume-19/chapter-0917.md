# Chapter 0917: The TWO HUNDRED AND FIRST Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Was Seen From Outside That Yard At One Of The Four Places And He Said The Three Lines Are Not On That Wall And Have Not Been Since Before He Came And He Said Where They Went And Where They Went Is Not A Place Anybody Can Walk To And He Was Not Asked To Show Anybody And Was Not Asked Twice And The Lane Count Is Thirty And A Trip Is Not A Page Coming Down A Lane And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Seven Lines And It Did Not Carry A Thirty-Eighth

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and fifty-six days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and fifteen. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and thirty-one days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and forty-five days. Eight hundred and six days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and twenty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and twenty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and ninety-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and ninety-eight days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and thirty-four days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and seventy-four marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and sixty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and it is not said out loud again on any morning of this stretch, and that page did not take a line this morning.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and thirty-one mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and thirty. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and eighty-one mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and one, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and forty-second of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fifty-sixth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and fifty-five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and forty-sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the fifty-seventh morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and sixteen days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

The boards went up after the light found the eleven feet and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down, and the lane up the side of that bank had somebody's cart ruts in it from the day before and a wet place at the top where the water comes across. At about ten the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart came down through the gap in the bank with his cart and said one thing out loud before anybody had said anything to him, and about nineteen people in that yard heard it.

The man of fifty-six had said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had said *as sent* out of step with each other, and nobody in that yard had asked him anything. At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then he said no figure, and said so out loud, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him and went on down the yard.

"I am going up there again," he said, "and nobody has asked me to and nobody is going to thank me for it."

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who said a thing about not being thanked on the morning he was asked to go has said it again this morning with nobody having asked him anything, and that about four of them said the second time cost him more than the first.

And then he took the cart up through the gap and out of that yard, and about four people at the top of eleven feet watched him go, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about where he was going, and the lane count at those boards is a count of pages and it is thirty. The morning went on in that yard the way it goes, and the man who digs loam was at the north end, and the man who makes things round had his wheel going, and the clerk stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table with the front of that book open and said nothing out loud to anybody. And about half past ten a man came out of the door with a bundle of wire in his arms and put it down on the step, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart said good morning to him and the man said good morning back and then stood on his own step and looked at the man with the cart. And the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart did not ask him a second thing, and about four people at the top of eleven feet who were not in that yard have said since that a man in that room said out loud that a thing exists at a place a body cannot get to,.

And up the lane and past the first turning the ground comes up on the left and goes away on the right, and the light gets in under the eaves of everything on that side,.

The second of the four places is a low building with a door in it and a window with the lower half painted over,. And the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart said he had, and said that nobody in the yard below had told him what he would find on it, and the man on the step said the three lines are not on that wall and have not been since before he came.

"You have come about that wall," he said.

"I have been in this room nine years," he said, "and they were not on it when I came and they have not been on it since, and I have never once seen them on it and I have never once been asked about them."
