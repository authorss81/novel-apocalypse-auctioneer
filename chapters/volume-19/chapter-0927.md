# Chapter 0927: The TWO HUNDRED AND ELEVENTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Reason Came Out Of A Mouth That Nobody Planned And It Came Off A Cart And Not Off A Table And The Man Of About Twenty-Nine Who Drives It Gave Away The Only Thing He Had That Nobody Else In That Yard Could Have Said For Him And Did Not Look At Anybody While He Said It And One Line Went On The Page At The Back And The Line Carries The Morning And Not The Reason And Nobody Was Thanked And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and sixty-six days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and twenty-five. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and forty-one days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and fifty-five days. Eight hundred and sixteen days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and thirty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and thirty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and eight days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and forty-four days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and eighty-four marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and seventy marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and one reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the page at the back of that lot book took one line for it this morning and the line carries the morning and not the reason, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before the two hundred and eighth morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and forty-one mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and forty. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and ninety-one mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and eleven, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and tenth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries forty-one lines and it did not carry a forty-second this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and fifty-second of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and sixty-sixth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and sixty-five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and fifty-sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the sixty-seventh morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and twenty-six days, the page at the back took one line this morning and the morning was read out loud before it was written, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

The boards went up after the light found the eleven feet and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down. At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on the stone afterwards.

---

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then he said no figure, and said so out loud, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him and went on down the yard.

---

The man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart came through the gap in the bank at about half past nine with a load of stone on the cart and the shafts up on his hip, and he slowed in the gap the way he slows in the gap, and he stopped, and he did not go on. He stood in the middle of that yard at about four feet off the near end of the second table behind the shafts with the horse's nose down to the stones.

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet saw him stop and looked at each other, and about four of them have said since that a yard has got used to a cart coming through it and going on through, and that three mornings ago the same cart stopped in the same place and said one thing and went on, and that about four of them have not said why a cart stops in a yard.

---

And then he said a reason out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing, standing at about four feet off the near end of the second table behind the shafts of his own cart, and he did not look at anybody.

"My father went down in a bank," he said. "Not far up there from this. And I slow in a gap every single time I come through one, and I have never said that out loud to anybody, and there is nobody in this yard I would have said it to."

---

And then he took the shafts up and went on through that yard and out the far side of it with the stone rattling, and he did not go up the lane, and nobody in that yard said one word back to him, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man has given away this morning the only thing he had that nobody else in that yard could have said for him.

And about four of them said he did not look at anybody while he said it and that he said it behind the shafts of his own cart, and that the reason cannot be checked by anybody standing in that yard, and that about four of them have not said whether a reason nobody can check is worth more or less than a reason somebody can.

---

And the turner put his wheel down at about eleven and sat on the bench with the piece of ash still in his hands and worked nothing, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who makes things round has now stopped his wheel twice in four mornings.

---

The man of about thirty-four said one thing out loud at about four feet off the near end of the second table and about nineteen people heard it.

"A yard gets its reasons off its tables," he said. "And this one came off a cart."

---

And nobody answered that, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a yard which has had a reason off a woman who keeps a scale and a reason off a man who digs loam has now had one off a man with a horse, and that nobody in that yard had any idea this morning that a cart was a thing a reason could come out of.

---

At about half past ten the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, before he said his number, and about nineteen people heard it.

"A yard that has reasons coming out of it is not a yard that is waiting for them," he said.

And nobody answered that either, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the man who keeps a tally of mornings has said out loud three things in four mornings and that nobody has told him what they were for.

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack of something and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody. The clerk stood with the front of that book open and said nothing out loud to anybody.

---

And about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and sixty-sixth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and sixty-five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and fifty-sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundredth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

And then the clerk read the morning out loud, and she read it before she wrote anything, in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, to about nineteen people at the foot of that bank.

"The two hundred and eleventh morning," she said. "One reason was said out loud in this yard this morning and it is being written on the page at the back of that book, and the reason is not going on it, and that is the whole of it."

---

And she wrote one line on the page at the back of that lot book, in her own hand, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched a clerk write one line for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and that page carries forty-one lines and not one of the forty-one is in the lot book.

---

Nobody thanked the man who said the reason and nobody thanked the clerk who wrote the line, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who drove a cart through that yard this morning said the thing he had and then went out of it, and that about four of them have not said whether he will go up that lane again.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up, and the boards went down the way they go down.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that three mornings have now carried a reason out loud in this yard and that the third one came off the last person anybody in that yard would have said, and that about four of them have not said what that leaves.

The page at the back of that lot book carries forty-one lines and it did not carry a forty-second this morning and not one of the forty-one is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a line on the back of that book is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a morning that goes like the others is not a page coming down that lane, and the measure of this stretch does not go up that lane at all. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and twenty-six days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and twenty-six days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and one reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and one line was written on that page carrying the morning and not the reason and not the name and not the question and not the answer, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and this morning went like the others, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who drives a cart said a reason out loud in that yard and went back to his cart, and that about four of them have said nobody in that yard was told where it came from except everybody in that yard.
