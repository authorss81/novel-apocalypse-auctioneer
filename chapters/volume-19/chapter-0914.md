# Chapter 0914: The ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-EIGHTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Makes Things Round Said Out Loud At About Eleven That A Page That Has Been Read To Oneself Has Not Been Read And Nobody In That Yard Answered It And Nobody Agreed With It Out Loud And He Put His Tool Down At About Ten Past Eleven And Did Not Take It Up Again That Morning And About Four People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Have Said Since That The Yard Got One Step Nearer The Thing It Cannot Ask About And One Step Back From Asking It In The Same Morning And About Four Of Them Have Said Since That Those Are Not Two Steps And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Seven Lines And It Did Not Carry A Thirty-Eighth And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and fifty-three days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and twelve. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and twenty-eight days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and forty-two days. Eight hundred and three days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and seventeen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and twenty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and ninety-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and ninety-five days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and thirty-one days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and seventy-one marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and fifty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and it is not said out loud again on any morning of this stretch, and that page did not take a line this morning.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and twenty-eight mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and twenty-seven. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and seventy-eight mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and ninety-eight, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and thirty-ninth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fifty-third night of that run, having slept on five hundred and fifty-two of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and forty-third afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the fifty-fourth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and thirteen days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said a figure out loud before the clerk's hand had moved, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

"A page that has been read to oneself has not been read," he said.

---

And nobody in that yard answered him, and nobody in that yard said out loud that they thought so, and about four people at the top of eleven feet opened their mouths and shut them again, and about four of them have said since that a sentence that true went out of a mouth in about nineteen people's hearing this morning and came back with nothing at all.
