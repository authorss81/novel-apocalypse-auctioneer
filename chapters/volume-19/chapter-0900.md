# Chapter 900: The ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FOURTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Man Who Digs Loam Asked Out Loud In About Nineteen People's Hearing Whether Anybody In That Yard Is Going To Fill That Space On That Form And About Nine People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Said No One After Another And Nobody Gave A Reason And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Seven Lines And Did Not Carry A Thirty-Eighth And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move And The Record Has Stood Ninety-Nine Days And Nobody In That Yard Asked What It Is Ninety-Nine Days Of

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and thirty-nine days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and ninety-eight. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and fourteen days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and twenty-eight days. Seven hundred and eighty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and seventy-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and eighty-one days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and seventeen days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and fifty-seven marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and forty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and fourteen mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and thirteen. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and sixty-four mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and eighty-four, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and twenty-fifth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-ninth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and twenty-ninth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the fortieth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood ninety-nine days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and the form was still under the clean page at about four feet off the near end of the first table where the clerk put it on the one hundred and eighty-third morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down, and about four of them have said since that a piece of paper can sit in the same place for five mornings and become part of those boards, and that about four of them said the form has been there since the one hundred and eightieth morning and that about four of them said it is the oldest thing on those boards that nobody has written on.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other, and about four of them have said since that on the one hundred and eighty-third morning the man of fifty-six got his four figures out before anybody had got a barrow down that bank and that about four of them said a man who has answered a question before half past eleven gets earlier afterwards.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is thirty, and it moved on the one hundred and eightieth morning from twenty-nine, and he said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the boy has said no figure more mornings this stretch than he has said one and that about four of them said nobody in that yard has asked him to say it and that about four of them said nobody has told him not to.

---

Then at about eleven the man who digs loam came up off the north end with his barrow and put it down at about four feet off the near end of the second table and asked a question out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it and nobody answered for a while.

---

"Is anybody in this yard going to fill that space on that form," he said.

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet said no, one after another, in about nineteen people's hearing, and none of them gave a reason, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard nine refusals of one question inside about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.

---

And the man who digs loam did not answer them and did not ask again, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who asked one question out loud in that yard on the one hundred and seventy-eighth morning got one answer about a page and a man who asked one out loud this morning got nine about nothing, and that about four of them said the second one was the faster and that about four of them said the fast one cost nothing.

---

And the clerk said nothing at all about it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clerk who said out loud on the one hundred and seventy-seventh morning that she is a clerk of the lot book and not of anything behind it said nothing at all this morning to nine refusals in her hearing, and that about four of them said that is not her agreeing with them and that about four of them have said since that nobody has asked her whether she agrees.

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that refusing is easier than the asking was, and that about four of them said that is not the same as being settled, and that about four of them said a yard has put a question out loud on six mornings out of ten and has said no out loud nine times over one of them, and that about four of them said the refusals took about as long as the asking.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody. At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-ninth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the seventy-third time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and that page carries thirty-seven lines and none of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it, and about four of them have said since that the page at the back did not move on a morning when about nine people in that yard refused something out loud, and that about four of them said a refusal is not an asking and that about four of them said a line on that page has only ever been for an asking.

---

And then a man of thirty-eight said one thing out loud at about ten past four, in about nineteen people's hearing, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, and he said it before the light went off the boards, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it and nobody said good afternoon after him.

---

"I went up that lane on the one hundred and seventy-fifth morning and came back down with a fact," he said. "The fact is on these boards and it is not on a page, and this morning about nine people in this yard said no to a space in it in about as long as it takes the man at the foot of that wall to say four figures, and none of them gave a reason, and I have not been given one either and I am not going to say I have one."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a yard of about nineteen people can hold a thing and cannot send it and cannot say why, and that about four of them said the why is the part nobody has looked at, and that about four of them said the man who came down that hill has now said out loud that he has no reason either and that about four of them said that is the first morning of this stretch on which a person in that yard has said out loud that the yard is holding something.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the record on those boards has stood ninety-nine days and that about nine people in that yard can say that figure out loud this morning without looking at anything, and that about four of them said not one of them has said what it is ninety-nine days of, and that about four of them have said since that the last morning of ten did not have a fact and this one did and that about four of them said nobody asked about the record at all today.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and about four people out of about nineteen said the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up, and nobody at the top of that bank said out loud what had been refused.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning and not one of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a refusal said out loud nine times is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and it is not moved by a decision. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood ninety-nine days and nobody in that yard asked what it is ninety-nine days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
