# Chapter 899: The ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-THIRD Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Clerk Laid The Form On The Boards Under The Clean Page And Said Out Loud That It Is Not Going In The Lot Book And A Woman Of About Fifty Asked The Man Of Fifty-Six A Question Out Loud And He Was Given The Answer He Gives At About Four Every Morning And One More Line Went On The Page At The Back Of That Book And Nobody Thanked The Person Who Asked And Nobody Thanked The Man Who Was Asked And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and thirty-eight days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and ninety-seven. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and thirteen days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and twenty-seven days. Seven hundred and eighty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and seventy-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and eighty days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and sixteen days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and fifty-six marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and forty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a woman of about fifty asked the man of fifty-six something out loud this morning and one more line went on the page at the back of that lot book, and the count of things this district has made did not move on the morning that line went on and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and thirteen mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and twelve. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and sixty-three mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and eighty-three, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and none of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and twenty-fourth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a woman of about fifty asked him something out loud at about half past eleven and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard that as well.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-eighth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-seven of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and twenty-eighth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the thirty-ninth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood ninety-eight days, the page at the back took one line this morning and the morning was read out loud before it was written, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said a figure out loud before the clerk's hand had moved, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down, and the form was still on the boards where it had been put down on the one hundred and eightieth morning, and about four of them have said since that it has been on there four mornings now and has not been in the lot book and is not going to be, and that about four of them said that is the plainest sentence anybody has said in that yard this stretch.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment, and nobody went near him, and about four people have said since that a man who is going to be asked something before eleven o'clock in the afternoon gets his morning out of the way early and that about four of them said he does not know he is going to be asked anything.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is thirty, and it moved on the one hundred and eightieth morning from twenty-nine, and then he said a figure, and he said it before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

"Two hundred and four," he said, at the near end of the first table.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet said that after him out of step with each other and about four of them have said since that a boy of seventeen said that figure on the third morning of ten and that about four of them said the figure and the lane count have been said in that yard in the same week for the first time since the boards went up.

---

Then at about eleven the clerk put the form flat on the boards under the clean page and said one thing out loud, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, in about nineteen people's hearing, and she said it once.

---

"That is not going in the lot book," she said. "I have read it out loud to about nineteen people and I am not going to enter it and I am not going to put a line on the back for it either, because a line on the back is for a morning somebody asked in this yard, and this is not that."

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard that and about four of them said out loud that the lot book has five lines on it and that not one of those five came down that lane, and about four of them said the clerk is telling the yard the truth and about four of them said telling the truth about where a thing cannot go is not the same as knowing where it goes, and then at about half past eleven a woman of about fifty came up about four feet of that bank with a barrow and put the barrow down and asked the man of fifty-six a question out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet went quiet while she asked it.

---

"Are you going to be at that wall tomorrow morning," she said.

---

And he gave her the answer he gives at about four every morning, in the same words and at the same speed, flat, and about nineteen people heard it, and about four of them said the second part after the first out of step with each other, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the answer came back the same to a question nobody had asked before about a morning, and about four of them said it has been used about a different thing this morning and about four of them said a man cannot help what his answer is about and about four of them have not said what they make of it.

---

Then the clerk read the morning out loud, and she read it before she wrote anything, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people at the foot of that bank.

---

"The one hundred and eighty-third morning," she said. "Somebody asked out loud this morning and it is being written on the page at the back of that book and the question is not going on it and the answer is not going on it, and that is the whole of it and I am not going to say anything else about it in advance of having done it."

---

And she wrote one line on the page at the back of that lot book, in her own hand, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched a clerk write one line for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and that page carries thirty-seven lines and none of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the page has not taken a line for four mornings running and that this morning it took one and that the morning it took one for was a morning a woman asked a man whether he would be at a wall tomorrow.

---

And then it turned out out loud that nobody in that yard carries anything up that hill, and the man of about thirty-four said so, and the turner said so, and the man who drives the cart said so, and a man of thirty-eight said so and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard all four of those inside about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and about four of them have said since that that is now a fact and not a policy, and that about four of them said a policy is something a yard decides and a fact is something a yard finds out, and that about four of them said nobody in that yard decided anything this morning.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the lane count is thirty and did not move this morning and that it is not going to move because about nineteen people in that yard have said out loud that they will not carry anything up that hill, and that about four of them said that is the first time anybody has said out loud that the count is not going to move, and that about four of them said the count has never once moved for a decision in the whole of that yard, and the woman who keeps a scale weighed a bar of iron at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-eighth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-seven of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the seventy-second time running, and nobody asked him what any of them is for.

---

Nobody thanked the woman who asked him and nobody thanked the man who was asked, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that they looked for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall to see whether anybody was going to and that nobody was, and that about four of them said the woman did not want anything and about four of them said the man at the foot of that wall has not wanted anything since before the middle of the winter.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-seven lines and none of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move this morning, and a line on the back of a book is not a fifteenth of it. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a clerk reading a morning out loud is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and it is not moved by a decision, and a yard that has said out loud that it will carry nothing up that hill has not moved it. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the form that came down that lane is on the boards under a clean page and is not going in the lot book. The record has stood ninety-eight days and nobody in that yard has asked what it is ninety-eight days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
