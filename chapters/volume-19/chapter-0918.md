The board carries eight hundred and sixteen. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and thirty-two days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and forty-six days. Eight hundred and seven days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and seventy-five marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and sixty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and two, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and forty-third of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fifty-seventh night of that run, having slept on five hundred and fifty-six of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and forty-seventh afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the fifty-eighth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.
