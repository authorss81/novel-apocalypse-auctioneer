# Chapter 892: The ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SIXTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of About Forty-One Asked The Clerk Out Loud At About Four Feet Off The Near End Of The Second Table Whether The Page At The Back Of That Lot Book Can Go Up That Lane To Whoever Asked For It And She Said No Out Loud And Gave No Reason And Nothing Was Entered And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and thirty-one days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and ninety. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and six days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and twenty days. Seven hundred and eighty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and ninety-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and seventy days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and seventy-three days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and nine days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and forty-nine marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and thirty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and six mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and five. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and fifty-six mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and seventy-six, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and seventeenth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-first night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and twenty-first afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the thirty-second morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood ninety-one days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had come down that bank earlier than they had to, and a man of thirty-eight was in that yard at about half past six and stood where he stands, and about four of them have said since that he has not said one word about a door at the top of that lane and that about four of them said he said the whole of it yesterday at about eleven and that about four of them said a thing said once is a thing that is used up.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment, and the man who makes the stone was at the near end of the low wall with a chisel and a piece of stone and no particular hurry.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is twenty-nine, and it has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and he said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a boy of seventeen has said no figure on more mornings than he has said one this stretch, and that about four of them noticed which mornings and about four of them did not.

---

Then at about eleven a man of about forty-one came the rest of the way down that bank, which he has done twice in this stretch of mornings, and he stopped at about four feet off the near end of the second table and asked the clerk a question out loud in the ordinary voice in about nineteen people's hearing, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet went quiet while he asked it.

---

"Can the page at the back of that lot book go up that lane," he said, "to whoever asked for it."

---

And the clerk said no out loud, in the ordinary voice, and she gave no reason, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard both of those things.

---

It is the third time she has said no out loud in this stretch of mornings and given no reason, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the third one was said in the same words as the first and at the same speed, and that about four of them said a person who says a thing three times the same way is not deciding something new each time and that about four of them said she does not look like a person deciding anything.

---

Nobody asked her why, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that nobody asked her why this morning because the answer was not going to be about nineteen people's knowing and that about four of them said they had asked her why before and got the same no.

---

The man of about forty-one stood there a while after that and then went up about nine feet further along and stood at the near end of that second table with his back to the boards, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who has been up that hill twice came down it with the same answer twice and that about four of them said he did not say the answer was wrong and that about four of them said he did not say it was right.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman who said on the one hundred and sixty-seventh morning that she was not going to ask anything else in that yard has not said anything else on any morning since, and that nobody has said out loud that she is obeying herself.

---

About half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and at about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-first night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the sixty-fifth time running, and nobody asked him what any of them is for.

---

Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and that page carries thirty-six lines and none of the thirty-six is in the lot book, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clerk was asked out loud this morning whether a page can go somewhere and gave the same no she gives for everything, and that about four of them said a page cannot go anywhere because the yard has never sent one and that about four of them said that is not a reason she gave, it is a thing that is true.

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman who has said no three times out loud without giving a reason has stopped being asked things and started being believed, and that about four of them said those are not the same thing, and that about four of them have not said which of the two they mean, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that this morning was the first morning in this stretch on which about nine people in that yard took a thing a woman said as a fact about the world rather than as an opinion about a person, and that nobody in that yard checked it.

---

At about half past four the light came off the boards all at once, the way it does when there is cloud on the top of that bank, and about four people out of about nineteen said the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up, and a woman who keeps a scale was still at her scale when the light went.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning and not one of the thirty-six is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move, and a question asked of that clerk this morning does not move it. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled. The record has stood ninety-one days and nobody in that yard has asked what it is ninety-one days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
