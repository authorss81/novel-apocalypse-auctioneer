# Chapter 895: The ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-NINTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month Nobody Would Say Out Loud Who Would Carry A Page Back Up That Lane And About Four People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Have Said Since That They Know Three People And Have Not Said So And Mavis Dorr Said One Thing Out Loud About A Page That Has Never Been Read And The Page At The Back Did Not Move And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and thirty-four days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and ninety-three. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and nine days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and twenty-three days. Seven hundred and eighty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and ninety-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and seventy-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and seventy-six days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and twelve days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and fifty-two marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and thirty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and nine mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and eight. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and fifty-nine mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and seventy-nine, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and twentieth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-fourth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-three of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and twenty-fourth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the thirty-fifth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood ninety-four days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet were standing at about four feet off the near end of the second table at about half past six, and about four of them have said since that the yard has asked two questions out loud in three mornings and got two answers that were about something else, and that about four of them said so before the boards were up.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment, and the man who made the stone said out loud that there is a morning for everything and there is not one for this.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is twenty-nine, and it has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and he said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that about nine people in that yard were waiting to hear which person in that yard would carry a thing up that hill and that about four of them said the boy knows where things go and about four of them said the boy is seventeen.

---

Then about nine people at the top of eleven feet asked one another, one at a time and out loud, who in that yard would carry a page back up that lane, and about nine answers came back, and every one of them was a person who goes up that hill on a day and not on a day, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that about nine people in that yard know somebody who could do it and that none of the nine said a name out loud.

---

And then a man of about thirty-four said out loud, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, in about nineteen people's hearing, that he knows three people.

---

"Three," the man of about thirty-four said, at the near end of that second table. "I know three people."

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet asked which three, and he did not say, and about four of them asked him again at about eleven and at about one and he did not say then either, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who has gone down that lane twice in his life has now put three people in a yard of about nineteen people and will not say where he has put them.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that there are three people in that yard who go up that hill and that about four of them said the woman who keeps a scale is at her scale on the days she does not go up that hill, and that about four of them said that is not the same as naming her and that about four of them have not said why it is not.

---

Then Mavis Dorr said one thing out loud, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, in about nineteen people's hearing, and she said it once.

---

"A page that has never been read is not a record of anything," she said. "That is all. I have got nothing else this morning."

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it and about four of them have said since that the yard has already forgotten that the woman said it about a page and thinks she said it about a bag of something she carried up that hill in the spring, and that about four of them said the two sentences are not the same sentence and about four of them said the second one is more useful, and about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and at about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-fourth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-three of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the sixty-eighth time running, and nobody asked him what any of them is for.

---

Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and that page carries thirty-six lines and none of the thirty-six is in the lot book, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it and about four of them have said since that a morning on which about nine people could not name a carrier has been named on that page without a line being taken for it.

---

And then a man of thirty-four said one thing more out loud, at about ten past four, in about nineteen people's hearing, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it.

---

"I am not going to say the three," he said. "That is not me not knowing. If I do not know, then somebody can say that I do not know and we can all carry that. If I say, then it is three people and a lane and a morning, and I have put that on about nineteen people. I am not going to do that and I am not going to pretend I cannot."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man in that yard has refused to name three people out loud as a decision and not as an accident, and that about four of them said the reason he gave is the first reason anybody has given in that yard for keeping something off the page, and that about four of them said the clerk did not take a line for that either and nobody asked her to.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning and not one of the thirty-six is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move, and a refusal to name three people does not move it. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled. The record has stood ninety-four days and nobody in that yard has asked what it is ninety-four days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
