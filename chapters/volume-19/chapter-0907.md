# Chapter 907: The ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-FIRST Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Clerk Said Out Loud At Those Boards In About Nineteen People's Hearing That Nobody In That Yard Has Read The Page At The Back Of That Book And Two Men Said Out Loud That They Have Not And Nobody Asked Her To Read It And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Seven Lines And Did Not Carry A Thirty-Eighth And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and forty-six days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and five. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and twenty-one days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and thirty-five days. Seven hundred and ninety-six days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and ten days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and fifteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and eighty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and eighty-eight days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and twenty-four days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and sixty-four marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and fifty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and it is not said out loud again on any morning of this stretch, and that page did not take a line this morning.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and twenty-one mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and twenty. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and seventy-one mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and ninety-one, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and thirty-second of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and forty-sixth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty-five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and thirty-sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the forty-seventh morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and six days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and the form was still under the clean page at about four feet off the near end of the first table with the space on it empty, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down, and about four of them have said since that a yard gets to the boards earlier than it gets to a question, and that about four of them said that is not a criticism of anybody in it.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other, and the clerk took the lot book out from under the clean page and opened it and did not write anything and closed it again, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clerk has been opening that book and shutting it again on a morning with nothing in it for several mornings running and that about four of them said nobody has asked her why she is doing it.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is thirty, and it moved on the one hundred and eightieth morning from twenty-nine, and he said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the boy says no figure out loud and that a boy who says no figure in a yard where nothing is being said is the only person in it being exact.

---

Then at about ten the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart put the horse's head about and asked the clerk a question out loud at about four feet off the near end of the first table, in the ordinary voice, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it.

---

"Is that the same book that came down that lane," he said, "on the morning the page came down it."

---

And the clerk said that it was the same book, and she said it in the ordinary voice, and then she stopped, and then she said one more thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, at those boards, in about nineteen people's hearing, and she said it once and she did not say it again, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard every word of it.

---

"Nobody in this yard has read the page at the back of that book," she said. "That goes with me as well. I am not saying anything else about it and it is not a reason for anything."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clerk said out loud this morning that a thing she keeps has not been read by anybody in that yard including her, and that about four of them said that is not the same as saying she has not read it, and that about four of them have said since that neither of those two things was what she said.

---

And then the man at the end of the second table said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, at the end of that table, and he said it to nobody in particular, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it.

---

"I have not read it either," he said, "and I have been at this end of this table long enough for that to be worth saying."

---

And then the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one thing out loud from about nine feet further along than the place he stands in, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it.

---

"I have not read it," he said, "and I am not going to, and I want that said out loud this morning so that nobody has to think about it later."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who keeps a tally in that yard asked for a thing to be said out loud on a morning when about nine people had already said it, and that about four of them said he was not being listened to and that about four of them said he was not asking to be, and that about four of them have said since that the two men who said it out loud are at the two ends of that yard and the clerk said it in the middle of them. And about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard a fact said out loud that morning by the person who would know it best, and about four of them have said since that not one of them used it, and that about four of them said the using of it would have had to be out loud to count, and that about four of them said there was nothing to use it for this morning because nobody had asked her anything that it could answer.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a bar of iron at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and forty-sixth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty-five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the eightieth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and that page carries thirty-seven lines and none of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clerk who said out loud this morning that nobody had read that page wrote a morning on it in her own hand an hour later and took no line for it, and that about four of them said she said it was not a reason for anything and that it was not.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and went up that bank one at a time, and about four of them have said since that a yard can be told a thing out loud by the person who would know it best and still go up that hill without having settled it.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning and not one of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a fact said out loud by a clerk is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and nothing has come down that lane since the one hundred and eightieth morning. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and six days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and six days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and a clerk said out loud at those boards this morning that nobody in that yard has read the page at the back of that book and about nine people heard it and not one of them used it.
