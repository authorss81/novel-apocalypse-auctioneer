# Chapter 0916: The TWO HUNDREDTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month Nothing Happened In That Yard And The Boards Went Up After The Light Found The Eleven Feet And The Man Of Fifty-Six Said The Four Off That Wall Twice And The Boy Said The Lane Count At About Ten To Eight And Said No Figure And The Woman Who Keeps A Scale Weighed A Bar Of Iron And Said Nothing And The Man Of About Forty-Eight Who Keeps A Tally Said His Number Into About Four Hundred Yards Of Air And Nobody Wrote It Down And The Two Men At The Foot Of That Low Wall Said Good Afternoon And The Clerk Named The Morning And The Light Went Off The Boards At About Half Past Four And The Goodnight Was Said By About Four People Out Of About Nineteen And About Four People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Have Said Since That Nothing Is What Happened And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Seven Lines And It Did Not Carry A Thirty-Eighth And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and fifty-five days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and fourteen. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and thirty days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and forty-four days. Eight hundred and five days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and nineteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and twenty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and ninety-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and ninety-seven days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and thirty-three days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and seventy-three marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and fifty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and it is not said out loud again on any morning of this stretch, and that page did not take a line this morning.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and thirty mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and twenty-nine. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and eighty mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and forty-first of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fifty-fifth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and fifty-four of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and forty-fifth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the fifty-sixth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and fifteen days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

The boards went up after the light found the eleven feet, and the light stayed on the top of the boards longer than it usually does, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down and went to what they do.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on the stone afterwards the way he does and took it off again. At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then he said no figure, and said so out loud, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him and nobody said anything back to him.

---

At about half past seven the man who digs loam was at the north end with his barrow and the frost had come out of the ground there overnight and gone out of it again by the middle of the morning, and the ground was the colour of a wet slate and it turned over in slabs when the spade went in.

---

The form was still under the clean page at about four feet off the near end of the first table with the space on it empty, and the lot book was on the boards turned round with five lines in it, and a column with one name in it and the heading over that column still saying whoever.

---

At about a quarter to nine the man who made the stone came across with his chisel and set the stone on the low wall and worked at the joint along the top of it for the better part of two hours, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who works a joint that has been worked for weeks does not usually do it in the same place twice.

---

At about ten the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart came through the gap in the bank and went through that yard at the speed he comes through it and did not stop in the middle of it this time, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a cart went through that yard this morning and about four of them said nothing about it, which is what they said the last time it went through. The clerk stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table with the front of that book open from about eleven until about half past twelve and said nothing out loud to anybody, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came and looked at the front of it and went away again.

---

At about half past ten the man who makes things round had the wheel going and a piece of ash in it, and the shaving came off it and dropped into his hand, and about four people at the top of eleven feet did not stop what they were doing this time, and about four of them have said since that a ribbon off a turning wheel has been heard in that yard every morning for longer than anybody there can remember.

---

At about twelve the man of about thirty-four came down that bank and went to the near end of the second table and stood there with his hands in his pockets, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who knows three people and would not name them has been spending whole mornings at the end of that table saying nothing, and that about four of them have not said what he is waiting for.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a bar of iron at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and she wrote nothing down either, which she does not often not do. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fifty-fifth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and fifty-four of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

And about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

And the man at the end of the second table worked at the far end of it all morning and turned round once, in the middle of the morning, and looked down the length of the boards, and then went back to what he was doing, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who turns round once in a morning is not the same as a man who turns round twice.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the eighty-ninth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

Then the clerk wrote the morning on the page at the back of that lot book in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and that page carries thirty-seven lines and none of the thirty-seven is in the lot book. And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a morning with nothing in it and a morning with a word said out loud in it on another morning are the same morning to work in and are not the same morning, and that about four of them have not said which of those two is the one that counts.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning and not one of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a morning with nothing in it is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and nothing coming down that lane is the same as nothing being said about a lane. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and fifteen days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and fifteen days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and at about half past four the light went off those boards and about four people out of about nineteen said the goodnight, and that is the whole of this morning.
