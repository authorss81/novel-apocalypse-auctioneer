# Batch 0001 Review Record — The First Hammer, Chapters 1–10

Audit history for the first prose batch of Volume 01. This file is the record of what each review pass found, what it fixed, and what it deliberately left alone. It is not story state; `state/batch-0001-summary.md` is the handoff a writer needs, and this file is what a reviewer or a maintainer needs.

## How this batch was reviewed

Three passes, none of them independent. `scripts/novel_runner.sh` invokes the audit with `--agent novel-reviewer`, but `.opencode/agent/novel-reviewer.md` declares `mode: subagent` and `opencode.json` sets `default_agent: novel-writer`, so opencode refuses it and falls back. The fallback is visible in the run log:

```
logs/batch-0001.review.log:1  ! agent "novel-reviewer" is a subagent, not a primary agent. Falling back to default agent
logs/batch-0001.review.log:3  > novel-writer · space-bunny-free
```

So all three passes ran on the same model that wrote the prose minutes earlier, reviewing its own work. The fix belongs to the pipeline owner; see "Open for the owner" below.

## Pass 1 — first audit after the prose

Found and fixed: the memory toll was being given to Adrian while he stood at the fare box, which contradicted the toll's scope and undercut his later undertaking (he now pays the toll echo only, because he was never a traveler); the crossing count was recorded as seventeen travelers against a recorded refusal of one of the nine children; the witness-travel rule was broken because Alma Dren boarded the bus, which destroyed the appeal path (Alma now stays, and the far-bank duty passes to two escort stewards on sealed sheets); and the return platform's geography did not survive contact with the rest of the batch.

## Pass 2 — second audit against the bible, cards, and state files

No structural failure: no duplicated paragraphs, no meta text, no panel-discipline breach, no software jargon, and no ending that was a manufactured cutaway. Every chapter changes the situation and closes on a real beat, and the batch ends on an emotional shift earned in Chapter 9 rather than an interruption. Everything found was a concrete factual or bookkeeping defect, fixed in place, with the prose otherwise preserved.

- **The toll count was internally contradictory.** The charter named four of five staying on the far bank *and* named Oren Fisk as a further person there, making seventeen travelers against a recorded sixteen. Fixed by making Fisk one of the four. "Drivers" became "medicine crew" in every count-dependent position, including the Chapter 9 opening line, the spoken count, and the Chapter 16 return card. The audited arithmetic is 8 children + 2 escorts + 4 crew on the far bank, plus Tarin and Dessa who returned, equals the sixteen in the public book; the refused ninth child and the relief driver who gave back a held place are both outside it.
- **"Oren Fisk" renamed to "Renn Fisk."** Orren Pike and Oren Fisk were one letter apart and both speak in Chapter 9, so a reader could not resolve the collision in the exact scene where it occurs. A verification pass caught that the most dangerous instance had been missed: Fisk's acceptance of the far-bank duty still read "Oren said", immediately after Mara had refused Orren Pike any role, so a reader could have taken it as Orren accepting. That is now "Renn said."
- **The Chapter 2 circle and the Chapter 5 ring were asserted to be the same mark**, re-importing the identity the first pass had meant to dissolve, and calling a circle a ring. Chapter 5 now draws a fresh ring on Tarin's paper and leaves the old Line Six sheet folded away in the back office, distinct from it.
- **Iven Tallow and the blank lot were named in four state files and in a chapter card but never once in the manuscript.** Added a restrained passage at the end of Chapter 1 establishing the mentor, the unfilled lot card, and Adrian's platform memory, plus a short Chapter 2 beat where he sets the blank lot beside the token on the strength of an old instruction and gets nothing. The blank lot's function is still unrevealed and Iven is still presumed dead.
- **The near-side platform was declared empty in Chapter 4 and populated in Chapter 9** by an unnamed "Ward Nine shelter steward" who appears once, is never recorded, and cannot be Alma Dren. Chapter 4 now says the old shelter was emptied into the east ward and that people still wait under the canopy when a bus is due; Chapter 9 replaces the steward with a clinic runner and a handcart, motivated by Dessa's own statement that she would bring the next team across if the first load held.
- **The return platform's bank was the batch's worst defect, and it hid from the first audit because the arithmetic was right while the geography was wrong.** Chapter 9's destination and return both used the *Ward Nine old platform's* signature features — bell post, cracked rails, canopy — while Chapter 4 put that platform on the near apron and Chapter 10 puts the bell post a few steps from the Lot 17 fare box. The return therefore read as landing across the water, three paragraphs before a Warden lifts the token into Lot 17's glass case. An attempted fix made it worse by naming the far destination "the Ward Nine platform, the far one", which contradicted the charter's own marked return. Resolved in the prose: the bus crosses to a **receiving ramp on the far side** and comes home to the **old platform on the near apron**, with the river on its own side of the wall and the depot lamps burning where they had been all along.
- **Chapter 2 put the stranded bus on two different banks inside one chapter.** The near-side reading is now canon, because that is what keeps Chapter 5's "a small red ring inside the driver's own wide one" and the near-side return platform working.
- **The margin warning sat on three surfaces at once** — manifest, old map, Tarin's map. It is on the manifest margin throughout, the only surface Chapter 2 shows it being written on.
- **The route's approach to the line was called two things** — tram wall in Chapter 9, west wall in Chapter 10. Both are the west wall.
- **Chapter 6 called the token "under the audit seal"** in Mara's suspension line, one beat before she presses a new seal on it. She now says it goes back under seal the moment she finishes writing.
- **Chapter 10's public book under-described its own rule.** RESPONSIBLE PARTIES said the stewards were "named on a sealed page", singular, when Chapter 9 created two sheets. It now says two, and where each one is.
- **State-file corrections, no prose change:** the two sealed sheets are recorded; the Chapter 6 memory bleed is recorded as a one-off mystery instead of being left to be mistaken for a rule; the Chapter 9 summary states that Mara recorded that closing personally, so a later writer does not give the panel a refusal it never made; the Chapter 4 summary no longer credits Mara with identifying the bell post, which is Selik's; Adrian's character-state entry says outright that he was never a traveler. The **Quiet Registry**, named in Chapter 7 as the body Lina's Unlisted households fear, was an unregistered institution and is now logged in `state/open-threads.md`.
- **Verified clean:** Adrian's memory toll versus toll echo is correctly scoped; the sixteen reconcile by side in both Chapter 9 and Chapter 10; the witness-does-not-travel rule, the bare-claimant default, the inactive custody charter, the Stage 1 limits, the toll-versus-security separation, and the broken-circle constraint all hold; the ending constraints are untouched and Iven is not prematurely identified.

## Pass 3 — third audit, findings fixed in this phase

The prose was preserved; every change below is a repair to a specific defect.

- **The clinic porter could not be resolved against Renn Fisk.** Chapters 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6 carry an unnamed clinic porter who sets the six-hour coolant clock that drives the batch: he boards in Chapter 5, is pulled out of the wreck in Chapter 6, and is listed by Mara among the affected. Chapter 9 then introduces Renn Fisk, "the crew's porter", as one of the four who stay on the far bank and the only one whose name was on the cold-chain sheet — the same sheet the Chapter 5 porter checks coolant against. The prose implied the two were one man, never said so, and no state file settled it, so either reading failed: a reader had to absorb a silent third crossing, or the clock-setting porter exited unresolved while a never-before-seen porter took the far bank. Fixed in the prose, not in a note. Mara's Chapter 6 affected list now reads **Renn Fisk, clinic porter**, which is what a registrar does when writing a list of affected people. Chapter 9 identifies him in the scene as the man who kept a hand on the nearest cold-box lid, called the coolant down from six hours and then four and forty minutes, crossed once and was winched back out of a flooded service wall with Tarin. His second crossing is now an informed decision by a person the reader has watched, not the entrance of a stranger. Recorded in `bible/terminology.md`, `state/continuity.md`, `state/character-state.md`, and the Chapter 6 and 9 chapter summaries.
- **The bank disambiguation was in the continuity file instead of the charter that caused the collision.** Three uses of "Ward Nine" inside nine lines of Chapter 9 meant opposite banks: the marked return at the *old* platform on the near apron, the people waiting in *Ward Nine* on the far bank, and the return condition at "the Ward Nine platform", bare. The disambiguator "old" appeared once and was absent from the term and from all three Chapter 10 lot-book entries, so a reader had no in-fiction way to resolve it. Both now carry the bank in the text: Chapter 9's use, term, and remedy name "the old Ward Nine platform on this side of the water", and its beneficiaries name "the people waiting in Ward Nine on the far bank"; Chapter 10's USE and TERM name "the old Ward Nine platform on the near apron of this bank" and BENEFICIARIES names "the waiting clinic line in Ward Nine on the far bank". The Chapter 9 card's continuity note was aligned to match.
- **New protagonist canon lived in prose and state but not in the bible.** The Chapter 1 passage establishing Adrian's platform memory (a clean hand that got eleven people off and left four) and naming Iven Tallow was treated as canon in `state/character-state.md` and `state/open-threads.md`, but `bible/characters.md` carried neither, even though the same pass had updated `bible/world.md` and `bible/terminology.md` for the geography. Adrian's fear section now states the origin, Iven's entry now records that Adrian names him and the blank lot in Chapter 1 and sets the lot against the token in Chapter 2 and gets nothing, and the **Blank lot** entry no longer says only that Adrian "carries it from the opening of the story" now that it is on the page in Chapter 1.
- **"on it" in Chapter 5 lost the antecedent the fix was meant to preserve.** Pass 2 changed "He drew a small red ring on the sheet" to "on it", but the nearest nouns are the old Line Six and a service joint between the tram boards, and the next sentence introduces the old Line Six sheet as a different object. Canon is Tarin's patched map, which Chapter 6 depends on. It now reads "on the patched map".
- **The crew and the escorts were identically dressed.** "Nine children and two escorts in blue coats" sat beside "five of the medicine crew stood with it in blue coats", so the reader had to separate five crew from two escorts on clothing alone at the exact moment the batch asks them to hold a sixteen-person count. The escorts are now in grey canvas coats in both of their appearances, and the medicine crew keep the blue.
- **Four contentless nods.** Chapter 8 (twice), Chapter 9, and Chapter 10 each used "nodded" as a beat with no content in it, which `AGENTS.md` names as mechanical filler. Chapter 4's "He nodded toward a line of red-stained cable" is directional and load-bearing, so it stayed. `wrote` (26 uses) and `counted` (7) were checked and left: nearly every instance is Mara putting a term into a record, and the spoken count is a scene event. Across 22,281 words, 26 uses of `wrote` is roughly one per 850 words and is the subject matter of the book, not filler.
- **The audit record was living inside the handoff file.** `state/batch-0001-summary.md` had grown a second-pass audit log and a card reconciliation, roughly 25 lines that the next writer must load without needing. This file is now that record; the batch summary keeps the plan deviations, which the next writer does need, plus a short pointer to this file and the two facts from this pass that change the fiction.

## Card beats the prose declined, and why

The approved cards in `outline/batches/volume-01-batch-0001.md` promised beats the finished prose does not contain. Each was a reasonable thing for the prose to decline and none leaves a hole a reader can feel, so they were not written in.

- **Chapter 10, "Resistance"** is the largest gap: the card listed a crowd demanding confiscation, Selik demanding a permanent bridge closure, Unlisted families fearing the board, and Adrian wanting another crossing before the next shortage. In the prose Selik *accepts* the review, no crowd appears, no Unlisted family voices fear, and Adrian is notably cautious. Only Halloway's offer to manage future crossings survives.
- **Chapter 10** also promised the Tally marking the Last Fare's use as temporary. The Tally does not appear in Chapter 10; the equivalent information arrives through Mara's civic ledger and municipal seal.
- **Chapter 9** promised Selik's Wardens detaining the buyer and separating the carriers from the counterfeit token. No carrier is detained and no carrier ever held a counterfeit; only the buyer's arm is caught. The same card promised Adrian stopping "the bidding" when a passenger refuses, but there is no bidding in Chapter 9 — it is a charter reading, and the refusal comes from the boy.
- **Chapter 7** had Lina reached through an Unlisted courier, but she is physically present from her entrance onward; the courier carries messages, not Lina.
- **Chapter 4** had Adrian refusing to surrender the token to Selik's administration, but Selik never asks for it, and the beat survives only as pre-empted dialogue in the car.
- The **four-seat board composition** was never written into the batch-0001 card file; it exists in the batch-0002 cards, which is where the next writer needs it.

## Open for the owner, not fixable by the writer

These are pipeline defects, not manuscript defects. They are recorded here because the writer is forbidden from editing `scripts/`, `.github/workflows/`, `.opencode/agent/`, `opencode.json`, and `state/phase-ledger.json`, and because a future maintainer reading only the manuscript would never know they exist.

- **The review phase is not an independent review.** `scripts/novel_runner.sh` runs the audit with `--agent novel-reviewer`, but that agent is declared `mode: subagent` and opencode falls back to the default writer agent. Both earlier audit passes on this batch were therefore the writer checking its own prose, which is how the porter identity defect survived two passes. Either promote the reviewer to a primary agent or invoke the reviewer through a supported path.
- **`state/phase-ledger.json` is dead state.** Last touched at bootstrap, it still reads `currentPhase: phase-000-bootstrap`, `status: planned`, `attempts: 0` after four phases, while `PHASE_SYSTEM.md` states that self-dispatch selects the next batch by reading it. Nothing in `scripts/` or the workflow references it; progress is actually driven by `.done` markers. Either wire the ledger up or correct the documentation.
- **`workspace/volume-01/batch-0001/.done` does not exist**, so batch-0001 is still formally in progress to whatever reads markers. Batch writers must not create phase markers themselves.
- **A stale WIP branch would roll back the whole batch.** `origin/novel-wip/batch-0001` is an unmerged sibling of `main` from before the final two passes, and merging it would revert the renames, the geography, and the ring fix. Nothing unique is lost in it, but any stale-WIP handler that resurrects that branch reverses the current work. This needs a maintainer with branch rights.

## Deliberately not fixed

- **Chapter 9 is 3,970 words** against a 1,700–2,200 norm for the rest of the batch. Defensible for a climax, and cutting finished prose to hit a word count is a worse outcome than a long climax. The batch simply has no short chapter in which to land the emotional beat; the beat lands inside Chapter 9 instead. Noted, not actioned.
- **Whether anything is left to take from a traveler who has already paid the memory toll once.** The Chapter 16 return depends on it and the prose has not answered it, which is the point of that card.
- **The ending.** Iven remains the final antagonist, the blank lot's function stays unrevealed, and no new System or final enemy was introduced by any of these three passes.
