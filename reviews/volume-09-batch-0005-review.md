### 2. Chapter 433's clock, rebuilt on one rate

This was the review's finding 6 and it is a real defect: the day printed three incompatible totals and a per-mark rate that supported none of them. It started *about ten past eight*, reached the mark for the first of this month *about twenty past ten*, said separately that the watching was *an hour and a half* and the second pass *about an hour and a quarter*, and put a finger on a mark *every eleven or twelve seconds*, which is thirty-one marks in about six minutes.

**The clock was rebuilt so that one rate supports both passes and every other time already on the page lands inside the result. No scene was moved.**

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| per-mark rate | *every eleven or twelve seconds* | *about every four minutes* |
| first pass | 08:10 → 10:20, and *an hour and a half* | 08:10 → 10:14, at about four minutes a mark |
| second pass | *about an hour and a quarter* | *about two hours*, 10:30 → about half past twelve |
| the protagonist watching | *At about one*, eleven minutes | *At about twenty past twelve*, the last eleven minutes |

Thirty-one marks at about four minutes is 124 minutes, which is the 2h04m the prose now implies, and both passes finish where the day's other anchors need them to be. Verified on the page after the edit: the man of fifty-six missing his half-past-nine reading; the mender silent until half past eleven; the reed cutter at twelve; the man the figure of twenty-one years is against watching the tail of the count at twenty past twelve; the mender's question at half past one; the first reading of the day at half past five. **The man the figure of twenty-one years is against's paragraph already said he watched a woman count a month for about eleven minutes — that sentence was true of the scene and false of the clock, and moving his arrival to twenty past twelve makes both true at once.**

The closing passage's garbled date is fixed separately: the board runs this morning, then thirty, then the first of the eleventh, then the first of the tenth, then a gap, and the mark that is missing is **the first of the ninth**. *The ninth of the ninth* is also a real day in this book, which is what made it a bad corruption — the sentence was denying the existence of the very mark whose absence is the passage's argument.
