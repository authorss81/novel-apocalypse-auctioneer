# Volume 04 Summary: A Price for Tomorrow — the roll for Chapters 151–200

**Volume 04 is complete at Chapter 200.** This is the roll for the whole volume and it is the first document a Volume 05 writer should read. It is not a summary of chapters; it is a statement of what the volume built, what it cost, and what is still open. The per-chapter record is `state/chapter-summaries.md`; the five batch records are `state/volume-04-batch-0001-summary.md` through `state/volume-04-batch-0005-summary.md`; the running state is `state/current.md`; the live threads are `state/open-threads.md`; the canon card for the last ten chapters is `outline/batches/volume-04-batch-0005.md`. **The roll for Volume 03, which a Volume 04 writer read first, is `state/volume-03-roll-summary.md`.**

## The sentence the volume turned on

**An underwriters' network offers abundant water and seed by accepting future labour from families who cannot inspect the fine print, and one missed payment can take a child's claim to a public shelter.** The instrument the district lacks is the one that would let a stranger read a cost off a piece of paper instead of off a person, and fifty chapters of this volume are the method of Volume 03 — ask a person in the six things, and then write down what they said in their own words — applied to a subject on which the method is not neutral, because asking a family what a claim costs is asking a person who cannot read the sheet the claim is on.

**And the volume's finding, which arrives in its last ten chapters, is that the method's best work was the disclosure and its worst property was the room.** A yard that published nothing got a man found at the end of a lane anyway, because the district's instrument is a room with a door on it and the door has a name over it. A page a stranger may read is a place a house can look for a man. **The man who wanted a power, and who could not have one, finally has one — and the power required a book a stranger can walk up to, and the book's price was a hand on a page, and the hand was found on the twenty-ninth, and the man who put it there had known on the nineteenth what that would cost and had not said.**

## The opening position, restated for a Volume 05 writer

**The derivation, and it is the one a Volume 05 writer should copy out rather than inherit: from the eleventh of February, seventeen days remain in that month after the eleventh; March, April, May, June, July and August come to 31 + 30 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 31 = 184 between them, and 17 + 184 = 201 at the thirty-first of August; September adds thirty, October thirty-one, November thirty, December thirty-one, and 201 + 122 = 323 at the thirty-first of December; the first of January is 324; January is thirty-one, February is twenty-eight and March is thirty-one, which is 90, and 324 + 90 = 413 at the last day of March, and the first of April is 414.** This was read out loud in Chapter 200, in two runs of words with the sums under the figures, by the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray.

**THE YEAR IN THAT DERIVATION, WHICH IS IN NO CHAPTER AND IS THE ONE THING A WRITER WHO DERIVES LITERALLY WILL GET WRONG.** The eleventh of February is of the **seventeenth year**, the year before the eighteenth the volume closes in, and the tally runs from it as day zero across the year boundary: 1 January is 324 because the eleventh of February to the thirty-first of December is 323 days. Counted as a plain interval inside one year, the eleventh of February to the thirty-first of March is **forty-eight** and not four hundred and thirteen. **Two checks hold the year in place: 400 days on the eighteenth of the third month in Chapter 194, 393 on the eleventh in Chapter 191 and 408 on the twenty-sixth in Chapter 198, and the board's 97 days at the last day of the volume, which is 413 less 316.**

**February in the eighteenth year after the Long Fracture has twenty-eight days, and Chapter 186 stands on the twenty-eighth as the last day of the month.** A count that uses thirty-one for February is wrong, and one figure in a Volume 04 draft was wrong for exactly that reason and is disclosed in the batch record.

## The volume's method, stated flat

**A person is asked in the six things, and then what they said is written down in their own words, and the count of what was not asked is kept in a column and is not a bucket.** That is the instrument and it is the only one this district has, and it works if a person comes to the yard, and the volume spent its last ten chapters finding out that this is a strength and a door at the same time. **A page a stranger may read is a place a house can look for a man. A page nobody owns is a place nobody can look in. Neither of those is a protection, and the second of them is not one either. A name on a page is a claim on the person and not on the house. A person at the back of a book is not a bearer and has never been able to be. A mark may not carry a job and a line of charcoal is not a mark and a line of stones is not a mark either. A toll is a thing a named person pays under a term, and a yoke-load is a job, and work done by hand is not a toll and is not nothing, and a district that calls it nothing has decided not to count it and a decision not to count is a decision and is entered as one. A name cannot be taken off a figure by the person it is put on. A house that has not named the day has left the naming to a person who cannot read. A person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way and the record says not asked. A person who withdraws has not thereby consented to the season they withdrew from. A year is not a price and there is no rate in this basin that turns one into coppers. A date is the last day somebody can do something, and the name came off on the thirty-first of March because a date arrived and not because a man asked, and a court that can write a thing down is not a court that can end anything, and infrastructure and justice are two fights and this district has now won the first one and is going into the second one with nothing.**
