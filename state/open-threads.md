# Open Threads

Author-facing promises after Chapters 1–20. The fixed series ending remains intact.

## Volume 01 live threads

- **The missing return group — the batch's live pressure.** Nine people are unaccounted for between the banks: **Tarin Kest, Dessa Rill, three escort stewards, and five of the eleven shelter children.** They boarded the far receiving ramp's bus after it turned at nine minutes past, and the route closed at twelve minutes with the token still in the slot. **Do not resolve them, restore the memory, or name the cause in Chapters 21–30.** The full reversal is Chapters 23–26: several returners are diverted into a dangerous false destination, backwash exposes Adrian's concealed return term and his private workshop guarantee, and Mara stops the route and challenges his resemblance to Iven. The aftermath — recovery, a published failed charter, a real payment of part of the backwash, Selik's extended closure and his argument for a permanent Warden route, and the counterfeit becoming organized public work — runs to Chapter 33.
- **Twenty people are stranded on the far bank.** Five medicine crew, nine signal hands and riggers, six children of the second shelter group, and two of the three escort stewards. The public road is suspended, so there is no announced way to fetch them, and that is a direct cost of the suspension rather than an accident of it.
- **The maintenance spur has not been searched.** The search party stopped at the river wall on Selik's order. The near-side maintenance spur, a few streets west of the depot, is the Chapter 6 false destination, and the Chapter 20 bell-post arm has been turned four yards past the joint with nobody near the post. Both facts are in the public record with no cause attached, and that is the state they must be written from.
- **The return condition Adrian promised and did not write.** He stated in a yard, to two hundred and forty people, that it would be written within the hour. He then said the sentence that makes a signed promise non-binding — *the outward crossing is the emergency, the return term can be written within the hour, and nobody is being deceived because nothing has happened yet* — and it is now in the public book in his own hand, twice, by two different people. He has learned the shape of the mistake and not yet its cost. Do not let him repair it by simply writing the term; the cost has to arrive first.
- **The private workshop guarantee.** Inspection press (three hundred and eighty by ward market), repair-crew term (one season, three named hands), four hundred and ten plus a season, forfeit to the remedy on breach. It is real, it is his, and it is **not in the public lot book**. He is Stage 1 and cannot post security; the book already records the press and crew as *available, not posted*. This is the asset that backwash exposes in Chapters 23–26, and the public's real question is not whether he owns a press but why the number that made them feel safe was not in anything they could read.
- **The cracked token.** The Last Fare is cracked through the plate after three activations: one bare, one witnessed, one witnessed again. It is not known whether it opens again, and repeated use may strand the route permanently. It has also started answering questions nobody asked it in plain lines — `A TRAVELER WHO HAS ALREADY PAID: NOT STATED` in Chapter 16 and `RETURN: NO CONDITION IN THE RECORD` in Chapter 20. That is a behaviour to escalate carefully, not a mechanic to rely on.
- **Rell Bode's challenge.** A remedy reached by naming the person is not a remedy for a person who refused to be named. The board upheld it in part and the **no-name remedy** is the standing answer, but it works by failing loudly in public, and nobody in Alder Reach has yet had to pay for that twice. This is the thread that runs under the Unlisted boundary, the appeal path, and the whole question of what a public lot is for.
- **The public lot board.** Four seats, no owner. It can refuse to record a closing, publish a result, call a review, uphold a challenge in part, and set or renew the public custody term. It cannot grant a use. **Every chapter that gives it authority must name all four seats aloud and put them in the room.** Nessa Pell's seven-day steward term has been renewed twice in public and has two days left. The open question the batch poses and has not answered: can a board hold a public undertaking, or does it only hold objects?
- **Adrian's signed undertaking.** Breached, out loud, in a yard, and entered in the public book. It is now evidence against him rather than reassurance. Mara's next task is to make it enforceable by somebody other than her own reading of his character.
- **Adrian and Mara.** Conditional professional trust, strained past the point of procedure. He cannot be reached by the public process he helped build. She suspended the road herself. There is no romantic resolution and no softening, and he has not begun to understand what he has to give up.
- **Tarin Kest.** Missing. His refusal in the Chapter 19 yard — no return term, no charter, and he would rather fail in daylight — was a form of safety, and he then owned his own part in the postponement rather than letting Adrian be the one who finally said it. He is also the only person who can explain a named-pair return point, and Chapter 20 has produced a turned bell-post arm that only he can interpret. Bryn is a personal pressure, not a plot rescue, and has had exactly one page so far.
- **Selik's bridge.** Tighter and more credible than at the start of the batch, on the strength of a false alarm and a correct evacuation that turned back a woman with a feverish child. The gauge housing is certified; the manifest is tightened; the review continues on the ninth; Selik accepted the review's written question and refused Mara's remedy. He holds a flag on a real joint and refuses to hold one on a sentence, and he said in a yard that Adrian is the only person in Alder Reach regulated by a promise he wrote himself. **Chapters 27–33 are his volume: he uses the failure to argue for a permanent Warden route, and he must be argued with rather than defeated.**
- **Halloway's counterfeit.** A printing plate, not four forgers: three brokers in three parts of the city carrying identical miscut type, with a false salt-loading return and a seven-day carrier labor clause folded inside a fold. Halloway has publicly conceded that he sold the first one, rents the room to the three, cannot shut it down, and now offers a **fifteen-day term falling on himself** written on the first page. He is not defeated and he is not a cartoon. The carriers who refuse the fold are still the only reliable source of information about it, and the counterfeit still reaches people who cannot afford certainty.
- **The Unlisted boundary.** A group, a count, a steward, a destination and a remedy in the book; a paper note of who chose to take part held outside it by the people the house chooses; a sealed page for anyone who must be identified. Wenna Keel chose to have her name written; Bett Ruhl's household gave nothing and took care anyway. Lina has stopped expecting to be filed and says so out loud. It must not deny water, shelter, or medicine, and it must not become a new private monopoly on secrecy.
- **Orren Pike.** He gave Adrian the worst of the six warnings in Chapter 17 — *say what it costs and who pays it separately* — after Adrian had already promised to, which is what makes Chapter 18 a choice. He keeps the counterfeit evidence, he has separated his mother's name from it, and he wrote the first third-page warning sheet in the city at four in the morning. He still does not trust the process. Do not let him become a safe apprentice, and do not restore Sela Pike.
- **Sela Pike.** Orren has cut the margin note away from the counterfeit evidence and left it with Bett Ruhl. The record of her erasure is no longer in the bundle he is bringing to the public process, and he has not told anyone that he made that choice. The name remains evidence and grief, not a person restored.
- **The broken circle.** One impression on unrelated documents, used as a claim that a piece of paper came out of one settlement. **It is a coat, not a signature, and Orren has established that by finding it on a Warden fuel return, a school ration ledger and a pre-Fracture salt permit.** No office, clerk, place, or person has been identified and the regional heading is still scraped away. Volume 01 must not answer it.
- **Iven and the blank lot.** Named in Chapters 1 and 2. Iven remains presumed dead; the blank lot's function is unrevealed. Chapters 23–26 are where Mara will say, out loud and without proof of a conspiracy, that Adrian is making the same emergency exception Iven made and calling it temporary. **That accusation must be moral and professional, not a reveal.** Iven's living custodianship and the Great Closing stay closed.
- **The Quiet Registry.** Named in Chapter 7 as the body the Unlisted fear. It has not acted, has no named officer, and must not be developed in this volume.

## Volume 01 planned escalations

- Chapters 11–16: **done.** The first public road hardened; the lot book given a proposal schedule, a no-name remedy, and a board that runs its own closing; the counterfeit traced to a plate and to a mark that is a claim about a system; the Unlisted witness channel made administrable; a false-alarm evacuation tightening Selik's span; the fourteen on the far bank brought home.
- Chapters 17–26: in progress and **not to be completed before Chapter 26**. The larger medicine crisis, the first full public auction, the breach of the signed undertaking, the private workshop guarantee, the outward success, and the missing return. **Chapters 21–30 should carry the search, the discovery of the diverted returners and the false destination, the backwash's exposure of the concealed term and the private security, and Mara's stopping of the route — and should not spend the last of them on repair.**
- Chapters 27–33: Adrian publishes the failed terms, pays a real portion of the backwash, loses reputation and inventory, and helps make the counterfeit dispute a public service rather than a moral warning. Selik argues for a permanent Warden route from the failure.
- Chapters 34–42: affected people write terms instead of bargaining for an exception. The first multi-bearer public auction fails. Mara forces a rewrite with visible release and appeal, and Adrian posts Lot 17's inspection press and a named repair-crew term as distinct security, beginning the Binder transition.
- Chapters 43–50: the witnessed public climax sells one defined use of the Last Fare, not the object or a permanent road. The convoy and shelter group cross, the return memory is lost, the token is damaged, and public custody replaces private ownership.

## Mystery schedule

- **Answer by the ending:** why the Tally exists, why anchor lots remember the failed Common Measure, why it recognizes Adrian, what Iven's custodianship concealed, and how the Great Closing is broken without private rule.
- **Reveal in progression:** the Last Fare's shared closing mark; the First House and removed founding signature; the blank lot as the Common Measure's distributed settlement seat; the difference between mechanical recognition and valid consent.
- **Intentionally unresolved:** who first proposed the Common Measure, the identity and purpose of the removed signature, the precise order of the Year 0 contract failures, and whether comparable records exist beyond the Morrow Basin.

## Fixed endpoint

Iven remains the final antagonist. His linked founder-mark forced record must be replaced through the Tally's replacement rule: the final term-limited covenant must expressly name the override, state its remedy, and receive acceptance from at least two fresh independent witnesses. Adrian pays a voluntary one-time toll rather than losing security through breach, gives up exceptional reading, and leaves a Civic Tally that works without him. No new System or final enemy may replace this resolution.
