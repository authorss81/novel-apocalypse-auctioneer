# Open Threads

Author-facing promises after Chapters 1–30. The fixed series ending remains intact.

## Volume 01 live threads

- **The search is closed; the accounts are not.** The nine were found at the near-side maintenance spur on the **nineteenth**, at nine minutes past seven. Eight lived. **One of the six children died in the small hours of the twentieth with no name in any public book**, at an hour nobody in the room was keeping and an hour the registrar has refused to invent, and the remedy was called and **not discharged**. The nineteen stranded on the far bank were walked home over the footbridge on the eleventh and twelfth, five crossings of five on the deck, and **the middle plank of that footbridge went into the service cut on the fifth and last crossing with four children on the deck**, so the far bank has no crossing of any kind again. What a footbridge can carry is a working number rather than a written rule: **five on the deck, twenty-two minutes a crossing, four usable hours a day.** **The volume's mid-arc question is now: can a public route be written so that a failure of it lands on paper instead of on a person?** Chapters 31–33 finish paying for the batch, and Chapters 34–42 are where terms get written and the first multi-bearer auction fails.
- **The unpaid return toll is still unpaid.** Nobody in Alder Reach has paid it, because the return leg never ran. The backwash it produced landed on four documents and not on a body, and the object has said twice that the toll is not paid. **It has not been discharged by Adrian's payment**, which is a payment on a claim that has never been stated to anybody. Whether the spill is finished, ongoing, or waiting is not known and is the volume's standing dread.
- **The movement of the marking.** Established: a hand was in the bell-post socket, inside a day or two of the crossing. **Not established, and not to be established in this volume: who, how, or why.** One nailed-heel print going toward the river wall belongs to nobody. Adrian has wanted a person to blame since one o'clock on the nineteenth and has not found one. The nine-line return term now in the book answers it procedurally — *if the marking is not where the pair says it is, the joint is the return and the arm is not* — and that answer is unsatisfying on purpose.
- **The pilot line.** A certified gauge housing on an empty, shut span drew a mark at 7:37 on the morning of the nineteenth on a channel with no instrument on it. The certificate is suspended by the water office on the twenty-first, **over Selik's own written instruction to leave the pilot attached**, the span is shut, and nobody in Alder Reach can account for it. **That sequence is canon: Selik sent two words on a slip, decided not to act on them, sent the reading to a public board instead, and was reversed by a fitter who was annoyed with the slip and not with him. Do not retell it as Selik procuring the suspension.** **Two separate unexplained things now stand in Alder Reach within three hundred yards of each other — a bus that arrived with nobody driving it and an instrument that moved on a bridge that did not — and neither has been joined to the other in any document.** Do not join them. The joining is the next volume's mistake, not this volume's answer.
- **Rell Bode's challenge, now twice paid for.** A remedy reached by naming the person is not a remedy for a person who refused a name. It has now been discharged once, publicly, in a yard, and it has failed once, publicly, over a child. The no-name remedy is the standing answer and the batch proved its cost in the one currency it is written in. This thread runs under the Unlisted boundary, the appeal path, and the whole question of what a public lot is for, and it is the thread Chapters 34–42 must be written on top of.
- **The public lot board.** Four seats, no owner. It renewed Nessa Pell's term for seven days in public on the twenty-first, refused to record a closing whose return field held a promise, refused to let a line be struck off the notice board, and cannot strike one. **The open question the batch asked and answered halfway: a board can hold a public undertaking now — it did, by entering Adrian's private undertaking as *offered and not verified* and handing the valuation to the yard. What it cannot do is audit one.**
- **The nine-line return term, offered and unattached.** It is in the public book, it has been read by a barrow-hauler and a tea seller and found to have no fold in it, and it is attached to no use. **It is the only good thing Adrian has produced since the token appeared and it governs nothing.** The next charter that reaches for it will be the test of whether a good term survives contact with a crowd.
- **The public road.** Suspended. No token opens at Lot 17 until a new proposal is entered, read out in the yard, witnessed by two fresh people, and recorded. The token is cracked through the plate, on its fourth and last activation, and it may not open again. **Chapters 34–42 have to produce a road out of terms rather than out of a token**, and the whole of Volume 01's promise is that a Hearth Line is administratively possible and not dependable.
- **Adrian and Mara.** The rupture has completed and become work. She reads his failure out in the middle of the day; he publishes it. **No romantic resolution, no softening, and he has begun to understand that the thing she is asking for is the right to be wrong in public.** Her process is now on a document she cannot audit, her own instrument is in the spill, and she has agreed to a limit imposed by a cook.
- **Tarin Kest.** Home. He obeyed the far-side condition, saved eight people, and lost a child, and he says both halves out loud and puts it on himself. He is the only bearer there is, he has said so, and in Chapter 25 Adrian reached for him and the yard would not have him. Bryn has had one page and one refusal, and a refusal with a reason.
- **Selik's bridge and his standing order.** Shut to all carriage, certificate suspended by people who are not him, inspection public and paid for by the ward market. **The closure order states that one office is responsible for the crossing at St. Orra**, and a standing order for a permanently staffed crossing with no expiry is being drafted in a room Adrian is not in. Selik is right on the merits, has said in public that Adrian's failure is why his instrument got examined, and is not gloating. **Chapters 34–42 must argue with him, not defeat him.**
- **Halloway's counterfeit, now a service race.** The fourth page is published on the back of ward market stock and staffed by named people at four in the morning. **Halloway says in public that the counterfeit is more credible after the failure, and he is not lying**, and he has said in a yard that Adrian has built the only thing he has been afraid of in six years. The carriers who refuse the fold are still the only reliable source of information about it. Orren's third page is what people carry; Orren's fourth page is what the table is made of; **the counterfeit is a demand problem, not a police problem, and no arrest resolves it.**
- **Joss Amery's three days.** A carter who lost three whole days on a counterfeit with no fold in it, a barrow-load of clinic stores at the bottom of a flooded maintenance trench, and a coal ration board altered in the wrong quarter. **Nobody can put a claim on a person they cannot point at, and his household cannot ask him anything.** This is the standing demonstration of why disclosure has to be organised as a service and not as a warning, and it has not been paid out.
- **The Unlisted boundary, now public.** Lina Vale has put her name in the public book as the steward of a passage of nineteen people, and she has said in a kitchen that this is the price and that the reason she pays it is that on the night of the nineteenth there was nobody in any book who could be sent to look for the nine. The exchange has no address and is described publicly as *the place with the tea*, and Bett Ruhl's floor and the four coppers of flour and Colm Vaun's refusal are the standing proof that a protected category is a thing somebody pays for. **It must not become a new private monopoly on secrecy, and it must not become a conversion.**
- **Orren Pike.** His Chapter 17 warning is now the front of the public book as a list of nine things the charter did not contain. His stolen method is public work. He has been limited by a cook and accepted it. He is not safe, not an apprentice, and not a restoration of Sela.
- **Sela Pike.** The erasure record remains separated from the evidence and in Bett Ruhl's keeping, and Orren has still told nobody that he chose that. **Unresolved, and to be left unresolved in this volume.**
- **The broken circle.** One impression on unrelated documents, a claim about a system rather than a signature, no office, no clerk, no name, the regional heading still scraped away. **Volume 01 must not answer it, and this batch adds one temptation and no answer: two unexplained events three hundred yards apart in one week.**
- **Iven and the blank lot.** Adrian says the name in a yard in Chapter 25, as evidence against himself, and says nothing about whether the man is alive. Mara's accusation is about a **shape** and proves nothing. Iven remains presumed dead from Adrian's point of view; the blank lot's function is unrevealed.
- **The Quiet Registry.** Undeveloped, unacted, no named officer, and must stay that way.
- **A yard that cannot agree on a word.** Chapter 26 ends with the market unable to choose between *urgent*, *scheduled*, and *priority* for a medicine case, and the inability is institutional rather than emotional. **A vocabulary for scarcity is a Volume 02 problem and is seeded here.**

## Volume 01 planned escalations

- Chapters 1–16: **done.** The first public road hardened; the lot book given a proposal schedule, a no-name remedy, and a board that runs its own closing; the counterfeit traced to a plate and to a mark that is a claim about a system; the Unlisted witness channel made administrable; a false-alarm evacuation tightening Selik's span; the fourteen on the far bank brought home.
- Chapters 17–26: **done.** The breach, the private workshop guarantee, the outward success, the missing return, the find, the dead child, the bill, the second emergency exception, the accusation, the publication of the failed charter, and the loss of Adrian's standing.
- Chapters 27–33: **partly done in Batch 0003.** Adrian has published the failed terms, paid a real and named portion at a figure he set, lost the ward market and two lots and the reading, and the counterfeit dispute has become an organised public service. **Chapters 31–33 have left to pay for it and to put Selik's permanent-route argument in front of a public book rather than a review table.**
- Chapters 34–42: affected people write terms instead of bargaining for an exception. The first multi-bearer public auction fails. Mara forces a rewrite with visible release and appeal, and Adrian posts Lot 17's inspection press and a named repair-crew term as distinct security, beginning the Binder transition. **Note: the press has already been priced by the yard at six hundred and twenty-nine and already been given away, so the Chapter 34–42 posting is a different asset and a different question, and the batch's new rule about a payment that is not a security is the language the rewrite should be argued in.**
- Chapters 43–50: the witnessed public climax sells one defined use of the Last Fare, not the object or a permanent road. The convoy and shelter group cross, the return memory is lost, the token is damaged, and public custody replaces private ownership.

## Mystery schedule

- **Answer by the ending:** why the Tally exists, why anchor lots remember the failed Common Measure, why it recognizes Adrian, what Iven's custodianship concealed, and how the Great Closing is broken without private rule.
- **Reveal in progression:** the Last Fare's shared closing mark; the First House and removed founding signature; the blank lot as the Common Measure's distributed settlement seat; the difference between mechanical recognition and valid consent.
- **Intentionally unresolved:** who first proposed the Common Measure, the identity and purpose of the removed signature, the precise order of the Year 0 contract failures, whether comparable records exist beyond the Morrow Basin, **why the bell-post arm moved, and why the pilot line deviated.**

## Fixed endpoint

Iven remains the final antagonist. His linked founder-mark forced record must be replaced through the Tally's replacement rule: the final term-limited covenant must expressly name the override, state its remedy, and receive acceptance from at least two fresh independent witnesses. Adrian pays a voluntary one-time toll rather than losing security through breach, gives up exceptional reading, and leaves a Civic Tally that works without him. No new System or final enemy may replace this resolution.
