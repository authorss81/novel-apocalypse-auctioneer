# Open Threads

Author-facing promises after Chapters 1–40. The fixed series ending remains intact.

## Volume 01 live threads

- **The road does not exist and the terms do.** The public road is suspended, no fifth activation is on the record, the token is behind a locked door on a seven-day term renewed four times in public (the twenty-first, the twenty-ninth, the fifth and the twelfth), and the official span is shut to everything. **What exists instead is the first multi-bearer terms-closing, which was suspended in its first hour on the ground that it cannot be performed as written and was not checked, and a review called for the fifteenth.** **Chapters 41–50 have to sell one defined use or admit that they cannot, and the second is now on the table as a real possibility, because the token may never open again and nobody has written the day anybody would find out.**
- **Teo's question, open and unanswered.** *Who tells the four thousand households of this district, and by whom, and for how long, if the crossing never opens?* Adrian said *I do not know* in a school gym in front of ninety people, the board published it as an open question with no term attached, and called a review. **It is the volume's last unclosed promise and it is a Volume 02 question wearing Volume 01's clothes, because a market that has spent a month building a paper and a reader and a levy has a bill coming on a day nobody has written.**
- **The two undecided people at the far receiving ramp.** The fourth entry of the proposal of the twenty-ninth is a mark; the man it stands for was asked one question and said he does not know what the job is; **he has not been told what the job is and has not decided, and the entry stands until he is.** The woman by the kettle has not been in a household since she was nineteen, and she asked that the only thing in Lot 17's book entered because she said nothing be the fact that she said nothing. **Both are unnamed and both must stay unnamed, and one of them is a bearer in a suspended charter.**
- **A mark was given two jobs and a man is in a hospital room.** The service-cut yards household, the line-holder, the foot of the crown of the service cut at four minutes past eleven on the seventh, an ankle that will turn on wet ground for the rest of his life, and no name in any public book. **The remedy for him is a hearing that failed in public and is recorded as not discharged.** The batch's cost, and it is a person.
- **Nessa Pell's index of marks.** She has kept an index of every use of a mark since the fourteenth, because Registrar Quill asked her for one, and it is **the only piece of administration anybody in this district ever asked for.** It is what found the double use in an hour and ten minutes. **It is the most load-bearing document in the volume and it is a clerk's ledger, and nobody has said so publicly.**
- **The two unexplained things, still not joined.** A certified gauge housing drew a line at twenty-three minutes to eight on the morning of the nineteenth on a shut empty span on a trace with no instrument on it, and a bus came out of a Fault with nobody in the driver's seat. **Selik said both out loud, to Ivo Stenn, in a counting room, and Stenn wrote them on two separate lines and did not join them. Selik was still refusing to put them in one sentence at the end of the batch.** Three hundred yards apart, one week, joined in no document. **The next batch must not join them either.** The second water-office fitter who suspended the certificate is annoyed with the slip and not with him, and the two words on the slip were enough before noon.
- **Selik's standing order with no expiry**, in a room on the second floor of the water office with a door on it, signed by a floor and not by a man. **Nobody in Alder Reach has read it and nobody will name it.** Culler Bray told a yard about it once and went home knowing it would cost him.
- **Selik's own review.** He asked for it on his own motion, with nobody's name on it, on the day the ward market votes on the paper levy, and it has not happened. **It is the seed of the Volume 02 argument, and the first batch of Volume 02 has to sit in that room.**
- **The paper levy and the public reader.** Four coppers a quarter for eleven years for paper that does not exist, now funding a bridge inspection and a reader at a counter at about forty-one coppers a quarter. **The reader cannot help a household that cannot get to the counter, and Chapter 32 is the proof of that in one scene, and the woman who asked what the money was for is the standing test of whether the levy survives the winter.** The ward market is a market, not a printing office, and a printing office with bad printing is what it turned out to be.
- **The counterfeit, still a demand problem and now better armed.** Tove Alard printed *what the ninth line actually says, in plain words, and who is abearing it* under every claim, knowing that a city that can read a claim in a minute does not need a fold, and printed it anyway. **Hask Vell is exposed by a rent receipt and is not broken, and he does not fold because he has never folded, and he is still selling honest fast claims to about a third of the people who cannot read.** Halloway Reed did not appear in this batch and was not named in it; his argument is still the best one in the city and is still unanswered. **No arrest resolves this and no batch of Batch 0005 should try.**
- **Rell Bode's third objection, which has half an answer and needs the other half.** A remedy that points at a person is not a remedy for a person who refused a name. A split makes it worse because the loss lands on whoever is nearest the fault boundary and that is geometry. **And a book that records its failures loudly is a book that will be used against the people in it.** Mara Quill conceded the third in a yard of six hundred and gave the only answer there is — the book has to be fat, because a selective book is the one nobody can check — and **that answer is an obligation, not a solution, and the next batch has to start paying it.** The fatness is the standing task: every refused draft, every number anybody set and anybody else checked, every reading and what it cost.
- **The service-cut yards, named for the first time in a yard.** Four streets west of the depot, in two blocks the ward market has never had a rate sheet for, and they are the people nearest the fault boundary, and the reason a split route is worse than a single one. **They are not in any count in any book, they are the reason the remedy of the fourth will keep failing, and they are the first place Volume 02 should go looking for who is actually going to be hurt by a public route.**
- **The split-use remedy is a machine that fails on a schedule.** Called and not discharged on the twelfth, the pool published before and after, the two numbers that differ now both being the count. It fails because the person who would absorb the loss is nobody's neighbour. **Chapters 41–50 have to either repair it or decide that a remedy which fails loudly on a stated day is the thing this district actually wanted, and either answer changes what the Civic Tally is.**
- **The Unlisted boundary, still public and still costing.** Lina Vale's name is on a page; Bett Ruhl's floor, four coppers of flour and a charcoal wall are the price; the second service exists and is built so that **no courier has ever been in a room where a reading happened**; and the four lines in charcoal under the three lines are the only words in Lot 17's public record entered by anybody who is not a registrar. **It must not become a new private monopoly on secrecy and it must not become a conversion, and the category must not become a bucket.**
- **A person is accounted for by a count, not by a household.** Four lines, entered on the second, and the first institutional rule the affected people wrote themselves. It is the volume's constitution and it is not finished: nobody has yet worked out how a *payment* reaches a person who is not in a household, and that is the next volume's central problem and this volume's last one.
- **The nine-line return term is still offered and unattached.** In the book, read by a barrow-hauler and a tea seller, found to have no fold in it, governing nothing. **The next charter that reaches for it is still the test of whether a good term survives contact with a crowd, and Chapters 41–50 are the last chance in this volume for that test.**
- **Joss Amery's three days and his unpaid loss.** Still standing, still unexamined, still the proof that a warning does not stop a person holding a barrow at four in the morning, and now there is a reader at a counter four days a week and a reader who cannot reach him either. **Nobody has paid him and the batch did not touch him, and a reviewer should notice that.**
- **Dessa Rill.** Six tolls, home, the whole of the skill, none of the reason, and in this batch the only person who could say the truth about what a book is for. **She must not be restored and she must not become a mystery to be solved.**
- **Orren Pike and Sela Pike.** The warning is still the front of the book; the stolen method is still public work; he took one sentence from a man who had said nothing all evening and put it in the book, and the argument about whether that was the most useful or the most damaging thing he has ever done runs for two years. **Sela's erasure record is still separated from the evidence in Bett Ruhl's keeping and he has still told nobody he moved it. Unresolved and to be left unresolved in this volume.**
- **The broken circle.** One impression on unrelated documents, a claim about a system rather than a signature, no office, no clerk, no name, the regional heading still scraped away. **Volume 01 must not answer it, and this batch added a mark, an index, and a two-book problem and did not add a single name, which is the correct discipline.**
- **Iven and the blank lot.** Untouched in this batch and to be left untouched. The accusation of Chapter 25 turned out to have a second application — a permanent office proposed by the man who spent nine years refusing exceptions — **and the batch uses it once, about Selik, and does not make it a finding about anybody else.**
- **The Quiet Registry.** Undeveloped, unacted, no named officer, and must stay that way.
- **A yard that cannot agree on a word.** Still unseeded since Chapter 26's failure over *urgent*, *scheduled*, and *priority*. **The ward market has now built a counting house, a reader, a levy and a rate, and the vocabulary of scarcity is still missing, and it is a Volume 02 problem and it is waiting.**

## Volume 01 planned escalations

- Chapters 1–16: **done.** The first public road hardened; the lot book given a proposal schedule, a no-name remedy, and a board that runs its own closing; the counterfeit traced to a plate and to a mark that is a claim about a system; the Unlisted witness channel made administrable; a false-alarm evacuation tightening Selik's span; the fourteen on the far bank brought home.
- Chapters 17–26: **done.** The breach, the private workshop guarantee, the outward success, the missing return, the find, the dead child, the bill, the second emergency exception, the accusation, the publication of the failed charter, and the loss of Adrian's standing.
- Chapters 27–33: **done.** The failure published and paid for at a figure Adrian set; the ward market suspended; the counterfeit organised as a service; **and now, in Batch 0004, the payment closed in public: the inspection funded out of an eleven-year paper levy nobody had audited, a public reader at a market counter, a permanent-route proposal argued in a yard and tabled, and a Warden asking for a review of his own order.** The two services exist. The counterfeit is better armed and unstopped. **The route does not exist and the terms do.**
- Chapters 34–42: **done through Chapter 40.** Affected people were convened and produced a list rather than terms; the eight existing terms were collected and read aloud; the split was drafted and refused and entered over a refusal; the count was found wrong by a household; a remedy that fails was entered and called and not discharged; the terms-closing was built by a board out of its own powers and suspended in its first hour; a man was hurt on a rail; the book was reopened in front of him; and Adrian posted a real, inspectable asset **as neither a payment nor a security**, because a reader cannot post one. **The Batch 34–42 material the outline asked for is complete, and the outline's own summary line — that the posting is the inspection press — was impossible, because the press had been given away in Chapter 24. That impossibility was used rather than hidden: a payment conveys nothing, so the lease was still his, and the posting is a different and larger question.**
- Chapters 43–50: **not done.** The witnessed public climax has to sell **one defined use** of the Last Fare, not the object and not a permanent road, with Tarin and a rotating return crew, a convoy and a shelter group, fresh witnesses who accept the full toll, and a term that expires. **The batch's own new clauses now stand in the way of the easy version**: the release condition must be read aloud at the opening *and* the closing by a different person each time; a successful crossing may not be treated as a transferable right; the far-side holder has still not decided; the fourth bearer is a mark; the count is wrong by a household; and Teo's question is open. **A climax that opens the road and does not deal with all of that would be the Chapter 19 mistake again, and this district will not accept it twice.**

## Mystery schedule

- **Answer by the ending:** why the Tally exists, why anchor lots remember the failed Common Measure, why it recognizes Adrian, what Iven's custodianship concealed, and how the Great Closing is broken without private rule.
- **Reveal in progression:** the Last Fare's shared closing mark; the First House and removed founding signature; the blank lot as the Common Measure's distributed settlement seat; the difference between mechanical recognition and valid consent.
- **Intentionally unresolved:** who first proposed the Common Measure, the identity and purpose of the removed signature, the precise order of the Year 0 contract failures, whether comparable records exist beyond the Morrow Basin, **why the bell-post arm moved, and why the pilot line deviated.**
- **A third is now open and joins the list of things nobody can account for: why a bus came out of a Fault with nobody in the driver's seat after eleven hours and forty minutes.** It is an observed fact, it was introduced once, it has never been used again, and it must stay that way. **None of the three is joined to the others and none may be joined in this volume.**

## Fixed endpoint

Iven remains the final antagonist. His linked founder-mark forced record must be replaced through the Tally's replacement rule: the final term-limited covenant must expressly name the override, state its remedy, and receive acceptance from at least two fresh independent witnesses. Adrian pays a voluntary one-time toll rather than losing security through breach, gives up exceptional reading, and leaves a Civic Tally that works without him. No new System or final enemy may replace this resolution.
