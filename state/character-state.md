# Character State — after Chapter 0050 (Volume 01 close)

## The public lot board

Four seats and no owner. It can refuse to record a closing, publish a result, call a review, uphold a challenge in part, and set or renew the short public custody term. **It cannot grant a use, cannot waive a toll, does not audit a security, and cannot strike a line off a notice board — it can only record what happened.** It acts more than once in this batch and each time it works with the five it has.

**Seats at the end of Volume 01:** **Mara Quill** as registrar, not a vote and not independent; **Nessa Pell** as sitting steward; **Sabin Tull** and **Marda Osk** in the two unclaimed places. All four are named aloud every time the board acts, which is now written into the prose as a reflex rather than a formality.

**What the board did in this batch, and it is a list of everything it can do:** sat its own called review of the suspended terms-closing and **sustained seven items, refused one entry, and refused a sentence about a place**; **upheld a challenge in part** on the far-side condition and let Selik Marne make it bigger; **published a result** — that a damaged object in a public case is a lot with a condition and not evidence; **called a review** and sat it; **set and renewed a custody term twice, in public, on the record**; **took four sets of fresh witnesses** — Rutt and Mrs Halder, the tenth hand and Ferrin Usk, and two at the staging line; **performed a closing with a condition unmet and entered the fact, in four reasons, in its own words**; and **refused to let an answer to a question be entered as a finding, and refused to let it be entered as a confession either**, which is the finest thing the board has ever done and it took about ninety seconds.

## Adrian Vale

- **Age/role:** 38; independent auctioneer at Lot 17, and the author of the first bounded charter this lot has ever closed by himself.
- **Still a Stage 1 Reader. The volume did not promote him and the volume says so in the book.** He can close one charter with one bearer, a short term and a visible toll, and on the eighteenth he did exactly that, in a yard of about nine hundred people, and he said in public what he could and could not do before he started. He cannot split a use, transfer it, renew it, or post security, and the entry of the twelfth still says all of that in the same line as the figure.
- **The reading ledger is closed: five in the month of the crossing, four in this month.** The eighteenth cost him **two hours** — one before the vehicle went and one at the closing — and after the closing the limit is not before the twenty-fifth and the twenty-fifth has passed and he has not read again. **The next opening is on somebody else's word and not on his, and that is the sentence the last reading earned.**
- **What the volume gave him, and it is not a power.** A road that opened once. A limit he held in the rain in front of nine hundred people and was then told by a Warden administrator that he held for the wrong reason. Two hundred and twelve coppers of a stranger's coal he could have moved and did not. A question he asked the brass once, in public, that cost him nineteen minutes and an hour of not being able to tell whether a thing was nearer or further than it was. **A child of six asked him what it was worth and he did not give a number, and the yard went home after the child and not after him, and the tenth hand wrote that down.**
- **His refusal of the steward's key is the volume's last act of character.** Eleven seconds, in a yard, because on the twelfth he put a thousand five hundred and thirty-six coppers into a steward's hands and **if he takes the key back he took it back, and there is no version of taking it back that is not that.**
- **His standing objection is unanswered and he agrees it is fair: a limit checked by one man is a man and not a limit.** He declined to say which of his two reasons for holding it was the true one, and the book records that he declined, and nobody in Alder Reach knows.
- **What he has not done and must not do in Volume 02:** become a Binder by a panel, become the permanent address for frightened people, treat the third of the new month as a date he owns, or ask the brass a second question.

## Mara Quill

- **Age/role:** 34; municipal registrar, author of the public lot book, the proposal schedule, the no-name remedy, the split-use remedy, and now **the first instrument in Lot 17's history that was constructed in a room and not in a back office.**
- **What the volume gave her, and she had to be first to say all of it in public:** that a charter's terms are agreed by people and the anchor is not a party; that there is no procedure for asking an object anything, and that the reason there is not one is that **a record kept of the object is a record nobody in the room is protected from**; that a damaged object in a public case is a souvenir with a lamp on it and not evidence, because evidence requires a stated claim and this district has never stated one; and that **she signed a custody page with the hand that is in the spill and said so before anybody asked, and four hundred people did not look at her hand, and the book records that this is the fifth time and the first time it has cost anybody anything and she cannot work out why.**
- **Her own fear is in the book, in her own hand, at the top of an entry: a registrar who has discovered a hole in her own instrument is the correct state for a registrar to be in, and it goes in the book so that nobody in four years can say she was calm about it.** And she signed a record she **cannot certify in advance**, which is the first in eight years.
- **The four sentences on the kitchen wall are still there, in charcoal, under three lines, and there is now a fifth line in chalk above them** — a reader, at sixteen, wrote it and a cook with a bad hip has not rubbed it out.
- **The delay is on the page.** A person was asked four days after the road went, said yes, and asked for it to be entered that he was asked after and that the delay is the board's and not his. **The book entered the delay and did not apologise in so many words, and that is her decision and not an oversight.**
- **Relationship:** nine chapters further apart in law and closer in practice, and **not one word said about it, in public or otherwise, in either direction.** She holds a book he cannot post into. He asked a question she built the instrument for. They pass a slate in a kitchen at a quarter to seven in the morning.

## Tarin Kest

- **Age/role:** 43; mechanic, route keeper, founder of the Needle Road House. **He drove the fifth activation, out and down, and stopped it on the stamped plate under the lamp, and came home with six tolls and no reason.**
- **The far-side condition is now his instrument and it is bigger than Selik's.** He extended it to everyone on the route who is not driving it, on the ground that a charter with one condition in it and twenty-three people who have none is a charter about one man — **and Selik Marne accepted the extension in a yard and added three terms of his own to it**, which is the first time in the volume that the two of them have agreed on anything, and Tarin named Selik as the person who keeps the terms in the book.
- **The cost of being the bearer, said out loud, at ten past seven in the morning, on a line, to three hundred people rather than nine hundred in a crowd:** every frightened person in this district now has exactly one address, and it is him, and he is going to be asked all day by people he likes to keep the road open, and **the moment he is the man who says no to the road being longer, the road is his and it is a queue with a timetable.**
- **His standing finding at the end of the volume: the timetable he warned the district about has got a committee, and he does not know whether that is better, and he says so in front of six hundred people.** That sentence is the volume's most honest institutional finding and it is his.
- **He drove the bus that came out of a yard on its own, every morning, for a fortnight, before the crossing, and said into a recorder that he did not look for a reason because looking for one is the job of a man with a lamp and a page and not of a man with a shoulder.** Bryn: out of this batch entirely and must stay out.

## Selik Marne

- **Age/role:** Civic Warden and administrator of the official St. Orra service approach. **Shut, right, reviewed, and not thanked.**
- **He asked for the review of his own order on the market's rate day and it sat, and the market wrote the finding, and the finding is that the order is correct and the district does not want it.** He said both halves out loud in nine minutes, said he hoped he was not the one who was wrong about the road and could not find the sentence to say it in, and entered that the road is to be opened by a public lot and that he has no standing to stop it and is not going to interfere.
- **He accepted the extension of his own far-side condition to every person on the route who is not driving it**, and added three terms of his own: a line-holder named in the book, a duty to say the ballast is not ground, and **nobody past the second rail, ever** — which is now the standing safety rule of the Hearth Line as well as a rule on his spur, and the **twelfth of this month is entered in the minutes as the day it was argued with and held** by the tenth hand, in a yard, and did not move. He asked for it to be tested against him.
- **He lost a crossing because he wrote a condition with an end on it and a man in a room with a door on it put a permanent office in a draft, and he says the best argument in this city for a public road is a sentence his own office has been making for nine years and nobody listened to.**
- **And he said the volume's standing objection to the whole arrangement, at twenty to seven in the evening on the eighteenth, to about a hundred and forty people: the limit was checked by one man, a limit checked by one man is a man and not a limit, and Adrian should be replaced in about a year by a room with four seats in it and a notice nailed up in eleven places, and if he cannot let them then everything this district has read for five weeks is a note from a man.** Adrian agreed it was fair.
- **His permanent-office proposal is tabled and not adopted and not dead. The span is shut. The standing order with no expiry has not been read by anybody. He enters in the minutes of the twenty-sixth that he does not accept the rotation of the key and will say so in a public yard on every published day for as long as he holds the post.** He is argued with, never defeated, and must not be defeated in Volume 02 either.

## Orren Pike

- **Age/role:** 17; salvage runner and copy clerk. **Not Adrian's apprentice and not going to be a safe one.**
- **He found the hole in Mara Quill's profession in two nights and eleven lines**, brought it to a yard at half past seven in the morning before anybody else was awake, and said *you have to do the thing my mother could not do, which is write the list of what is not in it.* The instrument was built in public in four hours and the eleventh line is the only one that mattered.
- **He was a fresh witness on the eighteenth**, with the toll read to him, on the ground that he counts and does not travel, and he said that if the count of twenty-four is short of what the people on the ground say it is he will stand at the rail and say so and will not be argued with.
- **At the end, at the rail, overheard by about nine people: she said it was the eleventh line, it took him two nights and she did it in an afternoon, and he would like it noted that he was faster and that nobody has offered him a job.** His sheet of eleven lines went out of the yard in his coat, with his mother's name on the sixth of them, and nobody saw it, and it is not in the book and it is not going in, and he has still not told anybody he moved the erasure record.

## Lina Vale

- **Age/role:** 27; Unlisted memory courier; the steward named in the public book; Adrian's younger sister.
- **She has not been thanked and will not be, and she said beforehand that she would not be, and Bett Ruhl said she would have been insulted.** She has written a fifth line on her own wall in chalk — *the answer is no, and it is said out loud, and no reason is owed* — and the cook has not rubbed it out.
- **She booked the autumn.** *Do it again in the autumn and I will bring somebody else, and that is not a threat, it is a booking*, and Mara entered it as a booking and not as a promise, because a promise in this book has been the expensive word all month.
- **Her two services are still two services and her network is still not enrolled**, and the category has now carried a passage twice, a loss once, a failure of a remedy once, and a refusal to be asked again once. **It must not become a bucket and must not become a conversion**, and she is the person who said so first and is the person who will say it again.

## Fen Ambrose

- **Age/role:** the school shelter's second steward; one of four stewards; keeper of the roll. **The only person in this district who has ever asked a job what it is and made him say it back.**
- **She went down three hours of river stair on the fourteenth to tell a man what the far-side holder is, and came back up with a yes, and the yes cost a woman the rest of her life being asked**, and she said that in front of eleven people, and named the price: **the count became a number that cannot be checked by asking, and it is the only count in this city with no way to be found wrong aloud.**
- **The mark is retired**, filed off a pin on a goods-shed floor in six minutes, and the retirement is in Nessa Pell's index with its date and its two dates and its three documents, and Nessa reads it aloud.
- **Her arithmetic is now in the book**: a crew of three on foot from the far ramp to the near apron does nothing, a party cut off is held and counted and not sent anywhere, and a far-side responsible party is a different job from a holder and is still unnamed. **She is the one who left the volume's next problem at the top of a stair with her name on it.**

## Rell Bode

- Unlisted, washes at the river stairs, has a sick child, and owns the standing objection of Volume 01.
- **Her third objection is now answered as well as it can be and she says the answer is not enough**: a book that keeps a piece of evidence in a drawer is a book with a drawer, and the object is now in a case in public view with a published inspection, a rotation of keys and a reader at a counter. **She is not a convert, and the chapter says so, and the volume ends with her objection still open on the page.**
- **She is the reason the board's third reason in the closing of the eighteenth exists**: a condition is a condition and not a consent, and the person on a sealed page keeps the right to refuse and to challenge and to say so on the record, and none of those rights is exhausted by the road opening.

## Ivo Stenn

- Ward market freight clerk of six years and cooperative secretary of two, the man who has spent six years saying no.
- **He put a rate on a promise and got the district's first yes out of a market that has only ever said no** — forty-one coppers a quarter, in columns, with three unassigned coppers and a reason — **and refused nine coppers more, in public, with figures, on the ground that the thing has no day on it**, and entered that the refusal is not a statement that the yard is not wanted and that the nine comes back to his room on its own when the road has a date.
- **He is the man who found out the ward market can say yes to a room and not to a road, and he has no idea what to do with that.** His district can no longer refuse a rate, which means somebody is going to have to spend it well, and that is the Volume 02 problem and he is the man who made it.

## Nessa Pell

- Lot 17 clerk and sitting steward. **The person who times everything and never tells anybody, and who has been the only key in this lot for five weeks.**
- **She read the mark's retirement aloud, read the toll three times at Bett Ruhl's request, read the release condition at the opening of the case, and read the count of the crossing out loud in a yard of about nine hundred people.**
- **She let the custody term expire and did not notice for twelve hours**, with the object in a case in a building with four hundred people in it, and the lock was hers and the authority was nobody's; she said it in public before anybody could say it for her, and entered that the reason is that she is a clerk and **a clerk's memory is not an instrument, and this district has spent five weeks building a paper with nine copies in it and it has not once built a reminder.**
- **She asked the board for a rotation of the key and the board has no power to create one and the minute says so and the request stands.** Her qualification for the light at the top of the river stairs is that she can see nine feet up a stair and cannot see the turn, and she has entered that as the standing failure of the arrangement and not as good practice.
- **She is not the clerk any more. Hester Vane holds the key from the twenty-sixth for seven days, and the term expires on the third of the new month, and Nessa is one of four seats and is not the person who holds the drawer.**

## Bett Ruhl

- Cook, keeps the kitchen exchange under the Ward Nine tram line, cannot read, and **read the release condition at the closing of the crossing in a yard of about nine hundred people**, saying so before she started, and not pretending to have understood the ninth line, and asking for it a third time. She has asked for a third reading four times now.
- **She has chalked a fifth line on her own wall and has not rubbed it out**, and when a clerk asked her why she said *it was the first thing in a month that had come in that was not needed by somebody.* Her hip and her floor and about nine hours a week are still the standing price of a service nobody can find.

## Rutt

- About fifty. A signal clerk before her shoulder went. **The reader at the ward market counter, four days a week, at nine coppers a day with the lie in it in her own line.**
- **She stopped reading a published document aloud on the sixth of this month and found the fourth bearer.** On the sixteenth she was a fresh witness on the question put to the brass and read out the part she did not understand before she accepted. On the twenty-first **she described the object in a yard instead of reading the entry about it, and weighed it, and put her thumb in the break at the rim, and said it was warm and that she is not going to tell a yard of four hundred people what that means because she does not know.** On the twenty-sixth she read the whole entry of the crossing at the counter and in the yard, and the rule of the counter is now standing: if anybody has told you a different number, bring it and she will read it out again in the words you did not bring.

## The tenth hand of the signal crew (the counter)

- Still unnamed. Still the tenth and not one of the nine, and all nine have been home since the twelfth of last month.
- **He has now driven the crossing.** He is a fresh witness on the charter, and the last thing he asks is *if the count is short of what the people on the ground say it is, I will stand at this rail and say so and I will not be argued with.*
- **His page is now the most important piece of administration in the district and it has a known flaw**: the number twenty-six, with two noes in it, is the first thing in five weeks he cannot put on it, because a number that came out of a refusal is a different instrument from a number somebody said out loud. **He told a yard of about four hundred people at six in the morning that he was telling them so it would be said by the man who found it.**
- **He asked the registrar to keep two facts in two lines and she agreed, and no document in Lot 17's book joins the bus that came out of a yard by itself to anything.** He argued with Selik Marne's rule in a yard on the twelfth and the rule held and the argument is in the minutes.
- He wrote in the margin, at the end of the volume, in his own hand: *they went, all together, after the child, not after him.*

## Teo

- The boy who refused to board on the ninth, who wrote the public complaint, who asked the open question in a school gym on the twenty-sixth of last month, and **who said the word in a yard on the fifteenth of this month, in front of about six hundred people, and the count went down by one and the sale did not stop.**

## Hester Vane

- Twenty-seven. A cooper's wife off the lower terrace. **The first person in this volume to hold the key to Lot 17's public case, and the first holder of the rotation.**
- **She travelled out and came back and paid twice and cannot say why she set out, and she said it in a yard in front of about nine hundred people and did not want anybody to be kind about it.** She came off the bus with a canvas bag with nine pounds of somebody else's seed in it, and she knows the road and does not know the house.
- **Her qualification, which the book gives her in her own words and in about thirty seconds:** she can hold a count, she has not been replaced on a day in her life, she is not a bearer and not a witness and not a beneficiary, **and she wants the key because the last time there was one in this lot it was in the hands of one person for five weeks and on the nineteenth of this month that person's term expired at midnight and nobody noticed for twelve hours.**
- **She asked the one question nobody had an answer to and it is in the book: what am I supposed to do if the term expires and nobody comes.** Nessa Pell's answer and Selik Marne's entry against it are both on the page. **Her first term expires on the third of the new month and that is the first date Volume 02 has to carry.**

## Halloway Reed

- **Sells on a tram arch, in daylight, in front of about nine hundred people, and does not come into the yard.** A claim on the **next** closing, eleven coppers, the toll on page one, the labour term on page three, and the sentence *your public road is going to fail and on the morning it fails the only thing anybody can buy is speed, and I sell speed with the toll printed on the front*.
- **He is technically attractive, morally dangerous, honest, better than he has ever been, and right.** He is not humiliated. Adrian reads the sentence about a claim upon a successful crossing in front of him and admits in public that he has no answer to the other half. **Forty-one places sold in an afternoon.**
- **He is the volume's last standing argument and it is unanswered: on the day a public road fails, a book does not open a door, and the only thing anybody can buy is speed.** Volume 02 has to live with that.

## Iven Tallow

Iven remains presumed dead from Adrian's point of view. **Nothing in this batch touches him and nothing should.** The accusation of the twenty-fifth had a second application and the volume used it once, about Selik, and did not make it a finding about anybody else.
