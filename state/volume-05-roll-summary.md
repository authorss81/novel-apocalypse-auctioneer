## 3. The covenant

**The first regional public covenant in this basin, adopted in public, in a barn about nine miles up the only road in this district that climbs, on the nineteenth of the fifth month at about half past ten, by show of hands, with about seven hands up and two down. Hand-copied by a man of about twenty-three and read out at three of the four counters in this district; the fourth counter is a man of about nineteen who was not well enough to be asked to hold a sheet and was not asked. Entered in the book in the room where the books are and nowhere else, where a stranger may walk up and read it. Its text is reproduced in full in `outline/batches/volume-05-batch-0005.md` section 3 and must reproduce exactly.**

**WHAT IS ON IT.** It says at the top of its own face that it is a covenant and not a constitution. Nine structures stand in the water above four districts; they are nine and not ten; **they are finite; no person and no body is the holder of any of them; and no person shall hold this alone.** Four stewards, changed every season by a show of hands in the places they come from; a steward carries the figures for one season, is not a holder, is not a bearer of the whole, and may be asked a question by anybody at any time inside that season and by nobody at all outside it. The tolls are public and every figure is written where a stranger may walk up and read it, and no toll may be fastened to a party of a place and only to a named person, and a toll that cannot be fastened to a named person is not collected and is not refused, and **the record says not asked.** A term of three seasons beginning on the first day of the next month. **WHAT IS ON ITS FACE AND IS EMPTY. The third of its three columns is headed by a word and not by a name, and it is ruled and empty on the face of the sheet, and it is empty because the people who would go in it have not been asked, and a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way. **THE SINGLE-HOLDER SAFEGUARD IS SHOWN TO HAVE BEEN WRITTEN TO DISTRIBUTE POWER AND NOT TO CROWN ANYBODY (Chapter 246).** A man of fifty-six said out loud that nine people wrote that sentence four hundred years ago to stop a man exactly like the one who has been standing at the back of a book in this district for two months, and that one of the nine sat down afterwards and wrote under it that **a man thanked for it has been made something else** and underlined it twice. **He also said out loud that he was not going to say this sheet is that page, because a sheet in a yard is not a page in a cave and the two have never been in a room together, and that a man who says out loud that a sentence is not his is doing the thing this yard was built to let him do.** The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the finding in a hundred and thirty-four words: *you do not write it down by not having a holder; you write it down by having four of them and a door.*

## 4. The public index, the letter, and the nine walls

**THE PUBLIC INDEX IS A COLUMN IN A BOOK IN ONE ROOM, FOUR HUNDRED YARDS FROM A YARD A STRANGER CAN WALK INTO.** A registrar's own rule, written on the twenty-first of April and not rewritten since, is that a book is kept in one room by one person and that person is the only one who may look in it, and that a person may look at a document there and may not carry it out. **That rule is the reason a notice four hundred miles away could be written at all, because it names what the room is and what it does and prints no person's name, and it is also the reason a covenant can be in a book a stranger may walk up to.**

**THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST IS IN A COLUMN IN THAT BOOK AND IS CALLED THE MAN WHO CONVENES (Chapter 248). He is a Convener. The promotion is not a screen. He said the price out loud before he took it, in a hundred and forty-nine words, in a barn, in front of about nineteen people:**

> a name in a column in a book a stranger may walk up to, which is a worse door than a page; and a name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on, which is this yard's own rule and which he wrote into this district's practice in April; and he was not going to try to take it off himself, because if he tried he would be the first man in this basin to be given a thing by his own good sense and to want it back a week later.

**He did not ask anybody to take it off.** At ten past seven that evening a stranger stood at the gate of that yard, four hundred yards away, having come four miles **on the strength of the name and not on the strength of a page**, and asked whether the man who convenes was there, and was told he was standing about nine feet from the gate with a bucket in his hand, and he answered from there without being fetched. The stranger said he had come for a sheet of paper with a name on it and not for anything else, and was going to take it back down the road and put it in front of two other people and ask them whether they would pay a toll. **Nobody in that yard was asked whether they wanted to be put in front of two other people, and the record about that says not asked.** A man of fifty-six said that four strangers in four months is not a market, and that a yard that starts taking a name and handing back a page is about four weeks away from being a counter of somebody else's, and that he was not going to help it get built out of a name. **Nobody agreed with him and the record about his objection says not asked.**

**HE IS NOT A FOUNDER, IS NOT A HOLDER, IS NOT A BEARER OF A TOLL, IS NOT A PARTY OF THE FOURTH OF FOUR, AND IS NOT ON THE STANDING ORDERS OF THE NINE.** The registrar of the records office **signed one line out of six** of the covenant and told a barn of about nineteen people which one and why: the sixth, the one about where the figures are written and who may read them, and she signed it as the person who keeps the book and not as a bearer and not as a holder and not as a steward. **She is not a bearer, not a holder, not a steward and not a party of anything, and she said so out loud before she signed anything. **THE LETTER CAME INTO THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN ON THE TWENTY-ONE OF THE FIFTH MONTH, FOLDED ONCE, PUT THERE BY NOBODY, READ OUT AND READ BACK (Chapter 249).** It is the first document in this basin in four months that carries a person's name on it, and it carries one name twice and no place. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing refused to deny it, in a hundred and twenty-eight words, on the ground that a denial said in a yard a stranger can walk into can be asked about at a gate four miles away, and a clerk entered that **a man who refuses to deny a word does not thereby claim it**, and that a man who has not said a word has not said it, and that the record about the word says not asked. ## 5. The nine locks

**They are called gates on a stranger's sheet and locks in the word the people who keep them use, and the two words are not the same word. A lock does not open a road. A lock holds a road shut, and the water it holds decides which roads are passable, and when a lock stands open every road below it opens with it, all at once, whatever anybody meant. Nine of them stand in the water above four districts. They are finite. They are nine and not ten. No person and no body is the holder of any of them, and no person shall hold this alone, and none of the nine is on any of the nine sheets.**

**WHAT WAS FOUND ON THE FIFTEENTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH (Chapter 243).** About nine people walked nine miles up the only road in this district that climbs and about two hundred yards of shoulder past the top of it, and stood in the cold on a piece of ground the size of a room with a drop on two sides. The frame at the top is cast iron about four feet high, bolted to two pieces of oak let into the bank, the oak older and darker than the oak at the loams. The screw comes up through the middle of the frame to a handle about a foot and a half long and the pin goes through the screw. The sill at the bottom is a stone about two feet wide with one notch cut in it. **The board was in the notch. It had been put in by somebody. It was wet to about a hand's breadth above the water and the water was standing above the board about a foot deep and no more, and the road below that frame was under about a foot of water where the cart track comes out.**

**SO THIS DISTRICT CAN NOW SAY FOUR THINGS, IN THE WORDS OF THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM, IN A HUNDRED AND FIFTY-THREE WORDS: *we can say the board is in. We can say the water is about a foot over the board and the road below is shut. And we can say that nobody in this basin is holding this, because there is nothing on it to hold. What we cannot say is whether the ninth of the nine nights happened on the twentieth of last month, and we cannot say it because a plank does not keep nights, and there is not one mark on this frame and no book and no man, and it is not anybody's job to remember it, and that is the first thing this district has ever found out by walking to a thing instead of saying a number out loud.***

**THE MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-ONE WHO KEEPS A ROAD HOUSE AT THE TOP OF THAT ROAD, IN A HUNDRED AND FIFTY-SIX WORDS: *I did not put it in and my father did not put it in and neither of us has ever put one in. There is somebody who comes in March and goes down again before the water is down and I do not know what he is called and I have not asked, and I am not going to ask now that you have all been standing on my shoulder, because the day I ask him is the day there is a man at the top of this road instead of a house, and this house has never had a man in it. He comes. He puts the board in. He goes. And in this basin that is the whole of what has ever happened on this water and nobody has ever been able to write it down, and the reason nobody has been able to write it down is that there is nobody to write.***

**NO KEEPER HAS EVER BEEN ON A PAGE AND THERE NEVER WAS ONE. NO KEEPER IS NAMED ANYWHERE IN THE FIFTY CHAPTERS.** Thirty-two occurrences of the word *keeper*, case-blind, in the volume and every one of them is a denial, a column heading on the stranger's schedule, or the March man described by what a man of about forty-one does not know about him. Four generations of people have kept the nine and not one of them is on a page and not one of them has ever been asked. **A lock is the only instrument in this basin that has never needed a person, and a person standing next to a lock is not holding it.**

**A man of about thirty-four had said in a yard on the tenth of the fifth month that he could have walked to one of the nine in a morning and did not. A clerk entered that a thing a man could have done and did not do is not a refusal and is not a consent and is not a count. On the fifteenth he walked.**

**The volume's last line is this argument in a body. On the twenty-second of the fifth month a man of about thirty-four stood up to about the knee in a channel at the sluice end of the loams for about four minutes with his left arm hanging at his side and did not touch the frame, and the water had come back up over the ten days to about a foot above the sill, and the frame was holding it the way it holds it, which is by being a frame with the weight of the water sitting on it, and there was no hand on any part of it. ## 7. The counting motif, the figures, and the claim that they reproduce

**THE CLAIM.** A number word attached by a claim phrase to a printed sentence standing in a paragraph that begins with a quotation mark resolves forward to that paragraph. The sentence is counted with words separated by spaces, a hyphenated numeral taken as one word, and the trailing full stop not counted.
