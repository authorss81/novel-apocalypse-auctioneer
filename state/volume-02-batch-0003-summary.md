# Volume 02 Batch 0003 Summary — The Borrowed Road (Chapters 71–80)

## Batch outcome

**The volume turned from the cost to the instrument, and the instrument turned out to be a method and not a paper.** The batch's spine is a question a chandler asked in a yard on the sixth of April — *how do you ask a person whether they will do a job* — and the nine minutes it took a yard of about sixty people to answer it, and the five things on that slate, and the sixth thing that has no name and belongs to a clerk with a pen. The method is then used three more times in the same batch, on the reader, on a signalman in a hut, and on the two women who hold the west end of a railway, and **on the twenty-ninth of April it is used on a boundary** and produces a yes from both holders and a refusal from the woman who was asked to carry the west end of the whole line.

Three of the four beats the prompt assigned are new institutions. **A Guild inspector appears for the first time in Volume 02** — Garnet Sill, forty-one, twelve years licensed, about two hundred and forty lots — and he is right about the object, wrong about the village, not a villain, not stupid, and cheaper to beat than to argue with, and the batch's canonical sentence is his: **the lot is clean and cannot be sold to a village.** He gave the fifth instrument's shape in about ninety seconds without knowing he was doing it, and his form has twenty-nine columns and not one of them is a place.

**Selik Marne's bridge administration staffs the hut at the ninth place, at a place it says is unsafe, and says so on every published day, and the district finds out that the safest and the most hated are the same office and does not take the man out.** The man is Corvin Sallow, thirty-nine, a signalman, and he is in that hut because of a four-link chain that nobody designed: a bridge clerk's book on the seventh, the office's second quarterly copy on the nineteenth, **an empty room over the counting office with a bed in it and nobody in it for two years**, and a lamp-man's pocket book in a sleeve in a yard. He asked to be sent. Nobody sent him.

**The counterfeit's Monday print lands and the district answers it with two lines of type and a date and an hour under a column that is still empty.** Halloway Reed put four coppers in the market's empty column, printed the market's two lines underneath his own price, gave the beating away for nothing, came into a public yard and sold nothing, and is not to be thanked in a street. He is still right about four things and wrong about one, and the one is printed on his own sheet in his own type.

**The Tongue travels between Faults and the west end becomes a sequence.** A lot that was in a hut eleven miles west is now in a hut four, carried eleven yards in the rain by a boy of nineteen who is not on a roll. The keeper cannot hold a pair she has to take to another village; on the twenty-ninth of April Sef Anyard asks for the word to be held at the fourth place as well and is refused, because **the line does not know it has stopped working, because a line is not a person and there is nobody standing at the end of it to find out.** There is no ground signal at the Harrow end, the signal going out of the same frame in the fourth year, so the west end cannot report itself at all. A signalman is offered the fix and refuses it: a place that reports every morning is a place nobody goes to.

**Tarin and Adrian's people protect the engine at the Harrow end in weather, without knowing who owns the signal's toll, and the answer is in a lease in a book in a room in Alder Reach.** The engine is a stationary pumping engine on a brick plinth in a brick house eleven feet above the flood, and it has kept the Harrow interchange at nine feet for nine years and would have it at fourteen in a month. The oil has cost **fourteen thousand seven hundred and forty-two coppers** over nine years, out of a Road House float, on nine years of receipts in a tradesman's hand folded under a tarpaulin on a shelf. The lease has been surrendered. The answer, in Junia Crale's hand in a room with a board over the window: **the person who held the paper is not the person the paper names, the paper named a road house, and the road house is not there any more.** The whole cost of the west end of that railway for nine years is **twenty-one thousand two hundred and twenty-two coppers in two books in two rooms in two hands**, and it is now in one figure for the first time because a man of seventeen asked a question in the wrong order. **The toll is not resolved and nobody says what it lands on.**

## The date map, and the month turn

**The month turns between Chapter 79 and Chapter 80. The new month is May. Last month is April; the month before last is March.** Chapters 71 to 79 are April. Chapter 80 is the first, second and third of May. **Any figure that depends on a date must be derived from it and never carried forward.**

**No weekday is attached to any date in this batch.** The four weekday habits that survive from Batch 0002 are the only ones: **Halloway Reed's Monday**, which is his printed promise and lands on the fifteenth of April; **a haulier's Tuesday**, described by the woman at the column as a habit and not a date, and Chapter 72 says *twice a week* rather than naming a day; **a lamp-man who would not start on a Saturday** is not restated; and a hypothetical bearer's Tuesday is not restated. Chapter 73's valuer is remembered by a woman as *you were here Tuesday*, which is a woman's memory of a man and not a date. **Do not build a week out of those.**

**Two dates in the batch are now pinned, and both were ambiguous when it was written.** **The last time Sef Anyard had the Harrow Tongue in her hand is the eighteenth of March**, when she said *that'll do till the end of the month* and put it back, and it stayed in the frame until the morning of the sixth of April. **The departure Adrian's second reading took is the seventeenth of February**, at six in the morning, to look at a pumping engine, and his own book's entry says the west gate. **The seventeenth of February of this year — seventeen years after the Long Fracture and before the flood year — and no batch may move it into the current month, because a departure inside the month he has been in Alder Reach would leave the loss of a way out with nothing to stand on. What the chapter rests on is that the day is written in a book he still has and the gate in it is not there.**

**Three dates in the batch are deliberately not pinned, and this is a record of an absence.** Chapter 71 says *the sixth of the month before last* twice and *the twenty-second of the month before last* once. **The arithmetic from the sixth of April gives February; Volume 02 opens on the first of March and has no February scene in it; and nothing in the canon, the outline or the key rotation is anchored to either date.** They were examined in review and left as they are, and **no batch may attach an event to them, date anything by them, or read them as March.**

## Beats completed

- **Chapter 71 — Nine Minutes (the sixth).** Opens on a key put on a crate. A chandler of about fifty-six, thirty-one years keeping a second counter, comes a day early to ask the question nobody had thought to ask. **The method is worked out in nine minutes in a yard of about sixty people and it is not Adrian's and not the tenth hand's and not the board's.** A job is asked in five things: what it is in sentences a person who has never done it can understand and not in the word the office would use; how long, with an hour on it; how you say no, and that no reason is owed and nobody will ask for one afterwards; who answers for you while you are saying it; and where you stand when you answer. **Five noes inside four minutes, six hundred people, and a sixth yes from a woman of about fifty-two with a stall who says the reason she will take it is that she has four days and nobody to leave it with and that is the reason and not that she is brave.** The new term begins at midnight the same night and runs four days, and there is no hour in which nobody holds the key and no hour in which two people hold it. **Twenty-two names on the sheet: nineteen noes, read out at the counter the next morning by a reader in the ordinary voice with the word NO in front of each one, and three people who said they would ask somebody at home, and asking somebody at home is not a no.** **Nessa Pell supplies the sixth thing, which has no name, is not a power, and has been hers for nineteen years: a person in the room who is not the one being asked and is not the one asking, whose job is to say what the question was** — and she says no to a thing nobody asked her for, and the finding printed under the wall is that **the board cannot ask a person whether they will do a job, the yard can, and the difference between those two things is the whole of what has gone wrong in this district since the eighth of March, and nobody has said it out loud, including the tenth hand, who has said the other half of it fourteen times.** At about ten past six that evening **a boy of fifteen walks nine miles with a drawing of a signal frame, because the keeper at the ninth place has found that the Harrow Tongue is not in it**; she looked for eleven hours, told nobody, and sends a boy and a drawing. **The thirteenth part of the results is nine words: the lot is not in the custody, and nobody in this district has the power to move a lot or the power to find one.** The last line of the entry is that somebody has to walk to the fourth place, four hours on foot, nine hours there and back in that weather.
- **Chapter 72 — A Hut Four (the seventh, eighth, tenth and eleventh).** Opens on a boy of nineteen woken on a bench with four pounds of cast iron beside his head. **Nevin Roke took the Harrow Tongue eleven yards off the ballast at the ninth place on the evening of the sixth, in the rain, because he thought it was a bar, and he is not on a roll in any book in Alder Reach, and nobody in this district knew he was there until the eighth of this month.** The party walks up at half past four in the morning, takes five and a quarter hours with all four changeovers, and stays two nights. **Rutt's rule goes into the entry: found on the seventh, taken on the sixth by a person who is not on a roll, and the person is not struck, is not asked to be a bearer, is not a witness and is not entered as anything.** Petrus Nye, a bridge clerk of thirty-four, writes the finding in the water office's own book and says the office has no use for the information and is writing it down because the book is copied twice a quarter and goes where the boy is not, **and adds a fourth thing: there is an empty room over the counting office on the second floor with a bed in it and a window that shuts and nobody in it for two years, and he has the keys.** The line's loss is worked out on the six-foot in about four minutes: **the ninth place is the only frame on the line with a bracket the iron fits, the bracket is empty, and therefore the only place on the line that can turn a train is a place that does not have the thing in it; the train cannot go east, cannot go west, and has stood fifty-five days.** **The boundary's word is a word about a train and there is no train, and the word has been said five times in ten days and every time it stopped a man with a barrow**, and the published entry is in the woman's own words: **a word written about a train is now a word about people, a word does not need its authors to agree with it, and this is the first entry that says so.** The fourteenth part of the results, published on the eleventh, says the west end is not a place but an order, that a district that can only count things will find that out in about a week, and that **the finding will not be a figure.** Nye reads it out at the rail at the fourth place and asks to go back.
- **Chapter 73 — Clean (the twelfth, thirteenth, fourteenth and seventeenth).** Opens on a guild inspector's case on a crate. **Garnet Sill, forty-one, twelve years licensed, about two hundred and forty lots certified, nine of them wrong, and eight of the nine were something somebody had added.** He came twelve miles on his own fee, off his own book, on two days' notice, and nobody in Alder Reach asked him, and he announces that he is the most expensive thing in that yard this month and earned it — **eleven coppers, examined in the person of the keeper, which is the highest figure on the guild's sheet.** The certificate says **CLEAN** and it is clean in the exact guild sense, and he says so first and at length. **And then the second sentence, in about ninety seconds: it cannot be sold to a village** — because a sale is to a person and a village is not a person, and because nineteen takings of one object have charged the origin, which is a place, and **no instrument in this basin can name what is being sold, because the seller cannot say what it is.** **Sef Anyard is asked whether she would know if the lot were gone and whether anybody would, and says no twice, and asks him to write the hole in the custody on the back of the certificate, and gives the reason she never wrote it herself: there was not one person in nine miles who could have asked her about it and not made her into something.** The woman at the column asks whether he will write down whether her sister is in there, and he says no, and gives the reason, and she calls it the first time a man has told her no in nine years and given her the reason he could not. On the back of the certificate, at his own cost and out of an hour he was not paid for, he writes that his form has twenty-nine columns and not one of them is a place, and that **a clean lot and an unfilled standing line are both true, and my form has no line for a custody, and a form with no line for a custody is a form that cannot be refused, and a certificate that cannot be refused is not a certificate, it is a receipt.** Ivo Stenn finds the River and Carriage uses a different fee sheet, refunds four coppers on the eleven and cannot give back the nine, and the minute enters the first figure anybody in Alder Reach has ever entered against a Guild. Sill explains himself at the counter on the seventeenth in four minutes: **a man from the Guild goes into a village to look at a thing, the place asks him a question, the place is the only party in the room with nothing to sell, and in every one of two hundred and forty lots the thing that was wrong was in a column, and not one column in this basin is a place.** Selik says **that is not enough** from the rail, and then says the certificate is the only good news anybody has had about that railway in four weeks and that the man is the first in nine years to put a document on a wall that says where the toll lands, and that he did it by accident.

- **Chapter 75 — Nine Feet and Not Fourteen (the eighteenth).** Opens on a man of sixty-eight coming up out of a wet chamber with water to his chest and a bucket. **There is a pumping engine at the the ninth of April: the vehicle overflow has stood in the rain against a wall for nineteen days and he has priced it in two figures, one of them four hundred and ten coppers and the other one nothing. **His figure is in the public book with his name on it, entered at his own request, because a method that is not written down is a man being angry and a method that is written down is a rate that has whether he is going to read the engine and says no, and the refusal is entered with the hour: **he is in the wrong room, and a man who has kept an hour for nineteen days will spend it here on a - **Chapter 76 — The Name on the Lease (the twentieth and the twenty-second).** Opens on a lease clerk turning a book round on a table and taking her hand off it. **Junia Crale has been in that room twenty-two years, has read out four thousand and something leases, and has never once been asked who holds the paper.** The hook has eleven leases on it: **nine name a firm, two name people, and both of the two that name people have been renewed.** The lease from the ninth year names the siding, the water column, **a shed sixteen feet by thirty on the north side**, and the ground between, and the lessee is **THE NEEDLE ROAD HOUSE, A FIRM**, and the signatory is Tarin Kest. **The answer, in her hand, is the batch's answer: the person who held the paper is not the person the paper names, the paper named a road house, and the road house is not there any more** — and she says the reason it took till the twenty-second to find out is that on the twentieth nobody in Alder Reach had thought to go and look. The shed was taken down in the flood year and is on the ninth-year survey in ink with a water butt added in a different ink, and four feet off its east wall there is a second mark on the survey: **a line of stones.** Tarin lays it out himself: his father put them down on the morning of the day he signed, across the overflow, nine to a foot, and that is the whole of the work. **The woman at the column has drawn two pails off that overflow for eleven years and the water arrives where the stones send it, and nobody in that room is going to tell her.** Orren Pike, unasked, stops him from going up the bank to say so: **because if you ask her, the true thing comes back to the wrong man, and in four years the story will be about you finding out.** Put it on a sheet, ask it in a room, with somebody in the room whose job is to say what the question was, and ask her twice. Junia Crale's finding on the rate sheet's held-by-nobody line: **held by nobody is the most accurate four words I have read out in twenty-two years.** On the twenty-second a quarterly return comes up a bank in a satchel: **the Harrow works maintenance has been one hundred and eighty coppers a quarter for nine years, paid, not arrears, not deferred, and the market has read that return every quarter for nine years, and the oil was never in it because the office did not know the engine was running, because the works sheet was written off a list of what was standing.** Ivo Stenn does the arithmetic nobody asked for: **six thousand four hundred and eighty in nine years, paid, against fourteen thousand seven hundred and forty-two in oil; twenty-one thousand two hundred and twenty-two in total, in two books in two rooms in two hands, and it is the first time the two have been in the same place** — and under it nine words: **the market refused eighty-eight coppers a quarter for eight posts. A river office paid six thousand four hundred and eighty. Neither asked the other if the thing existed.** The last thing on the twenty-second is a printed half-sheet: **a signalman will be in the hut at the ninth place from the twenty-fifth, for one month, renewed by the office, nobody asked, and the administration considers that place unsafe.**
- **Chapter 77 — The Safest and the Most Hated (the twenty-fifth and the twenty-sixth).** Opens on a signalman of thirty-nine putting a folding rule under the left-hand end of a bench and saying the floor is eleven inches out of level and that he is going to sleep on it. **Corvin Sallow is in that hut because of a four-link chain nobody designed** — Petrus Nye's book on the seventh, the office's second quarterly copy on the nineteenth, an empty room over the counting office with a bed in it and nobody in it for two years, and a lamp-man's pocket book in a sleeve in a yard — **and he asked to be sent, and nobody sent him, and the piece of paper with his name on it says so.** About ninety people go up the bank to be angry and stay to look and go back still angry, and all three are in the book. **A woman of forty-nine who voted for the office in March says the whole of it at a doorway: the most hated office in this district has put a man in the one hut on this line where the ground stops being good ground, and nobody in this district would have, and the district spent a month refusing to pay for exactly him.** Sallow says four things, including that he will find out what the boundary is when somebody asks him and not before, and that he is entered as not asked, because **a man who is sent to a place is not a man who is owed that place.** Then Orren Pike sees in four minutes that a signalman with a place, an approach and a stated week is three of the four things the eighth place's slot needs, **and Sallow refuses before the sentence is finished: if the standing of that lot is the standing of an office with a room and a bed in it, then there is nobody standing there at all, there is an office, and he has never known a public thing that survived an office finding out about it.** The fourth shape on the tenth hand's page: **a bearer is a person. An office is not a person. A slot is not a person. A line of stones is not a person.** In the last hour of light two women teach the newcomer the two things he has not got, and both attach something that is not a condition and has no end on it: **Sef Anyard will not hold a second word alone, and the woman of the column will not have an officer say her word — he must say it in front of her once and she will decide whether he said it like a person or like an office, and there is no third time, and she will not tell the tenth hand and will not write it down.** On the twenty-sixth the district finds out, in a count read out at a counter by a reader who does not read figures, **that the safest office in this basin is the one nobody would vote for, that it has been paying for a pump on a dead line since the flood year out of a river, and that it has sent a better railwayman than the district has to a place nobody would name** — and the only reason the district knows any of it is a seat on a satchel in another office's book. **Selik says that is not enough twice and then says the rest of it: a man he did not choose out of a room with a door on it asked to be sent, and the same sentence with one word changed is the reason his man cannot be a bearer, and he noticed the resemblance on a step on his way home.** On the second morning Sallow walks the section because he was told to and measures the twelfth stall at **ninety-four yards**, which is neither the keeper's figure nor the tenth hand's, and **neither number is struck.**
- **Chapter 78 — The Same Word (the twenty-sixth, twenty-seventh and twenty-eighth).** Opens on a reader reading eleven lines at the rail out of the page a man wrote about himself. **The asking was done in the yard in nine minutes on the twenty-sixth by the tenth hand, using the five things on the wall, on the reader himself for the first time in seven weeks**, and the sixth thing was done by Rutt, who stated the question so that the entry in a fortnight would be the question and not a memory of one. **Adrian Vale names the person who answers for him before anybody reaches the fourth thing: Bett Ruhl, who cannot read, and the finding is that the district has spent nine weeks treating literacy as a condition of being asked and the reader has just appointed somebody who is not eligible.** He asks Sef Anyard on the shoulder whether she wishes to speak before he starts, as she asked for on the eleventh of March, and she has one thing ready: **the iron is not in my hut and you are going to read it on a bench four hours from the place the lot is in, and I want the boy's name on your sheet before the iron's.** **The one System panel of the batch** is the lot, spoken before the hour rather than after: a lot may be read twice; a reading is an hour and a cost and the cost is a part of the toll and it is the same toll every time; **a certificate that says a lot is clean is not a reading and cannot be wrong about the toll, because it has no column for the toll**; a holder who is not at the custody is a holder who is not there, and a custody may be ended by the person who holds it and not by a person sent to find out whether it is ended; you may not read a document nobody has handled, you may not read a person, you may not ask a lot where the toll lands, **and if you ask it for more you will be given what you have already been given**; and none of this is an answer. **The reading is done on the bench in the hut at the fourth place for about an hour and it is the form of the eleventh of March word for word: TOLL: THE ORIGIN, THE OBJECT DOES NOT HOLD PLACES, and it says ELEVEN USES in March and ELEVEN USES in April.** The cost is entered in his own hand: **he cannot remember which gate he went out of, and there is no west gate, and in March it took a direction off him and today it took a way out of the city, and a departure is what this thing charges.** The finding: **a toll on a place takes memory of departure; he has paid it twice and got a piece of it each time, and the second piece is smaller.** The intervals have gone up on use — seven days, then eleven, then fourteen, and the next permitted hour is not before the eleventh of next month — and Stenn enters that this is not a rule getting stricter and will not defend it, and Adrian says he does not know what it is and is not going to help anybody name it, and asks for it to be written down that he noticed. On the twenty-eighth **Nessa Pell enters a boy's four questions under a heading nobody in this district has ever printed, NOT ANSWERED** — who tells us, by whom, for how long, is anybody obliged to answer — and gives, at last, her own reason for nineteen years of refusing to put a question in that book: **a question is a thing somebody is owed, and if I put one in then I am the clerk who put it in, and a clerk cannot be owed anything, and I would rather it was on a wall where it is nobody's.** Rutt reads it all out and finds that a refusal is a thing somebody did and an answer is a thing somebody gave, and that **giving does not have a shape.** Teo says the method is on the same wall and asks for the fourth question to be printed at the size of the other three. And **Nevin Roke asks for his name on the sheet before the iron's and says he is nobody's keeper, does not know what the iron is, will not carry it anywhere again, and is telling them the four changeovers in advance** — and the finding is that a custody whose keeper is entered as nobody is not a custody, it is a place where a thing is.
