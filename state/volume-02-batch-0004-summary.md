# Volume 02 Batch 0004 Summary — The Borrowed Road (Chapters 81–90)

## Batch outcome

**Other people build the instrument Adrian cannot close, the district votes on the office and the timetable, and the first thing anybody writes on a toll board is a name nobody can find in a register.** The batch's spine is a reader's discovery at a ward market counter on the second of this month — that a sheet can be taken cold by anybody in the basin and about four hundred people took one cold, and that **a board cannot be taken cold, because a board is in a place, and a place is the only thing in this district that has ever been able to make anybody stand still** — and the three toll boards that follow are that sentence made of wood.

All four of the volume's Chapters 81–90 items happen, in the order the outline sets them, and none was spent early.

**The multi-bearer charter is built by other people.** On the tenth of this month, in a room over a chandler's shop paid for out of the two hundred coppers the ninth place paid, five people struck two of the four places in Adrian Vale's own March draft, invented a fourth bearer whose only standing is the ending, and made him say in four seconds that he cannot close it. **Saying so is the whole of his part in that room**, and the entry says on its face that this is the first document in the district's history where it is true of him on the page. He wrote down what the room decided and nothing else.

**Three toll boards went up at three places on the line with a keeper at each** — the east end, the ninth place, the fourth place — and **the first thing written on the first one was a name**, in letters three inches high, because a name at the top is a heading and a name in the middle is an amount and this district cannot print an amount. Four people in four rooms then spent two hours trying to find that office in a register and could not, and the finding is that a toll nobody can name is still a toll and a board that says so in a street is worth more than a book that says it in a room.

**The second impression of the broken circle is found on a hut board at the ninth place**, on a water office notice that the water office has no copy of, by a boy of seventeen who was there to copy a line book, on the fourteenth of this month, and the district spent two days finding out what it was and finding that it was not a thing anybody could use. **The two lines are entered in two lines and are not joined**, and the registrar's reason is the reason she gives every time, and a boy of seventeen asked a second time in a yard and was refused in four seconds.

**The standing order was answered again in public, in a market, and the count each trade carried was read out for the first time**, paying the debt the twenty-fifth of March entered. **The office lost five to five on the market's own rule, which is by trade and not by household, and the timetable carried six to four, and the finding is that the district could not agree about an office and agreed exactly and inversely about a sheet, five to five both times, the same five on opposite sides, and the difference between the two questions is that one of them can be ended and the other one cannot.**

It is **not the fifth thing** and the board says so. It came from a reader's account of a fifth stranger who stood at a counter for a minute and a half reading the market's empty column, and **the instrument came out of the only job in this district which is to be handed a thing by a stranger and to say out loud what is written on it.** The three places are the east end, the fourth and the ninth; the sixth is a drain and the eighth has nowhere to stand and both reasons stand. **The three keepers are asked in the wall's five things, used and not re-derived: a signalman of a bridge administration who is not a bearer and gives the reason — KEEPING A BOARD IS NOT HOLDING A PAIR — and a clerk who is not a signal hand and gives three conditions and a disclaimer.** The room then fails to find the third keeper in eleven minutes and the failure is entered as a failure. - **Chapter 82 — The Name at the Top (the sixth to the ninth).** Opens on a sign-writer of sixty-one beginning at the top left, with a boy of nine not asked to hold the twine. **The first thing written on a toll board in this district is THE STANDING OFFICE OF CROSS-DISTRICT PASSAGE, ESTABLISHED, AND NOT AT THIS PLACE, in full, at the top, and it takes nine minutes.** The reason it goes at the top is the batch's governing sentence: **a name at the top is a heading and a name in the middle is an amount, and this district cannot print an amount.** The board carries eleven lines and four of them are about a place nobody can find; the column is four feet wide and empty with the reason printed inside it at the height of a man's eye; and the room tries three times to put the ninth place in the middle of it and refuses three times, on the ground that **a reader is the only instrument in this district that takes the whole of a thing away from him before he writes it down and a board keeps it.** A stranger asks the one question nobody had asked — *if I want to complain about that, who do I complain to* — and the entry is nine words: **he can complain to nobody, that is the toll.** Four people in four rooms then spend two hours failing to find the office: a registrar through the municipal roll, a bridge clerk through his own office's standing instructions, a licensed valuer through the guild's register, and a sign-writer through thirty-one years of lettering jobs in which he finds the phrase twice in his own hand and no office. **Two hundred coppers is collected on the seventh from four households on a sheet nobody there was asked about, and the entry says a rate that is not asked for is a rate that has not been agreed to.** The third board goes up at the fourth place on the ninth, kept by a paperer of thirty-eight who says she does not know what a pair is and wants that on the board.
- **Chapter 83 — The Man Who Cannot Close It (the tenth).** Opens on a man carrying a table up four flights of stairs into a room bought for six coppers out of the ninth place's rate. - **The prompt says Selik is not on the twenty-ninth of May's walk and is not to be put on it.** **He is not on it and is not in the room. He is at a rail in Alder Reach on the twenty-fifth, on a published day, and he says *that is not enough* and two other things, and the tenth hand answers him in public with the best argument anybody has made against him in nine years, which is that the district's one working instrument against a man in an office is a boy of seventeen who happens to be in the room.** Selik reads the entry and says that was correct. He is not thanked, he is not defeated, and he asks, in writing and in public, for the one thing in this district that is cheaper for an office than for a woman in a doorway, and says he knows what he is asking for.
- **The prompt says the woman of forty-five is not a name in the book and will say the word once more if it is needed and nothing else.** **She is asked a second time in May, in the same room, and says no a second time, and is not named, and says nothing except four sentences reported by the tenth hand.** She does not say the nine-word word. The batch's finding is that a no which is asked twice is not a refusal that expires but a column.
- **The prompt says the protected category's count is closed at five and the four conditions with no end on it are a different class of thing.** **Nothing in this batch touches the protected category, and no new condition with no end on it was added.** The woman of the column's standing request about the rate sheet is a request to a market and is entered under a heading that says NOT ANSWERED, which is a heading a book can carry and not a condition. The four conditions of March and April stand as they stood.
- **The prompt says a mark may not carry a job and the rule is not to be retired.** The rule is used twice and retired nowhere: a fourth place on a board is a place somebody will be asked to stand at in a hurry, and a keeper is not an answerable person.
- **The prompt says the fifth instrument is still unnamed and unowned and the three toll boards are its test.** **It is still unnamed. The boards say on their faces that they are not it**, in the same words the boundary's entry used, and the finding under all three is that a toll nobody can name is still a toll and a board that says so in a street is worth more than a book that says it in a room.
- **The prompt says the batch must be able to say what is different the second time the boundary is asked.** **Three things, and all three are in the book: the asker is a different person, so the rotation turned; a woman who said no in March was asked again and said no again, so a no is a column; and the renewal carries a renewed refusal with a date on it, so the refusal turned too.** The asker is a clerk and the entering is by a bridge clerk in a book that is copied twice a quarter, and the delay is entered by the registrar for the fifth time.
- **The prompt does not ask for it, and the batch adds two figures that are now in the book: a rehearsal's hours and a public office's ninety seconds.** A rehearsal is a walk done before the day it is for; the first in this district was the twenty-second of March and the second was the twenty-first of May, and the second produced the only corrected walk figure the district has.
- **The prompt does not ask for it, and the batch resolves one small thing: the bushel of white paint.** The standing failure of the twenty-ninth of March is entered as fixed on the eighteenth of May, at four coppers, and the finding is that somebody asked a chandler, which is the fourth time this district has had that finding.

## What the next batch inherits

- **The twenty-ninth of June, and nobody has been given the job of going up that bank, and the clerk who went in May has said in a room that it will not be her.** The boundary runs from the twenty-ninth of May to the twenty-ninth of June, renewable only by asking both holders again in a room with the asking, the hour, the answers and the reading-back entered. **A clerk can be replaced, and that is the point of her, and the next batch has to find the next one and the finding has to be that a rotation is only real when it turns for the third time.**
- **A hut held by an office for a month from the twenty-fifth of April, renewed by the office, nobody asked, at a place the office says is unsafe.** The term ended on the twenty-fifth of May and the man is still there on the third of June, thirty-nine days on a term of thirty, and **an office that lets a term lapse has not ended anything; it has stopped saying when.** The office has not been asked and has not been asked to be.
- **Three toll boards with a keeper at each and nobody paid out of a rate for it, and the fourth day is the day the keeper starts.** The bottom line of the ninth-place board has seventeen sentences on it and one of them is a question nobody has answered. **The four-day problem is answered for now and the answer is three people and it is not money.**
- **A rotating multi-bearer charter drafted by five other people and closable by nobody in this batch.** Four bearers wanted: three who hold a week, at the ninth place, the fourth place and the east end, and **one whose only standing is the ending, who is not a slot, who is to be named by the three holders, and about whom Mr Vale has no part.** **A bridge-office signalman remains positively disqualified as a bearer and says so before he is asked; keeping a board does not qualify him.**
- **Four securities and none of them a sum**, and thirty-six coppers of metered water posted by the people they are owed to, to nobody, entered as a hostage because the district has no word for it. **A yard on the tide that is not a security and is entered as not one.**
- **The office's name, spelled out in full at the top of three boards and on five hundred printed sheets, and not found in a register in two hours of searching by a registrar, a bridge clerk, a licensed valuer and a sign-writer.** A sign-writer of sixty-one has the phrase twice in his own hand in a book on a shelf and no office and nobody who gave him the words. **The same name is in a sheet in a sleeve at 411, and in two lines in two books that are not joined, and the registrar has been asked to keep them apart and has agreed and gave the reason she gives every time.**

- **A water office notice that its own office has no copy of, printed by somebody not using the numbered form, and a clerk who was wrong about his own paperwork in the direction that made his own sentence harder to say.**
- **A bound second copy of the Harrow line book, eleven coppers, sitting in the hut at the ninth place on a shelf beside a stove, and the finding that a book kept nine miles from the only office that keeps a copy of it is a book that can be lost and that this district has decided that is the correct place for it.**
- **A five-hundred-sheet timetable, printed on the first of June, nine hundred and six coppers, with the name at the top, days of the month and not days of the week with the reason printed, the toll column four feet wide and empty with the reason printed inside it, the count each trade carried at the foot, the eight hundred and ninety-four the market does not have in the same type, and about nine hundred households in no row.** It was struck once, on a trestle table, and the strike was out of the printing and not out of a man's book. **It will go out of date and the market has voted no date.**
