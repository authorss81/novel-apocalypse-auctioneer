# Batch 0002 Summary — The First Hammer

## Batch outcome

Chapters 11–20 turn one fortunate crossing into a public service, then break it. Lot 17 gains a written **proposal schedule**, a **no-name remedy**, a **named-pair return point**, and a board that is used on someone other than Adrian for the first time. Selik Marne's official bridge is tightened by a false-alarm evacuation that is real, correct, and expensive. The counterfeit is traced to a printing plate and to a mark that is a claim about a system rather than a signature. Fourteen people come home from the far bank in Chapter 16, protected by a category Mara invented, and not one of them can say what was done to them. In Chapters 18–20 Adrian breaks a public undertaking he signed in front of a room, uses a private workshop promise to make the sale look secure, and closes a one-activation auction that twenty-eight people travel on. The outward side succeeds. The return bus does not come back on time, and the batch ends with nine people unaccounted for between the banks, nineteen stranded on the other one, and a complaint already on the wall in a nine-year-old's hand.

## Beats completed

- **Chapter 11.** Three honest claimants arrive for one twelve-minute route: fourteen cold boxes, a standing three-day service, and fourteen people stranded on the far bank. Adrian ranks the medicine first in front of nine people, catches himself, and states aloud that he wants to be the one who decides. He refuses the ranking and gives it to the board. The board is seated (Mara, Nessa Pell, Coll Ferrin, Marda Osk), refuses to record a preference, and records three requests with no priority. The token's condition is read aloud: cracked through the plate, a second route may not be available. A courier brings two bad pieces of news: Selik's review has been moved up, and a private buyer has paid for the next crossing.
- **Chapter 12.** The supervised bridge review, at the Brass Verdict Hall. Selik offers a narrower condition; Mara offers a supervised group designation with a sealable page. **A Warden gauge faults, the span reports movement, and Selik evacuates the deck — everyone off, counterweight locked, no exceptions.** Petra Sill, who had just been granted a provisional berth, is turned back down the ramp with her feverish child. The reading proves to be a false alarm: a dropped fuel can bent the sump float, and a chalked maintenance depth mark on the second pier read at lamplight as a hairline crack. The span is sound. The evacuation was still correct. Selik tightens the manifest in the same breath. Mara writes the false alarm and the evacuation in one paragraph so the tightening cannot pass as free caution. Selik accepts the review's written question and refuses Mara's remedy.
- **Chapter 13.** Orren traces the counterfeit to a printing plate, not a forger: identical miscut type across three brokers in three parts of the city. **The broken circle is not a signature. It is a sentence about a system, pressed onto unrelated documents — a Warden fuel return, a school ration ledger, a salt permit — to make paper look like the output of a settlement.** Orren cuts the Sela Pike margin note away from the evidence so a household cannot be found on the same page, prints eleven copies of the third page overnight, and gets a carrier to refuse the claim before he signs. Chapter ends with Sabin Tull already holding a signed claim and intending to move at first light.
- **Chapter 14.** Lina's kitchen exchange, with Mara Quill's seven columns copied onto the wall in charcoal plus three lines somebody added. The protected category is argued into shape: a group, a count, a steward, a destination, and a remedy in the book, and a paper note of who chose to take part held outside it. Wenna Keel wants her name written; Bett Ruhl wants nothing written. Lina admits out loud, in front of eleven people, that she wants Adrian in the room and would rather he were not needed. **Rell Bode lodges the first challenge against a public lot**: a remedy reached by naming her is not a remedy. The board upholds it in part and refuses to record any closing whose remedy depends on finding a person who refused to be named.
- **Chapter 15.** Tarin's bench. Standing is a relationship between a person, a route, and a return point — a named pair, not a property of the token — and a post on the ground is a landmark, not a return point, because anybody can move it. The new return is the second rail joint east of the bell post, under the lamp, in Tarin's bus, coming down from the far bank, plus a far-side condition for what he may do when the far side is not the road he left. Adrian pays for the axle in labor (forty shop hours and an honest re-rating of nine carts). Dessa Rill cannot be the remembering passenger: what she kept was an old rule for a city she is not in - **and Adrian sees the hole in that argument, that nothing in her was left to be a belief about the river, and does not raise it.** That hole is what Chapter 19 collects. A second route card shows the pre-Fracture maintenance bay the public maps omit.
- **Chapter 16.** Mara writes the seven-item proposal schedule and runs the first proposal through it herself. She refuses a permanent grant and refuses Adrian's private guarantee. **The board runs its first closing of its own: a return crossing.** Tarin and Wenna Keel go out; the eight children, two escorts, and four crew come home. Sixteen distinct people, eighteen tolls. The eight can name the platform and not one can say why they were sent; a seven-year-old reports that the toll takes the *why* and that she looked for a third thing and it is not there. Renn Fisk says the toll is not pain, it is politeness. **Mara chooses the hearing over the sealed page:** the remedy is called and recorded as *not discharged*, with the protected category named as the reason. The crowd's next demand is larger than the charter can honestly carry.
- **Chapter 17.** The larger shipment. Selik's tightened bridge can move four boxes a day and the named work crew; it cannot move the eleven. Halloway offers a complete private package with a labor term that falls on him. Adrian reads the token three times in two days and loses an afternoon. He solicits six ways his terms could be misused and answers three. Then, at four in the morning, with nobody stopping him, **he ranks the yard** — and notices that nobody stopped him because the room needed a man to say the order. Halloway's private warning: the public board will be sold to the people who can afford certainty, in daylight, by a fair process, with no dishonest act anywhere in it.
- **Chapter 18.** The breach. Adrian writes the auction sheet in the back office at three in the morning. **The return is drafted as an administrative extension of the outward term, with no separate toll and no person in it.** Mara brings the lot book into the room and reads his own undertaking aloud from page eleven. Adrian answers with the sentence: *the outward crossing is the emergency, the return term can be written within the hour, and nobody is being deceived because nothing has happened yet.* Tarin and Selik both refuse the sheet. Adrian then writes a **private workshop undertaking** — inspection press, four hundred and ten coppers, one season of repair-crew labor — stamps it with his auction stamp, and keeps it out of the public book while putting a line about it on the sheet, deferred to the return term.
- **Chapter 19.** The first full auction, for one defined use, not ownership and not a permanent route. No money is bid; five groups post obligations instead. The counterfeit is exposed by a carrier opening the fold, not by a verdict, and Halloway reads his own longer labor term in public and concedes where the seven-day clause came from. Adrian discloses the outward toll and says the return may cost again, framed as the same administrative term. **Tarin says no** until the board records that the return is unwritten and that he said so before the case opened. Twenty-nine are offered and **twenty-eight travel** — one of the eleven children refuses, and the refusal is deducted aloud in the count. **Tarin names the passenger himself and then reverses himself in the yard, on the record and twice:** he ruled in Chapter 15, on a bench, with the woman it was about standing there to hear it, that Dessa could not hold the destination, and now says in front of two hundred people that it should have been said in front of two hundred people then; a person with no reason left is not the same as a person who knows the way, and if he is wrong the wrong is his and not Mr. Vale's and not the registrar's, because the registrar only wrote it down. **Adrian is the one who failed to raise it**: he did not make it to Tarin, and handed the whole of it to Mara three days later only because she asked him a direct question. **Bevon Rahl then uses his witness's privilege** to have the count, the passenger, and the toll read back to him before the token leaves the case, because all three changed after he gave his word. The outward side succeeds completely: the ward gets the boxes, the riggers walk to the cut, and Fen Ambrose divides the far side on the ramp — four children in to the school house with two of the stewards, six children back on the bus with the third. Then the bus turns with nine people on it — Tarin, Dessa, one steward, six of the ten children — and the route closes before it comes out, leaving nineteen on the far bank. **The reader's own record and the board's page both say: the return is unwritten.**
- **Chapter 20.** The same morning, from twenty to eight to five minutes past one. Adrian's first three moves are to extend, reprice, and reread. He reads the RETURN field four times and understands that this was not a silence but a sentence in the place a condition goes. The token itself reports `RETURN: NO CONDITION IN THE RECORD.` Mara forces a known-and-unknown list in the yard before he can act. Selik asks for the charter, the security, and the return term in that order, and says in front of two hundred people that there is no security on this charter. A repair-crew foreman says Adrian told him the press stood behind it, in a room. Adrian admits the breach out loud. Stenn withdraws the ward market's fuel. Mara suspends the road. The search finds the marked spur empty, the platform empty, and the bell post's one arm turned to point along the spur instead of across it, four yards past the joint Tarin Kest has never missed in his life. Nobody has been near the post. A public complaint goes up on the board in a school hand, written by the boy who refused, before anyone has agreed what to call any of it.

## New canon and state

- **The proposal schedule** (seven items) and the **no-name remedy** are new operating rules of the Lot 17 public lot, recorded in `bible/world.md` and `bible/terminology.md`. A closing cannot be recorded from a document made in a back office at night.
- A **named-pair return point** and a **far-side condition** are now required by standing, recorded in `bible/power-system.md` and `bible/world.md`. A landmark alone is not a return point because a post can be moved.
- **Tolls are counted by leg, and travelers and tolls are counted separately.** The Chapter 16 return carried sixteen distinct people and took eighteen tolls. The Chapter 19 outward leg carried **twenty-eight travelers and twenty-eight tolls out of twenty-nine offered**, because one child refused and the refusal is deducted aloud in the count, so the book holds offers, travelers, and tolls as three separate numbers. **Every headcount in this batch closes and has been audited: 19 far bank + 9 on the bus = 28 traveled.** Recorded in `bible/world.md` and `bible/terminology.md`.
- The public board may **uphold a challenge in part** and refuse to record a closing on the ground that no proposal is in the book. It still cannot grant a use. Both refusals are shown with all four seats present and named aloud.
- **Selik's tightened manifest** (responsible party physically at the near abutment; a second adult name for any child under twelve; the certified gauge housing before any passenger crossing; halved supervised slots) and the certified housing after the Chapter 12 false alarm are canon and recorded in `bible/world.md`.
- **Halloway's public labor term is fifteen days and falls on him**, written on the first page, and he states in the Chapter 17 yard that the seven-day version came from a room he rents to three brokers, that he sold the first one, and that he cannot shut it down. This is a change of position, not a correction: the counterfeit still circulates, and the carriers who refuse it are still the only reliable source of information about it.
- **The broken circle is a claim about a system, not a signature.** It is pressed onto unrelated documents to make paper look like the output of a settlement. It is still a clue only: no office, clerk, place, or person has been identified, and the regional heading is still scraped away.
- **The token rides in the bus and comes home by itself.** It is seated in the route-control slot above the old fare box inside the vehicle, so it goes into the Fault with the bus, and when the route closes it returns to the public case at Lot 17 without the bus. Chapter 19 is the first time that has happened with a vehicle still out there, and the batch's closing image is the object on the board in front of two hundred people while nine of them are not on any bank. **A returned object is not a returned bus, and the next writer must not use the token as a rescue.** Recorded in `bible/world.md`.
- Adrian's **private workshop undertaking** is a real, inspectable asset (inspection press, four hundred and ten coppers, one season of repair-crew labor with three named hands) and a real obligation, and it is **not** in the public lot book. It is not security: Adrian is Stage 1 and cannot post security, and the book says so.
- Adrian's **public undertaking is breached**, out loud, in a yard, in front of two hundred people, and by the reader's own hand at the bottom of the board's page. He stated the outward toll and that the return would cost again; he did not state the return toll, and he pre-consented twenty-eight people to it in a clause that required no one to be told what they were agreeing to.

## Relationship movement

- **Adrian and Mara.** She is no longer able to reach him through procedure alone, and the tool that worked — the public undertaking — is now evidence against him rather than reassurance. She objects on the record at every reading of the sheet, and she suspends the road herself. No romantic resolution, and no softening.
- **Adrian and Tarin.** Tarin's refusal becomes a form of safety: he says no in the yard in front of two hundred people and then offers the two honest alternatives. He owns his own part in the three-day postponement rather than letting Adrian be the one who finally says it. He is missing at the end of the batch.
- **Adrian and Orren.** Orren supplies the worst of the six warnings in Chapter 17 ("say what it costs and who pays it separately") *after* Adrian has promised to, which is what makes Chapter 18 a choice rather than a lapse. He still does not trust the process, and he still controls the evidence.
- **Adrian and Lina.** She stops expecting to be filed and says so in front of the room, while making clear she wants him present and would rather he were not needed. She is no longer filing him as a future problem and is not filing him as a solution.
- **Adrian and Selik.** Rivalry with respect, hardened. Selik holds a flag on a real joint and refuses to hold one on a sentence, and he makes Adrian's undertaking public property in a yard by pointing out that Adrian is the only person in Alder Reach regulated by a promise he wrote himself.
- **Mara and the Unlisted.** The protected channel stops being a courtesy and becomes a job with a cost, and Mara offers it as a job with a bad reputation and no authority, which is the only kind of offer Lina will take.
- **Tarin and Bryn.** One page, and it is a refusal with a reason: wanting it is not allowed.

## Unresolved handoff

Nine people are unaccounted for between the banks: Tarin Kest, Dessa Rill, one of the three escort stewards, and six of the ten shelter children who boarded. Nineteen are on the far bank — four clinic crew, nine signal hands, four children, two stewards. Nineteen and nine are the same twenty-eight, and the token is on its fourth opening. The bell post's arm has been turned four yards past the joint. Nobody has been near it. The token is cracked through the plate, the public road is suspended, the ward market has withdrawn, and three boxes of clinic stores are due at the near abutment by four in the afternoon. **Do not resolve the missing group, restore the memory, or name the false destination's cause in Chapters 21–30; the reversal is Chapters 23–26 and its aftermath runs to 33.**

## Plan deviations

The prose is canon. Where it departs from `outline/batches/volume-01-batch-0002.md`, it departs like this.

- **Chapter 12, the counter-argument witness.** The card specified "a witness Halloway sends." The prose uses Delia Fenn, sent and paid by Ossie Prent, a lamp-trimmer who supplies wicks to both Halloway's warehouse and the clinic. Reason: making the manifest's strongest defender a person *bought by* a broker is stronger than making her bought by the broker's employer, and it keeps Halloway from being the man who pays for arguments against disclosure — which would have made him a schemer in a batch where he is supposed to remain a better offer.
- **Chapter 12, the false alarm's mechanism.** The card offered "a chalked depth mark mistaken for a hairline crack, or a gauge with a bent float." The prose uses both, causally: a dropped fuel can bent the sump float, and the chalked depth mark is what made the first Warden on the deck call it structural movement rather than an instrument fault. Neither cause is the other, and the span is genuinely sound.
- **Chapter 12, the second record.** The card required *two* records: the manifest identifying a missing person after a fault, **and** the same rule used to expose an Unlisted household. The prose has Delia Fenn (a protective use of the manifest) and Petra Sill (a household the Wardens cannot read, a child with no record anywhere), and argues the exposure of a household in the abstract — *that is a registry with a lid on it* — rather than putting a named household in a book on the page. Reason: an on-page exposure would have spent Rell Bode's Chapter 14 challenge and Bett Ruhl's household before either has been earned, and the argument-in-the-abstract is what Chapter 23's no-name remedy is later made of.
- **Chapter 16, the boy who refused.** The card required the boy who refused in Chapter 9 to be in Chapter 16, dry on the near bank, *the one person who can tell the room what the crossing cost.* He is absent from the chapter: Alma Dren reports him unreachable and says, *I have got four sentences, and none of them are the crossing.* Reason: the return crossing runs on adults who have already paid, and a nine-year-old's account in that room would have settled the question the book is trying to keep open. The beat is deferred and inverted into **his** Chapter 20 public complaint, which is stronger, and is recorded in `bible/characters.md`.
- **Chapter 16, the remembering passenger.** The card required a new remembering passenger and said the closing must name what that person is being asked to carry. The prose makes it Wenna Keel, the mother of one of the eight, and makes the disclosure of *why* the load-bearing scene: she is asked to name the place and is not permitted to name the reason, which means the toll takes her reason on the way out while she is required to function. Her reason is spoken once in the yard and is deliberately not written down.
- **Chapter 16, the board's remedy.** The card's required beat asked whether a second payment takes anything at all. The prose answers it from the object (`A TRAVELER WHO HAS ALREADY PAID: NOT STATED`) and then answers it again in a child: the toll takes the *why* both times, she looked, and there was no third thing. The card also required the stewards to answer for the eight aloud; they do, and the roll they have kept for eleven days is the evidence that they accepted the duty for nine.
- **Chapters 15 and 19, Dessa Rill as the remembering passenger.** Chapter 15 rules flatly that she cannot hold the destination, because a route held open by a passenger's memory is held open by whatever that passenger still believes is true and what she has left is an old rule for a city she is not in. Chapter 19 needs a remembering passenger and cannot use her without reopening that. **The prose reopens it inside the chapter, on the page, in the yard, and in Tarin's own mouth:** Adrian watches the gap go past him in Chapter 15, does not pick it up, and never puts it to Tarin; in the Chapter 19 yard **Tarin names her himself before the registrar records her**, states that he said it on a bench with the woman it was about standing there to hear it, that he is changing it in front of two hundred people, that she cannot hold a place she has a reason for and has no reason left, that a person with nothing left is not the same as a person who knows the way, and that if he is wrong the wrong is his and not Mr. Vale's and not the registrar's. **Bevon Rahl** then has the count, the passenger, and the toll read back to him before the token leaves the case, because all three changed after he gave his word. **Chapter 15 is not wrong and must not be rewritten. It is incomplete, and the gap is Adrian's: he watched it go past him and did not pick it up, and never put it to Tarin at all.** This is the batch's standing model for how a prior ruling may be reopened: name it aloud, in public, by the person who made it, say where it was first said, and put the liability on him.
- **Chapters 19–20, the return party's size.** The cards did not fix a return count. The prose makes it nine — bearer, remembering passenger, one of the three stewards, and six of the ten children who boarded — chosen on the ramp by Fen Ambrose, who was not on the bus, which is how a charter with no return field gets its return party anyway. Nineteen are left on the far bank. Both numbers are audited aloud in Chapter 20, and both close: 19 + 9 = 28 travelers out of 29 offered.
- **Chapter 20, the object reports the breach.** The card's major turn was Adrian reading the term back from his own handwriting. The prose adds one Tally panel in which the token states the same thing independently — `RETURN: NO CONDITION IN THE RECORD.` Reason: it makes the cost of the breach a public, checkable fact rather than a private shame, which is the only form of honesty available to him in that yard.
- **Chapter 18, the objections list.** The card for Chapter 17 asked Adrian to ask others "to make the terms harder to misuse." The prose collects seven lines from six people and names the one that matters, and Chapter 18's opening shows that Adrian answered three. The warning he ignored was therefore in his own hand on his own table.
- **Chapter 11, the standing refusal window.** The card specified "a way for affected people to refuse." The prose tightens it: the window closes when the case is physically opened, and Nessa Pell reads the time aloud and the board checks it. This is carried into the Chapter 16 and 19 proposal schedule.
- **Chapter 19, the "auction."** The card calls for a public auction for one defined use. The prose has no bidding: five groups post obligations and there is no money in the room. Reason: Adrian is a Reader, not a Binder, and a bidding sale he cannot price would have made him overpowered for a chapter in which his power is supposed to be exactly what it has always been.
- **Length.** These ten chapters run 3,500–5,900 words, against a 1,700–2,200 norm recorded for Batch 0001, where Chapter 9 at 3,970 was accepted as a defensible climax. The batch is 44,000 words. Chapters 16 and 19 are the two the norm would flag; both are institutional set-pieces in which a great deal of the volume's procedure has to be visible at once, and cutting them to hit a number would remove the material Chapters 21–30 have to argue with. Flagged rather than actioned, as in the previous batch, and a reviewer should say if they disagree.
