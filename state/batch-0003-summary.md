# Batch 0003 Summary — The First Hammer

## Batch outcome

Chapters 21–30 find the nine, price the loss, stop the road, publish the failure, organise the counterfeit into a service, close the official bridge on the merits, and walk nineteen people home over a footbridge that is destroyed behind them. The batch contains the volume's midpoint reversal, and the reversal is **not a discovery**. Adrian's breach was entered in the book on page eleven and on the board's page and in his own mouth in Chapter 20; by Chapter 21 every person in the yard already knows what he did. The thing that arrives in this batch is the **bill**, and the bill is a child with no name in a public book, a child that Adrian cannot price, a bridge certificate he did not know was in danger, and a wreck of his own reputation. By Chapter 30 the Hearth Line is administratively possible and not dependable, and the public lot is the only instrument in Alder Reach that can still act.

## Beats completed

- **Chapter 21 — The Search Is a Public Thing.** Adrian converts a mob into a search: **a route, an end, a person who answers, and a way to stop it** — the last of which is the Chapter 16 refusal-window rule applied to a crowd hunting a bus, and Mara Quill enters it in the book as *the rule was never about charters, it was about rooms*. The board cannot grant a search, only Selik can, and Adrian must **go and ask for his own terms**, which is the first time in eleven days he has wanted another man's hand in one of his documents. Selik's fourth term is that a walking party does not go onto the ballast past the second rail, ever, and that the search walks the gravel once and the book says what it did to the place. **The three boxes went; the draught was refused at the scale as a cylinder and went by the river stair by hand over four hours.** The search finds one nailed-heel print in the platform gravel, made before the first party walked it, going toward the river wall, and **Nell Sarr finds a clean arc of broken rust inside the bell-post socket**: somebody put a hand in it and swung the arm, inside a day or two of the crossing, and nobody was near the post.
- **Chapter 22 — What the Span Recorded (Selik).** The three boxes cross on the supervised cargo exception and the draught does not. The Wardens' far-bank queue is eleven deep against four boxes a day and Selik refuses a fifth box on the record and sends all eleven a copy of the night's log at his own cost. The certified gauge housing — signed by two people who are not him, so the readings are his instrument and not his opinion — **logged a movement at 7:37 on the morning of the nineteenth, on a closed, empty span that did not move, on the pilot channel, which is the water office's fourth trace with no instrument attached to it and which has never moved in nine years.** Vasey and the second fitter suspend the certificate under the water office's own clause. Selik applies his own March sentence — *a structure that can report a fault it cannot account for is not a structure I carry passengers on* — to his own paperwork and says so before anybody else can. He copies the reading to Lot 17's public board at midnight, in his own words, in advance of any request, because a reading nobody can account for is about to be shaped by whoever talks about it first.
- **Chapter 23 — The Maintenance Spur.** The nine are found at the near-side maintenance spur, three streets west of the depot: the bus standing with its nose against a service wall, doors open, **engine idling after eleven hours with nobody at the wheel**, the barrow with the broken wheel taken off the platform, one escort steward and six children and Dessa Rill, and **Tarin Kest four streets away, on his feet, at the top of a service cut, having stopped the vehicle, got out, and walked back toward the wall exactly as the Chapter 15 far-side condition says.** The clause saved the eight and it cost eleven hours, four streets, and a child who does not wake. Tarin says both halves out loud and puts it on himself. **The cause of the broken pair is established: the marking moved, because a hand was in the socket.** Adrian writes what he does not know and refuses to let a cause be written by anybody else. Dessa has the route and the rules and no reason for any of it, including the reason she is the one who drives. The child dies between eleven and half past twelve; her name goes on a sealed sheet and nowhere else. **At ten past four in the morning the depot notice board has rewritten itself and four lines in Adrian's own unstamped hand name Lot 17's inspection press at three hundred and eighty, a season's repair-crew term, and the sentence about the entry following the return term.**
- **Chapter 24 — The Bill Comes Due (Mara).** The board sits at half past nine with all four seats named aloud and renews the steward's term on the record because *a term that expires in a crisis is a term that can be ended by anybody who is uncomfortable*. The child's death is entered as a line in a public book with no name in it, and the remedy is called and **not discharged**. Four documents changed in one night: the notice board, a clinic receiving slip, a page of the public lot book (which now says two different things about the security on two pages in the same hand, neither struck), and **Mara Quill's own sealed-page register, which now appears to record a fourth sheet that has never existed** — and she says in public, in her own words, that the registrar cannot certify her own instrument and that the change may have come from her page. Adrian wants the line struck off the board; the board cannot strike a line, only record what happened. Selik's Warden Culler Bray says the two words *hazard* and *story*. **Mara's proposal: enter the private undertaking as offered and not verified, and hand the valuation to the yard.** Four hundred people price Adrian's workshop in public and arrive at **six hundred and twenty-nine coppers**, and the last line in the yard is the repair-crew foreman asking which of the two words his brother was owed on.
- **Chapter 25 — A Second Emergency Exception.** Adrian writes a nine-line return term at five in the morning and decides at ten past six not to enter it. He proposes a second crossing to fetch the nineteen, reads the seven items properly, names Tarin as bearer, and puts **RETURN: term held by the reader and entered before the token leaves the case** in front of six hundred people — the same act in the same yard in the same words, four days apart, and the board's page from the nineteenth is still open on the crate saying *the return is unwritten* in Mara's hand above his signature. **Sabin Tull, a board member, names it** from the pages. The four seats refuse and Mara refuses with them, and states in public that **the suspension is the board's and not hers**. Stenn and the tenth hand of the signal crew both refuse his argument, and his argument is good and true. Adrian says Iven Tallow's name out loud as evidence against himself and is not believed. **Mara says the accusation**: not that Iven is alive, but that a man with emergency authority and a gift for reading damaged paper once worked a shape — the emergency is now, this part is written, the rest follows — and Adrian has described his out loud twice in his own handwriting. Adrian is not caught by surprise. He knew at ten past six and his hands were steady, and nothing in him objected, and that is what he cannot get past.
- **Chapter 26 — The Man Who Makes It Temporary.** The next morning at nine, Mara reads the whole of the nineteenth out in public — the undertaking, the RETURN field, **the two refusals that named the problem in advance**, and the refusals stay on the record. Adrian reads out the nine lines he kept in a tin, and Sabin and Marda check them and find no fold, and the term is entered as *offered for a future use and not attached to any use*. He gives the market the only thing he has: a list of four things he cannot do, and a refusal to put a number on his own loss in a yard where six hundred people are looking at a rate sheet. **The room turns to Mara and does not ask Adrian a single question, and the difference is not hostility.** Mara writes the notice to the far bank in the third person, because the third person is the only tense that will still be true in a week, and it is read aloud on the ramp and in the signal hut and in the school yard, and a woman who can read reads the second half of it herself. The yard ends the day unable to agree on a **word** — urgent, scheduled, priority.
- **Chapter 27 — Publishing the Failure.** The whole of the nineteenth goes into the public book in full, including the deferral line, the stamped private undertaking, the six objections in the order they were given, and **a two-page list of the nine things the charter did not contain** — Mara Quill's Chapter 17 line, entered at the front. The ward market suspends; two long-standing lots leave; the inspection yard closes for two days while he re-prices 140 salvage lots and finds them **four hundred and ninety coppers below** the flood-year figures, because thirty-one of them were priced by a man pretending he was not in a hurry. A trader brings him a damaged feed pump and gets four lines and a gap, and says *you used to be quicker than that*, and Adrian agrees and loses an hour. Halloway comes and says the only man in Alder Reach with a road-shaped offer is him, and that a market with one road in it needs a shopkeeper and not a public process. **On the twenty-ninth Adrian pays: the yard's own figure, minus the nine coppers and beside the two hundred and fifty-eight, plus salvage re-priced downward, plus the leases — one thousand one hundred and nineteen — and enters the shortfall in the same entry, uncomputed and not nil, because a nil would be a number he chose.** He says in public that the charter was *not breached* and that this is the worse sentence. A barrow-hauler's association of eleven meets at the tram arch, reads the failure out loud, and refuses Halloway **because they can check the next one** — the first recorded refusal in the book of a good thing, on the grounds that people can check it. The chapter closes at two in the morning on the first of the month with the first document Adrian has written in seventeen years that does not try to look finished.
- **Chapter 28 — The Second Backwash (Orren).** Counterfeit claims are at twenty-one and rising; a warning cannot stop a person holding a barrow at four in the morning. Orren's answer is a **comparison, not a warning**: the claim in hand set beside what the carrier is signing, ninth line opened out, printed **on the back of the ward market's own blank claim stock**, read aloud at a table at the tram arch at four in the morning by Tove Alard, a cooper's apprentice, and Marda Osk for anyone who cannot read. Twenty-two claims in three nights. Bett Ruhl sets the limit — the service has no address and is described as *the place with the tea*, and nobody prints a line that puts her floor in a public place — and Orren accepts it. Then **Joss Amery, a carter with a wife in the fever ward, crosses the footbridge in the dark on a counterfeit that has no fold in it at all**, because it is nine flat lines and it says the crossing is his and does not need a marshal. He loses the barrow-load into a flooded maintenance trench and **loses three whole days**; a coal ration board in the wrong quarter is changed on the second of the month; nobody can put a claim on a person they cannot point at. Halloway then says, in a public yard and not lying, that a published road that failed has made the counterfeit *more* credible, because paper has become checkable and what people now buy is speed and legibility — and that Adrian has built the only thing he has ever been afraid of.
- **Chapter 29 — The Bridge Comes Down (Selik).** The ward market's appointee votes against the closure and her dissent is entered in her own words. Selik carries his honesty about method into the room: he did not ask the water office to suspend the certificate, and the second fitter reached the same conclusion independently and was annoyed about it. Adrian is called and gives the concession that makes Chapters 30 work: **a public route can be less safe than a controlled one on any given morning, and a page is not a floor, and the one thing he will not say is that the book was enough.** The motion is not the closure — the closure is the water office's and the Warden's — and the order requires a public inspection on stated days at the ward market's expense, read out by a clerk the table appoints. **Mara Quill enters in the minutes that she came to stop the closure and could not construct an argument against it**, and Selik says that in nine years no registrar has ever examined this and that the failure is the review. Selik shakes hands with Adrian in the yard and puts in the minutes that Adrian's published failure is why his own instrument got examined, because a day a bridge closure is won *because a road failed* is a day the district believes roads are the answer. The footbridge is not a crossing and will not be made one. Culler Bray ends the chapter by naming a drafting instruction for a **standing order: one St. Orra crossing, one office responsible, one manifest, no expiry**.
- **Chapter 30 — A Witness Who Is Not Named (Lina).** Four documents on the kitchen wall. The arithmetic: nineteen people, four at a time, five crossings, in two days, with no roll and no possibility of getting one. Lina's design puts the protected category into public service — a **named steward in the public book** (hers, at the price of eleven years of being unrecorded), a count, a destination, a remedy, and a **split-strip witness chain** carrying a count, a place, a date and a **mark instead of a name**, matched out loud in a kitchen. She says the hard thing first: **a protected category has carried a passage twice and a loss once, and the only remedy available for the dead child is a number with her not in it, and nobody will ever be able to check whether the number was right.** She prices the cost in a list because a list can be written down and a feeling cannot. **Colm Vaun refuses to sign a strip, is not argued with, and is on the rope four times anyway; Bett Ruhl rebuilds the design so that the person inside the group never touches the paper at all.** The nineteen come home over two days — the last four at half past three with forty minutes of light, the marshal deciding on a lamp what no book could decide — and the middle plank goes into the cut on the last crossing, six feet above nine feet of water, saved by a rope practised for an hour the evening before. **The far bank is cut off again that afternoon, and a plank is in the water to prove the price.** Adrian comes to the reading of the full public book in Bett Ruhl's kitchen, reads the object off the open book and gets three lines — `NO CROSSING ON THIS ACCOUNT. / NINETEEN ACCOUNTED FOR BY HAND.` — and Mara says the object has declined to certify the passage and that this is the correct answer. Bett Ruhl's household hears itself counted in a public book without being named, and nobody in the room looks at her. Lina imposes a limit on Mara: the entry for the fifteenth is read and accepted and is thinner than the one she would have written on the eighteenth, and it gets read back in the room with two people in it who are not clerks. Two documents on one wall, two answers, and a Hearth Line that is administratively possible and not dependable.

## New canon and state

- **The three numbers close and must not drift.** Chapter 19: **29 offered, 28 travelled, 28 tolls on the outward leg, no return leg** because the charter had no return field. **Nineteen stranded on the far bank; nine unaccounted for; nine found at the maintenance spur on the twentieth (eight alive, one dead overnight); nineteen walked home over the footbridge on the eleventh and twelfth.** **No one of the nine comes home before the Chapter 30 passage** — a writer who quietly brings a signal hand back in Chapter 25 breaks the count. The signal foreman is on the far bank throughout; the counter is the **tenth** hand on this bank and is the near-bank voice of the crew.
- **The route closed at 7:37 a.m.** The bus went out at 7:25, was on the far side at nine minutes, turned at nine minutes past, and the term closed at twelve minutes. Chapter 20 runs from 8:30 to 1:05 that same morning, and **Chapters 21–30 are the same day and the following three weeks** — 21 and 22 are the afternoon and night of the crossing day.
- **The cause of the failed return is the movement of the marking.** A hand was in the bell-post socket; the rust arc is clean and the paint under the arm is unweathered; the move happened inside a day or two of the crossing. **Who did it, how, and why are not established and must not be.** There is one nailed-heel print in the platform gravel, older than the search, going toward the river wall, belonging to nobody present. The reader has looked for a person to blame for six hours and has not found one, and the book says that the reason he has not found one is not that nobody in the city *could* be.
- **The far-side condition worked and it was expensive.** Tarin stopped the vehicle in the service cut at 7:38, got out, and walked back toward the wall, and nobody followed him onto it; the bus came up the spur empty-handed and stopped at the service wall, which is how eight people got off it alive. It cost eleven hours, four streets, six children in a locked yard, and a child who died. **The batch refuses to treat the clause as a triumph.**
- **The bus idled for eleven hours and forty minutes with nobody in the driver's seat** and came out of the Fault under its own power when the term closed. This is observed fact and is **not** established as a mechanic. Nobody in Alder Reach can say what it is, and the maintenance spur has never been explained either.
- **Dessa Rill is home and is not restored.** She has the route, the rules (*green is the water, red is lamps*), and the skill, and she has lost the reason she is the one who drives the medicine, and she said so in those words. **Nobody in Alder Reach can say whether the road took that or the cold did**, and it must stay that way.
- **The unpaid return toll has produced backwash on records.** Four documents changed in one night: the Lot 17 notice board, a clinic receiving slip, a page of the public lot book, and Mara Quill's sealed-page register. The register now appears to record a fourth sealed sheet that has never existed. The spill is a **record** event, not a revelation: it names no person, no office, and no cause, and **the object never claims to have done it.**
- **The Last Fare has answered five unasked questions in short plain lines** — the fifth being `RETURN: NO CONDITION IN THE RECORD. / TOLL ON THE RETURN: NOT PAID. / NEARBY ACTIVE RECORDS: CHANGED.` It has still never said what a second payment of the toll takes. **This is escalating behaviour, not a mechanic, and there is no fifth activation in this batch.**
- **The private undertaking is now public and the yard has priced it: six hundred and twenty-nine coppers** (press 380 less a 9-copper leak, plus a season of three hands at 258 by the ward market's own rate sheet, computed twice by a clerk who would not be hurried). Adrian's payment on the twenty-ninth is **one thousand one hundred and nineteen**, at figures he set, with the shortfall entered as uncomputed and not nil. **The security is real and small, and the last line of Chapter 24 is the foreman asking which of the two words his brother was owed on.**
- **The claim-comparison service exists** at the tram arch at four in the morning, on the back of ward market claim stock, staffed by named people, and it has no address. Recorded in `bible/world.md`.
- **The protected passage is now a public service** with a named steward, a count, a destination, a remedy, and split strips marked rather than signed. Recorded in `bible/world.md`. Lina's name is in the public book.
- **Selik's span is shut to all carriage**, its certificate suspended by the water office, and the closure order of the ninth states that **one office is responsible for the crossing at St. Orra** and is closed-ended. A **standing order for a permanently staffed crossing is being drafted in a room Adrian is not in.**
- **The footbridge's middle plank is in the service cut** and the far bank has no way back at all. It is a plank, a rope, two lamps, a floor, a stove, four coppers of flour, and eighteen hours of nine people's time.
- **Nessa Pell's steward term was renewed for seven days in public on the twenty-first**, on her own request, on the record. **The board seats in this batch are Sabin Tull and Marda Osk** (plus Nessa Pell as sitting steward and Mara Quill as registrar, non-voting and non-independent).

## Relationship movement

- **Adrian and Mara.** The rupture completes and then becomes work. She cannot reach him by procedure, so she reaches him with a piece of paper read out in the middle of a day; he cannot answer, so he publishes. **No romantic resolution and no softening.** Her process is now on a document she did not write and cannot audit, her own instrument is in the spill, and she says so in public before anyone else does. Lina is the first person who imposes a limit on her, and she agrees to it in front of nine people.
- **Adrian and Tarin.** The clause Tarin insisted on is the reason eight people are alive and the reason a child is dead, and he says both halves and puts it on himself. He is the bearer Adrian reaches for in Chapter 25 and the yard that will not take him. Their friendship in this batch is a working one.
- **Adrian and Orren.** Orren's Chapter 17 warning is the one that gets published, as a two-page list of what was *not* in the charter, at the front. He converts his stolen method into public work, is limited by a cook, is not made safe, and prints Adrian's name on a public wall.
- **Adrian and Lina.** She names the cost of his publishing — that a book only works if the people who have to check it are the people it is accountable to — and refuses to let him have a triumph out of it. She will not be filed and she will not be thanked.
- **Adrian and Selik.** Rivalry with respect, two men conceding the same point in different rooms. Selik puts Adrian's failure into the minutes as the reason his own instrument got examined.
- **Adrian and Halloway.** The counterfeit is now a *service* organised against Halloway's *service*, and Halloway says in public that Adrian built the only thing he has ever been afraid of, and then sells a claim in the same yard. He is not humiliated and he is more credible after the failure, and he said so first.
- **Selik and the district.** He wins, he is right, and he has a standing order being drafted in a room he is not gloating about. His caution is vindicated by the same thing that hurt the child on the ramp and he knows it.

## Review pass

The batch was reviewed before closing and the findings were actioned in the prose, not in the summary. Fixed in the text: the Chapter 22 / Chapter 29 conflict over who procured the certificate suspension (**Chapter 22's sequence is canon and Chapter 29 now tells Selik's own decision against himself**, which is a better scene than the one it replaced); the housing's rebuild date; the Chapter 30 crossing arithmetic, which now stages **five crossings of five on the deck** and matches the plan announced in the first page; three chapter-number citations that had leaked into the narration; Adrian's Chapter 26 misstatement of the bell-post fact (date, actor, and distance all wrong) and his claim to have once run a bus party; Adrian's Chapter 29 "a boy on a bus tonight"; the repair-crew foreman's brother, **who walked over on the fourth under a marshal and a lamp and was never in any count** and is now said so in Chapter 26 and entered in the book; the weekday in Chapter 25; the reading count and the flood-year figure in Chapter 27; the Chapter 24 "four days apart" and the crowd size; the Chapter 28 claim arithmetic; the Chapter 29 letter arithmetic; the Chapter 30 intervals, hours, and Orren's age; the Chapter 30 reading time; a garbled self-correction in Chapter 26; and Chapter 21's duplicated numbered terms, which now run One to Six once each with Nessa supplying the sixth. The batch's two "reader-in-chief" figures were separated in `bible/characters.md` so that Nessa Pell keeps the tolls and Sabin Tull keeps the page.

**Left alone deliberately, and a reviewer should look at them:** seven of the ten chapters are set in the Lot 17 yard, and the seven that are not (22, 28, 30) are the three that most feel like a novel. Adrian gives four public self-indictations in the batch and Chapters 27 and 30 are close enough together to be noticed. The house tics — the hand on the crate, *I would like the yard to*, *and I am not going to* — are cut back but not removed, because they are the voice, and the voice calcifying is a volume-scale problem rather than a batch-scale one.

## Unresolved handoff

The public road is suspended and no fifth activation is on the record. The token is cracked through the plate in public custody under a seven-day steward term renewed on the twenty-first. **No public road exists and the official bridge is shut**, so the far bank has nothing at all, and the plank that would have changed that is in the water. Adrian has published the whole failure and paid a figure that is not enough. The counterfeit is a service that is winning on speed. Selik's closure order says one office is responsible for the crossing; the protected channel says a household can be accounted for without a name; **both are right and neither is dependable.** The pilot line on a certified instrument drew a mark at 7:37 on a bridge that did not move, and the certificate is suspended and the span is shut, and nobody can say why. Adrian is still a Stage 1 Reader with no posted security, a third reading-cost hour in the book this month, and the first document he has written in seventeen years that does not try to look finished.

## Plan deviations

The prose is canon. Where it departs from `workspace/volume-01/batch-0003/PROMPT.md`, it departs like this.

- **The day of Chapter 21.** The prompt's starting state says the batch opens "on the afternoon of the day after the Chapter 19 crossing," while the Chapter 21 card says "the afternoon of Chapter 20," and Chapter 20 is canonically the same morning as Chapter 19 (8:30 a.m. to 1:05 p.m.). **The prose follows the card and the continuity: Chapters 21 and 22 are the afternoon and night of the crossing day.** Ch 20's 7:37 a.m. closing time and its 53-minute interval to 8:30 are the reference.
- **Chapter 26 is the following morning, not the same afternoon.** Chapter 25 closes with Mara Quill announcing that she will read the charter of the nineteenth out at nine the next morning. Chapter 26 is that session, so the "same day" of the card becomes the next morning. The beat is unchanged and the accusation, the publication, and the far-bank notice all land in one room, which is stronger.
- **Chapter 25's second exception is a refusal, not an attempt.** The card's repetition is preserved exactly — Adrian writes the return term, decides not to enter it, and puts *term held by the reader and entered before the token leaves the case* in front of six hundred people — and it ends with the board refusing to record a closing rather than with a token being seated. Reason: Adrian is Stage 1 and cannot close, a fifth bare activation would be a third unmanned road, and the card itself requires that the proposal not be recorded. The moral content of the repetition is intact and the physical one is refused, which is what the card asks for.
- **The near-bank signal voice in Chapters 25, 26 and 29 is the counter, the tenth hand, not the foreman.** The first draft had the grey foreman speaking on the near bank, which would have put him off the far bank and broken the nineteen. The foreman stays on the far bank and reads the registrar's notice aloud there; the counter is the near-bank voice and says so in the yard.
- **The rider at the Chapter 25 yard is the tenth hand and no one has come home.** The card's "the nine who came home are home" is used as a pressure, not as a fact requiring a return: the Chapter 30 footbridge passage is the only thing that brings anyone back, so the nineteen is a single event and cannot be duplicated.
- **Chapter 22's major turn is a pilot channel, not a load trace.** A gauge fault on a load cell would have been an instrument fault Selik could simply replace. The fourth trace with no instrument on it is the water office's own anti-false-flat mechanism, so the reading is *correct about the instrument and unaccounted for about the bridge*, and the certificate has to be suspended by people who are not Selik. That is what makes Chapter 29 defensible without making Selik the villain.
- **The child dies.** The card's Chapter 23 cost ("a child who will not wake") and Chapter 24's "the bill" are written as a real loss, with the name on a sealed sheet and nowhere else, and the no-name remedy called and not discharged over it. This is the batch's load-bearing moral event and it is what makes the protected category's *loss* half (Chapter 30) a real problem rather than a speech.
- **The bus engine idles for eleven hours with no driver.** An observed fact with no explanation, added so that the find has a horror in it that nobody can price. It is not used again.
- **Chapter 28's harm is small and deniable and is a *counterfeit with no fold in it*.** The card's "something small and visible" is delivered as: a carter who crosses on nine flat honest-looking lines because a paper that looks legal told him the crossing was his, and who loses three days. This is worse for the argument than a fold, because the counterfeit's technique has already been published, and it is what forces the fourth page to be a second page rather than a warning.
- **Chapter 30's strip-writers are Marda Osk, Tove Alard and Wenna Keel**, not Bett Ruhl, because Bett sets the limit that no address exists and the writers have to be people who can be reached. The exchange's bank is deliberately never stated, in the prose and in the bible, and the closing image is *the street under the tram line* rather than the tram apron so that nothing in the chapter pins it.
- **Only three Tally panels appear in ten chapters** (Chapters 24, 27 and 30), against one or more in most chapters of Batch 0002. Reason: the batch's event is institutional — records, prices, refusals, a public order — and a panel in every chapter would have made the object look like the narrator. The three are the spill, the feed pump, and the refusal to certify, and none of them solves a scene. **The Chapter 30 panel is read off an open page of the public lot book rather than off the brass, which is a constrained extension of the Tally's existing rule that a reader may be answered by touching or naming a record he has handled; it is declared and limited in `bible/power-system.md` and must not be widened.**
- **Length.** These ten chapters run 4,000–5,400 words, against 3,500–5,900 in Batch 0002. The batch is 45,200 words. The two institutional set-pieces (25 and 26) and the two physical ones (23 and 30) are the long ones and none of them can be cut without removing the material Chapters 31–40 have to argue with. Flagged, as in the previous two batches, for a reviewer.
