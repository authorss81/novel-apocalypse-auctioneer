# Volume 02 Batch 0001 Summary — The Borrowed Road (Chapters 51–60)

## Batch outcome

Chapters 51–60 open Volume 02 and stop a railway. **There is no crossing, no charter, no closing and no activation in this batch, and the object is stopped by a board of four seats with no owner.** What the batch does is find a second dangerous object that nobody sold anything on, work out what its toll is, discover that the toll lands on a place, and find out that **every instrument Alder Reach built in five weeks points at a person or an object and cannot point at a place.**

The batch's cost is not a life. It is: a village of thirty-one people that has lost the ability to account for a single departure and has never been asked; a man of forty who has a brother and cannot feel that the brother went; a count of seventeen that was correct and was published and was wrong within four hours; a decision not to read, taken in front of about five hundred people on a day the reading was permitted; a needle of an office that has just been made unable to be silent; a rate raised from forty-one to fifty coppers; a lease that expires in the fourth month of next year and has not been told to anybody; a train on a bank; and the first date this district has ever given anybody, with the words *this is a date a person could be wrong about* printed on the face of the sheet.

## The date map, and the month turn

**The month turns between Chapter 50 and Chapter 51, and the new month is March.** Last month is February, the month before last is January. Chapter 51 the first and second; 52 the third, fourth and fifth; 53 the fifth; 54 the seventh; 55 the ninth; 56 the eleventh (walk out and read) and the twelfth (entered); 57 the thirteenth; 58 the fifteenth and sixteenth; 59 the eighteenth; 60 the twenty-first and the twenty-second at the counter. **Batch 0002 opens on or after the twenty-second and the walk of the twenty-ninth falls inside it.** **The flood year is the current year**: the west end of the Needle went under nine feet in the flood, Harrow has been dead since, and every reversal and every line-book figure counted "since the water came" is roughly nine months. The Harrow interchange was under about two feet in the fourth year, which is when the Tongue came out of it — and the Tongue has been in use **thirteen** years, so fourth year plus thirteen is the present year, and five of those thirteen are in the hut at the ninth place and eight are in eleven huts, three sheds and a box in a wagon. The four drafts of the standing order run the third year, the fifth, the eighth, and a fair copy in the tenth: **seven years, and Selik's tenure is nine years — do not conflate them. The fourth year belongs to the Tongue and to the drafts' neighbourhood and to nothing else on the line; the siding lease was signed in the ninth year.**

## Beats completed

- **Chapter 51 — The Third of the Month (Nessa Pell).** The custody key's first expiry is watched and the standing instruction nearly fails: **nobody had named anybody**, Hester Vane refuses in a yard of about sixty to name her own successor and gives Tarin's sentence back ("a key is a smaller road"), names a cooper's apprentice and withdraws it because he is fifteen, and at ten at night on the second says out loud what happens if nobody is named: **nobody holds the key in the morning, the case stays shut, the object stays on a lapsed term, that is not a theft and it is a lapse, and it is published.** The turn comes from Bett Ruhl on a doorstep: **the book needs a name, and a mark may not carry a job, and the woman who should hold it has never been asked whether she has a name she wants in one.** The **ward market's second clerk** — six years at a counter, in no book because nobody ever offered to enter one — is asked, and **Wren Ostler** gives her name with three conditions and one disclaimer (a name is a promise to turn up, and being late is to be entered as late and not as failure; and her name is not the road). The term is shortened from seven days to **four** with the reason entered: *a rule that has been kept once is a rule, and one that has never been kept is a habit.* Selik argues that a term checked by one board is a board and not a term, and Nessa answers it. The chapter closes with **Orren Pike asking whether the public lot book has ever had a place in it**, and Nessa entering the question as asked and not answered and sleeving the paper at **411** (a page number, and the joke is that Orren does not correct her).
- **Chapter 52 — A Place on the Stair (Fen Ambrose).** One river-stair day, physically: 261 steps, a turn at 140, fog at about eight feet, a kink at 190, two lamps. **A count can be taken on a voice and not on a figure.** The plan's asking step has no hour in it, and so nobody asked, and **a place travelled and a person did not**: a woman of about sixty-one in a household had her place on the roll and did not come up, and stood on a ramp all night. **Seventeen was published in the morning, whether it closed or not, and it was right when it was published and wrong within four hours, and the reason is underneath it and the reason is not a mistake.** The finding: **a step in a plan that asks somebody a question is not a step until it has an hour and a person.** Bett Ruhl reads it aloud and stops for four seconds at *which is a person the rate has never met*, and says privately, to a cook, *that is me.* The three-column money: **44 / NO SHEET / 58**, the middle column empty because the two blocks west of the depot are in no ward. The second lamp's eleven coppers a week is **unpaid and not nil** and Bett Ruhl distinguishes an instrument from a service. The chapter's hook comes from a goods-shed floor: **the water on the far bank comes nine miles west on the Needle from a place called Sarrow Water, and its column has been metered to a road house in Alder Reach for as long as anybody can remember, and nobody in this district has asked anybody at Sarrow Water anything.**
- **Chapter 53 — The Room With a Door On It (Selik Marne).** He goes up the water office staircase four minutes early with a book, having arranged his whole professional life for nine years not to go into a room he knew about. He takes three people, two of them the wrong ones, and **adds Adrian Vale in his own hand with the time**, entering that the addition was his. The four drafts: third year, fifth, eighth, and a fair copy in the tenth that **none of them knew existed**; the office *is to lapse and is to be renewed by no body and is to be continued until the crossing ceases to be a crossing*; the fair copy's *for the duration of the need* with the need undefined; the closing clause that the office may close the crossing without a ground and that a safety measure is not subject to appeal, **which is Selik's own March sentence written out as a permanent power by four men without him**; and the last page, which **cannot be ended by any body and creates its own ending**. On the eleventh page, the schedule of **eight places with a duty against each** — the only page worth keeping, and the only document in nine years that is about a river. The name **CROSS-DISTRICT PASSAGE** appears four times as a pre-Fracture authority and is in no register. Selik withdraws it, publishes it, has a plate taken off the door, and is refused his own instruction by **Ilse Markham** in one sentence — *then it is a paper a man took back, and in four years the district will believe a thing was never written* — and switches to publication. On the second landing the second fitter, who suspended his certificate and is not a Warden, tells him what she thinks of the fourth page and says it in fifty seconds, and it becomes the front of the order, and nobody in Alder Reach ever learns who wrote it.
- **Chapter 54 — The Hut at Sarrow Water (Sef Anyard).** The line, the hut, the four things in a hut, the water that belongs to a road house. **Nineteen reversals, all nineteen since the water came, and the first two she did alone.** What a reversal costs, in a keeper's words: the train comes back and knows the way and not the place, and then the wanting to know goes, because wanting is a thing you have. The eleventh reversal produces a young man's sentence: **I know it was us. I could not have told you it was us from here. Those are two different things.** Eleven stalls on the line and a twelfth in her hut floor she has been certain of for eight years and has never entered, because entering it would mean being asked about it. The **origin-held column** with four entries that are not places, one of them spelled out in full: **THE STANDING OFFICE OF CROSS-DISTRICT PASSAGE, ESTABLISHED, AND NOT AT THIS PLACE.** Orren's four and hers are not the same four, so there is a fifth in a column of sixty-one the line book has not got. The train has been on the siding **twenty-four days**, and **the siding is not a place a train is kept, it is a place the line stops.** The chapter's last four minutes: she stops answering him because he has asked about the lever for two hours and a half without asking whether she wishes to speak, and **the number that was said first was twelve, and his book did not have it, and both are in the book and neither is struck.**
- **Chapter 55 — Nine Miles of Line (Tarin Kest).** He tells them about the lease on the ballast at half past nine and nobody says anything for two miles. **It is a nine-year lease signed in the ninth year, it expires in the fourth month of next year, and he has not renewed it and has not told the other side, and he has not told the woman at the column either, and he is not going to renew it.** The arithmetic: a line is an order and not a length; the train cannot come east without being turned; the origin of a train on a siding is the siding; **a stall gets longer when a train goes through it** (344 yards on the ninth, 361 on the eighteenth, on an afternoon when nothing went through it, and nobody can say which number is the lie). A train goes through the stall in front of them and Osgood Trill's four-line answer from the cab: **I can tell you what I came out of, which is a place with a lamp on it, and I cannot tell you what I came out of. Not the lamp. The stretch. And I would like it noted that I am not frightened, because I am the man who drives it and I have a job. And I would like it noted that I did not stop.** Selik's fourth term comes off the ballast and **does not survive the first curve**, and the tenth hand — the man permitted to argue it, having argued it once — writes the finding: *a rule that transfers is a habit with a new address.* At the ninth place a woman of fifty tells Tarin he is the fourth of four this year and asks the only question she has: **is it going to be moved in the night?** He says he does not know and says what he does, including that he signed the paper eight years ago.
- **Chapter 56 — The Tongue of Harrow (Adrian Vale).** He has not read since the **eighteenth of last month**, and the interval between that reading and this one is **twenty-three days, and it is not the point of the chapter.** The reading is entered from a sheet made at a kitchen table nine miles west, entered at Lot 17 on the twelfth by **two people comparing the hand against his hand**, with the sleeve number on the sheet. **The ledger for the new month opens at one: the eleventh, one hour, and the reader is not to read again before the eighteenth.** Use: one vehicle back along the section it came from, one movement, one way, the use is not given back. **Toll: the origin.** Standing: a person who can hold a signal. And the finding: **the object does not hold places, the toll is not stated in a form that can be read at a person, or a household, or a town, or a water, and the object does not say what the origin is, and asked for more it repeats itself.** **Three of Adrian's four candidate readings, and a fourth that comes off the floor from a carter of fifty who is worse than all three of his.** Adrian refuses to choose in a yard of about six hundred because choosing which place is charged is a decision, not a reading. The satchel is refused three times in six minutes: a custody rule nobody has written, the fact that the tongue is the only thing that throws the points **so the siding is the end of the line**, and **it is in daily use by a person who is in no book.** The new category: **a reading with no lot.** The eighth line is found to be about a bus and not about everything, and then the tenth hand's question: **if a person is carried as a person, what does a person pay when a train is turned round?** Answer: nothing. **The passengers are free and the place is charged.** The echo: for about fifty minutes he could not say which way the river went, and Sef Anyard says she has stood where he stood and cannot tell either, and did not want to be asked. At the door he asks the question he should have asked on the seventh, and **she asks for somebody who is not a reader, on the fifteenth.**
- **Chapter 57 — The Word for a Train (Ivo Stenn).** The nine coppers come back to his own fourth column **because a date has arrived.** Nine coppers a quarter for a bushel of chalk and a lamp at the ninth place; the refusal at St. Orra stands and the reason has not changed. The rate goes from **forty-one to fifty** in five columns. The chandler objects and Stenn enters the reason before the vote; **the hauliers put their hand up four minutes after the objection because the ninth place is where they park their stock and the lease that caused it was signed by a man who is not in the room.** One abstention, entered. Five words proposed for scarcity and none adopted, and the finding: **the scarcer direction is the one going west and there is nobody at the west end of it, and the district spent a month failing to name a queue for a hole.** Halloway Reed's timetable, with **the toll in a column so a woman who cannot read a sentence can compare two**, honest on its face, and Stenn's own verdict on him. Rutt's counter breaks in public on the thirteenth: **she can read a claim and she cannot read an absence**, and the woman with the stick asks what is true. Stenn takes on the rate sheet for ground nobody has claimed and says he will have to walk onto it and count households, and does not like it.
- **Chapter 58 — The Village That Is Not a Stop (Teo).** Rutt and a boy of fifteen walk out on the fifteenth with a bag of the ward market's own blank claim stock, because a sheet of paper with nothing on it is a thing you can hand to a woman. Rutt reads the origin column aloud on a step to fourteen people, and by the end there are thirty-one and a dog. **Nine hundred and six movements indexed; one hundred and forty-one name this place as the origin held; the line has nine places with platforms and this one has had no passenger stop in nine years; the reason is that almost everything on the line stands here, because the west end is dead and the Alder Reach yards are full.** Teo asks *where do the trains come from* and then the real question. **Asked of thirty-one: answered by twenty-three, not asked by eight, and the count of yeses is one, and the one walked.** Twenty-three reasons are printed at length, four of them the same: **I have a brother and I cannot feel that he went** — Bev Tollard's, put in his words or not at all. Rutt takes the count and it is **four houses and a chapel, because the form asks for a household**, and the woman of sixty-one says: **we have never once been a household, we are a place, and you cannot lose what was never entered, and that is not a mercy, that is a hole with a shape of our own making.** A boy of nine asks Sef Anyard what Harrow was like before, and the answer is a barrow and a good afternoon, and it is the only local knowledge in the book. **Teo says the word for himself, a third time, is given no reason, and Sef tells him what it cost: a boy of fifteen who cannot be asked to do a thing for a year.** The chapter closes with the second half of his old question, which nobody asked for, and Nessa refusing to put a question in the book and putting the count, the reasons, and the nine words in it instead.
- **Chapter 59 — A Destination Can Be a Victim (Adrian Vale).** About five hundred people, three sheets on the wall, and a trader asks what happens **today.** Adrian refuses to read, in public, in his own words, and the non-event is entered with no cost and no time. **Three instruments fail in public and all three fail correctly.** The proposal schedule is for a use somebody is proposing, and the toll has been taken nineteen times since the water came. The no-name remedy is a remedy for a breach, and **there is no charter, therefore no breach, therefore nothing to remedy — the nineteen takings were the correct operation of an object nobody has ever bought.** The count rule, which is the district's best writing, **turned a village into a household and made thirty-one people invisible in the week it was written.** And the admission: **a destination can be a victim too**, plus the harder one — *I have been saying the charge is the person for five weeks and it is a rule about roads I built and not a law, and I did not know what else to charge and I did not ask.* Then a trader says the word on behalf of Sarrow Water, and Adrian stops him, because **the only act this district has made holy is the act that would put nine vehicles on a village's wall** — a refusal has never been costed, and every instrument assumes saying no is free. Bett Ruhl claims a sixth line for a kitchen wall. The payment: **1,190 / NO SHEET / 1,430**, as a payment and not a security, a reader cannot post security, the shortfall uncomputed and not nil, and the market told before the money moves. Selik says the safest thing available is to close the west end, which strands the train on the bank for ever, and that being right is the thing he has never been able to explain. The route-keeper remedy — written in Volume 01 and never once attempted — **is attempted, and is discharged in eleven minutes for the nine vehicles and not discharged for thirty-one people.** The board publishes a result stopping the lot **until the place its toll names has been asked**, and Adrian asks for it to be stopped by the board and is refused once and accepted the second time in the same minute.
- **Chapter 60 — A Result, Published (Mara Quill).** Two hours and fifty minutes, all four seats named aloud, **eleven** parts, the registrar's numbering, read at the counter and then in the yard by Bett Ruhl. Part nine is the two documents and the name **CROSS-DISTRICT PASSAGE** in two lines, not joined, at the tenth hand's asking, with the reason entered. Part ten is the **remote custody** — a keeper named and not an officer, asked and asked again on the twenty-ninth, the condition published in full, no case, no counter, no key, the keeper's term and not the board's, and the whole entry entered as deliberately worse than a case. Part eleven is **what this district has no instrument for**, five lines, four names, and the fifth with nobody's name on it. Then the **first date this district has ever given anybody**: the twenty-ninth, the west end walked by four people — **Tarin Kest, the tenth hand, Ivo Stenn, and a woman from the ninth place who has not been asked what she is going for** — and she is asked in the yard, in front of four hundred people, and dictates the clause, and it is on the sheet. **Selik is not on the walk and is not put on it; he said on the eighteenth that he will not be the man who does it, and Adrian is not on it either, because the sheet says the twelfth stall is to be measured by somebody who is not the reader.** Halloway sells two hundred and six places on the walk at four coppers, honestly, before the yard empties, and Stenn puts the date of that in a column. **The four days nobody held the key at all** — the seventh to the eleventh, entered as a lapse and not a theft — is entered in the same breath as the rotation, because a rotation is only real when it turns. **Mrs Halder** is named in the book on the fifteenth, holds the key from the seventeenth for four days, and attaches a condition: she will not open the case alone and wants a second person every time and cannot get one, and the board has no power to give her one, and it is entered as a standing failure. Selik says that is not enough. The last entry: the twelfth stall unmeasured since the seventh, the train on the bank, the toll the origin, the origin a place, the place asked one question by one woman, nobody paid, the rate sheet not existing, the standing order on the wall and unanswered, the name in two lines, and **the book has a hole in it and the hole is the shape of a place and the hole is dated.**

## The numbers, closed

- **The key.** Hester Vane from the twenty-sixth of February for **six days**, expiring midnight on the third. **The standing instruction nearly fails and the term is shortened to four days.** Wren Ostler named on the second, key handed over at ten past twelve on the second, four days, expiring midnight on the **sixth**, renewal to be on the fifth. **THE LAPSE: the board named nobody on the fifth or the sixth, so the seventh to the eleventh had no holder at all.** The case stood shut in a locked office on a term that had run out, the object sat on a lapsed term for four days, and it is entered **as a lapse and not as a theft** — nobody took anything and nobody was in a position to take anything. **It is the first lapse in the history of the public lot book and the first thing the standing instruction has ever actually cost anybody**, and it is exactly what Hester Vane predicted at the ward market counter on the second. Wren Ostler again from the **twelfth**, four days, expiring midnight on the **sixteenth**, renewed on the fifteenth. **Mrs Halder named on the fifteenth, holds from the seventeenth, four days**, and will not hold it alone. A term of four days is the shortest this board has ever set.
- **The line.** Twelve places in the near section. **Twelve stalls on the keeper's count and eleven on the tenth hand's, and the difference is one stall entered twice in a column of eleven: ten on the line, plus the floor in the hut at the ninth place. The number said first by the keeper was twelve and the reader's book had eleven, and both are in the book and neither is struck.** The stall at the fourth place: **344 yards on the ninth, 361 on the eighteenth.** Nineteen reversals since the water came, eleven of which left a stall in the book. **Nine vehicles on the siding since the eleventh of last month: twenty-four days on the seventh, twenty-six on the ninth, thirty on the thirteenth, forty-six on the twenty-ninth. Derive it; do not carry a figure forward.**
- **The books.** Sef Anyard's hut book: **61 movements** since the water came, four in a second ink. The line book: **906 movements** indexed, of which **141 name Sarrow Water as the origin held**; the tenth hand has **211** of the 906 indexed. The origin column has **four entries that are not places** in Sef's book and **four in Orren's, and they are not the same four**, so there is a fifth somewhere the line book has not got.
- **The village.** **Thirty-one people, four houses occupied 11 / 7 / 9 / 4, a chapel with a bell that is not rung.** Counted as four households and a chapel. **Twenty-nine** people worked the line in the first year, not thirty-one. Departures asked of thirty-one: **answered by twenty-three, not asked by eight, and the count of yeses is one**, and the one walked over the footbridge years ago. Reasons printed at length; four of the twenty-three are the same nine words.
- **The stair.** **Seventeen up, published the fourth, right when published and wrong within four hours.** Added to on the fifth: **eighteen**, with the authority named as a plan with an hour in it. **The finding is that the count was right in number and wrong in identity: one of the seventeen lines on the roll stood for a house with two people in it, and the other one of them was at the top of a ramp all night.** Fog at about eight feet; a count taken on a voice; 261 steps; the turn at 140, where the wall is nine feet, and four feet at the top, where the lamp-hook is; two lamps; the second lamp **eleven coppers a week, unpaid and not nil**. Money: **44 / NO SHEET / 58.**
- **The money.** The paper levy **fifty coppers a quarter** over **four thousand and one households**, in five columns — nineteen inspection, eleven reader, six boards, two oil, **nine line**, three unassigned with the reason entered as the cost of a sheet nobody has bought. One trade abstained. Rutt is on **eleven coppers a quarter for four days a week** and it is not what a day is worth. The payment on the unstated claim: **1,190 / NO SHEET / 1,430**, entered as a payment, not a security, reader cannot post security, shortfall **uncomputed and not nil**, market told first. **1,190 coppers is about six-tenths of one per cent of the quarter's take, and about twenty-seven days of the river stair** — do not describe it as a share of the levy. **Joss Amery's figures and the twenty-ninth's payment of the month before last are unchanged and were not restated.**
- **The counterfeit.** Halloway Reed's timetable, four columns, the toll in a column, printed on the same blank claim stock, honest on its face, **the news of the walk out and about two hundred and six places at four coppers before the yard emptied.** He is a step ahead by about a day and a half. The earlier reading of the month's claims is unchanged: nine in a night, five honest.
- **The reading ledger, new month.** **One reading: the eleventh, one hour, at a table nine miles west, entered from a sheet, compared by two people.** Limit: not before the eighteenth. **On the eighteenth he does not read, on a day the reading was permitted, and the interval since the eleventh is six days, and the non-event is entered with no cost and no time.** The ledger for the month closes at one.
- **The board's five powers**, in the order painted on the wall in Chapter 51: refuse to record a closing; **publish a result**; call a review; uphold a challenge in part; set or renew a short public custody term. And it cannot name a person. **Publish a result is the second of the five, not the fifth.**
- **The count rule's authorship.** **The four lines are Adrian's.** He wrote them at a table in a kitchen under the Ward Nine tram line in the second week of last month, with a candle on the table, and a yard of about six hundred agreed to them in the rain. Fen Ambrose wrote the *form* that uses them. Bett Ruhl found out what was wrong with them on a bank nine miles west, standing in the rain, on the fifteenth. **No woman on a river stair fought for the rule against a room, and no woman of seventy owns a wall at the ninth place; the man of seventy is the fitter's father-in-law and the column woman is fifty.**

## Relationship movement

- **Adrian and Mara.** Nine chapters further apart in law and closer in practice, and still **not one word said about it in public or otherwise.** She puts his payment in front of the market before the money moves; he is at the back with a notebook he did not write in; she hears him say the date is a bad answer and the only one available and **says nothing at all.** Her chapter is a list of five instruments that do not exist, four of which now have a name against them.
- **Adrian and Tarin.** Tarin says the lease out loud on his own ballast at half past nine, before they are two miles out, and nobody says anything for two miles, and **that silence is the scene.** He has not renewed it, has not told the other side, has not told the woman on the column, and is not going to renew it, and his reason is not conscience: *the day I renew it is the day a village becomes a thing in a road house's business, and the day I stop is the day a train on a siding is a thing on a piece of somebody's ground.* At the column he says the harder half — that he signed the paper, that this place was not asked in any of the nineteen takings, and that he has been carrying it for eight years. **Nobody accuses him and he is not let off.** Adrian does not console him and does not defend him; he asks whether the Road House gets a man on a reopened span and Tarin says **no, in advance, in his own words, and would like it in the book that he said it before a train had been late.**
- **Adrian and Selik.** The best two pages in the batch. Selik reads the paper, finds his own sentence in it, is refused his own instruction by his own clerk, publishes it, and asks a fitter to write the front page. Adrian refuses to read the draft for him and says why in a yard. Selik still says that is not enough and will go on saying it.
- **Adrian and Orren.** Orren indexes the line book, is paid nothing, notices that his four and Sef's four are not the same four, walks four miles in the dark with a man who cannot tell which way east is, and tells Adrian his mother's name is not going in the book and is the only person in Alder Reach who does not know about it. **Adrian notices, and says so, and cannot get anywhere with it.**
- **Adrian and Sef Anyard.** He is wrong in her hut for two hours and a half and she stops answering. She tells him she could not tell which way the river went either and did not want to be asked. She asks for somebody who is not a reader, on the fifteenth, and gets one, and it is a boy of fifteen, and it is the district's whole method in one gesture.
- **Mara and Rutt.** The counter's limit is found in public and Rutt is put in the book for the instrument that would fix it, having been asked.
- **Nessa and Wren Ostler.** The first person in the district's history entered in a public book by choosing to be, with three conditions and a disclaimer.
- **Teo.** Says the word a third time, about himself, and is told exactly what it cost, and says *it's mine to pay.* Nobody thanks him. He has four questions of his own and is not a clerk and is not going to be Adrian's anything.

## Review-pass flags

- **Length.** 4,002–4,702 words per chapter, **43,306 in the batch**, against Volume 01's 45,200–48,588 per batch and a 4,000–5,400 target. Chapter 58 is the short one and cannot lose the count; Chapter 59 is the long one and cannot lose the trader's refusal.
- **Panels.** **One System panel in ten chapters**, in Chapter 56, and it is the only one in the batch. Every other blockquote in the batch is a page in a public book or a clause read off a board. The panel is a refusal and it refuses to name a place, and it solves nothing.
- **Institution density.** The Volume 01 review named this as the volume's real risk and Volume 02's first batch is the correction. **Six of the ten chapters are substantially physical**: a river stair in fog, a walk out along a viaduct and an embankment, a hut, a walk back along nine miles of line in the wet, a train going through a stall thirty feet from a man's boot, and a market counting room. The four document-heavy chapters are 53, 57, 59 and 60, and 60 is two hours and fifty minutes of one registrar reading aloud, which is the medium rather than the subject.
- **Tics.** *in the yard* 16, against 23 in Volume 01's last batch. *I would like* **11**, against 13. *on the rail* **0** — the phrase is not available on a railway and was not forced. *in the book* 51 and *which is* 63 are structural to the register and are not flagged.
