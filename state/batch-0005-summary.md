# Batch 0005 Summary — The First Hammer (Volume 01 close)

## Batch outcome

Chapters 41–50 open the route. The St. Orra crossing was **sold, performed and closed** on the eighteenth of this month in front of about nine hundred people, with one bearer, one activation, one term of twelve minutes, **twenty-four travellers, twenty-six offered, thirty-one tolls, and seventeen people left on the far bank.** The token is cracked through and parted at the rim. **Volume 01 is complete.** The volume's last genuinely new act is Chapter 44: somebody asks the brass a question, in public, as a question, with its own limits written into the book first. ## Beats completed

- **Chapter 41 — The Day the Ward Market Votes (Ivo Stenn).** The rate is put to a room: **forty-one coppers a quarter** out of a levy of four that has produced no printed matter for eleven years, in columns — nineteen to the warden's inspection, eleven to a public reader at the counter, six to the boards and the appointed clerk's board, two to oil, and **three unassigned with the reason entered**, because a rate that pays for a thing nobody can name is the rate the market has been charging all along. **Mrs Kraus's motion is now the ward market's own motion**: the money is committed by the day and the market pays toward no office that has no day on it. **A trader asks what happens to a held asset if the charter is never breached, and the answer is that it comes back, and nobody in this district has ever put anything back.** Stenn is asked by eleven trades to fund nine coppers more for a standing crew at St. Orra and **refuses in public, with figures, and says the whole of it**: the crossing has no day, a rate paid for a thing with no day is this market's own paper levy with a different word on it, **and we may be wrong, and if we are wrong on a day we did not plan for, then four people will stand at St. Orra with a lamp and tell somebody they cannot help them, and that will be our doing and not the Warden's.** The market votes the inspection and the reader and refuses the third thing, and it refuses it on **eleven trades and four thousand and one households**, and the whole of it takes about four minutes. **Selik's review of his own order sits the same afternoon, in the same room, in front of the market that is paying for his inspection, and the finding — written by the traders, not by his office — is that the order is correct and the district does not want it.** He says both halves out loud in nine minutes and does not enjoy either. **Stenn's discovery:** the ward market can say yes to a room and not to a road, and he has no idea what to do with that. The chapter ends with the market's second clerk reading a paragraph of Adrian's held-asset entry aloud, twice, to nine people at a counter, because *not returnable to Mr. Vale by anybody* is the first piece of news this city has had in three weeks.
- **Chapter 42 — What the Job Is (Fen Ambrose).** Three hours down the river stair and three back, to a goods-shed floor with eleven people and a dog on it. She tells the man at the chain, in words, at length, what the far-side holder is: **every person who comes off that vehicle is somebody's and not a stranger's; a decline is a thing the count carries; and you do not go and look for anybody, and if the bus does not come back you do not go and find out why.** The job is a job where the whole of the work is standing still and counting and the whole of the danger is in a man who cannot stand still. **He says yes, on two conditions, and neither of them is about himself:** the count goes in the book **with the four in it**, and **nobody asks her again, ever.** The cost is named out loud in front of eleven people: the count becomes a number that **cannot be checked by asking**, because every count in this district is checked by asking and this one can only be checked by a man with a lamp counting heads, and he counts heads and not people, and **it is the only count in this city with no way to be found wrong aloud.** The mark is **retired** — the cross filed off the head of the pin in six minutes, the ring kept in the strip-book, the pin put back on the rail with nothing on it, because a mark is a refusal and a refusal is permanent and what came off it was a job and nothing else — and Nessa Pell reads the index entry aloud in the yard on the fifteenth, twice, and a third time because Bett Ruhl asked. And the chapter's discovery is arithmetic: **a rotation of three on foot from the far ramp to the near apron does nothing.** It cannot fetch anybody, it cannot search, and if it sets off before the bus is late the holder is not on the floor when the vehicle comes. The charter must therefore say that a party cut off is **held, counted, and not sent anywhere**, and the crew of three is a count and not a rescue. The far-side responsible party is left open and goes up the stair as an unsolved thing with Fen's name on it.
- **Chapter 43 — The Sale (Adrian Vale).** The board sits its own called review on the fifteenth at nine, **sustains the seven items and the eighth line, refuses the fourth entry, and does not sustain item three's last sentence** — a term about a place, the second finding on the same item, and the board's third reason is that a rule it cannot write is a rule it will still publish. Then the eighth line meets the clinic. **Adrian puts four ways of doing it on the crate and refuses to choose**, because the man who wrote two of them must not be the man who picks the third. **Marda Osk says the four things are three failures and one solution. Nessa Pell says the toll has always been charged to a person and never to a thing, and a woman with a bag is not a carriage.** The yard finds the reading and it is costed before it is agreed to: **six of eight clinic hands each carry one box; a person carrying a thing cannot be the remembering passenger, cannot be the far-side responsible party, cannot stop to write, cannot be handed the box by anybody else, and if they put it down it is theirs; and the six-hour clock starts when a box comes off the cart, and the boxes come off the cart in front of four hundred people, and the clock is already running.** The lot is described: one route, twelve minutes, one bearer, one activation, a toll read aloud twice in the words the travellers will hear, and **no bidding, because there is nothing to bid for and a bid for a place on a bus is a claim upon a successful crossing, which is a thing that does not exist.** Then the refusal window opens and **stays open all night**, and at about five o'clock **Teo says the word** — the boy who wrote the complaint and the open question — and eleven minutes later the girl of eleven who refused on the nineteenth and was never told the toll says it again. **Twenty-six offered, twenty-four travelling, and the sale does not stop.** That is the first time in this book that a refusal has not stopped a room.
- **Chapter 44 — The Question Nobody Asked (Mara Quill).** A charter's terms are agreed by people and the anchor is not a party, and in eight years and nine books Mara Quill has never written a clause that says *and the object was asked*. Orren Pike arrives with a sheet of eleven lines and says the shortest thing on it. There is no procedure, and the reason there is not one is Fen's: **asking the object is not asking a person, and a record kept of the object is a record nobody in the room is protected from.** The yard builds the instrument in public, the way it built the terms-closing — **and it writes the limit first**, in capitals, before the question: one question, asked once, the object not asked to agree to anything, and no second question until there is a new closing. Two fresh witnesses: **Rutt**, who stopped reading aloud on the sixth of this month, and **Mrs Halder**, who found the levy and asked what happens if the answer is a lie. Eight candidate questions are refused on the record — will it open, how many more times, what is the second toll, whose fault, the bell-post arm, the order with no expiry — and the ninth is the only one left. Adrian puts it, says in advance that he expects nothing and says what he wants to get, and **the answer's third line is that six replies were given without a question and none of them was a promise.** The cost is nineteen minutes and an hour of not being able to tell whether a thing is nearer or further than it was. **The board refuses to let it be a revelation** — a reply given without a question was never a promise and nobody signed anything on one — and enters that the defence is good and thin. **Sabin Tull answers the other half: a forged second page is not a promise either, and about two hundred people in this district have bought something this month on the strength of it.** Mara enters her own fear in her own hand, and signs a record she cannot certify in advance.
- **Chapter 45 — The Staging Line (Tarin Kest).** Four hundred yards of chalk and four hundred people and a boy of five asleep on a coat, because **a road is a thing people bring children to see** and that is the best public notice anybody has written in five weeks. The tenth hand of the signal crew brings his page and finds the first number in five weeks he cannot put on it: **twenty-six with two noes in it is not the same kind of thing as a number somebody said out loud.** Tarin then extends **Selik Marne's far-side condition to everyone on the route who is not driving it**, because a charter with one condition in it and twenty-three people who have none is a charter about one man — and **Selik accepts, and adds three terms of his own**: a line-holder named in the book, a duty to tell people the ballast is not ground, and **nobody past the second rail**, with the **twelfth of this month entered as the day that rule was argued with and held**, by the tenth hand, in a yard, and not moved. The yard fails on a word — *urgent* eleven, *scheduled* seven, *priority* four, all entered, none adopted — and **Renn Fisk declines the word and gives a clock: six hours and forty minutes, in chalk, on six lids.** And at ten past seven in the morning Tarin says the thing that is the cost of being the bearer, out loud, on a line, to three hundred people, rather than at nine the next morning to nine hundred: **the charter is to be performed with a person at the far end of it who has not been asked, and the only reason they are entitled to is a sheet of paper.** The line is walked. The count is read at the near end: **twenty-six offered, twenty-four travelling, two declines**, and Nessa Pell says the number that has never been said aloud in a yard in this city is twenty-six, because it has a no in it. The refusal window stays open all day and all night and closes on the eighteenth when the case is physically opened.
- **Chapter 46 — The Auction (Adrian Vale).** The release condition is read at the opening of the case by Nessa Pell, in capitals, and the sentence that is repeated afterwards is *a returned object is not a returned vehicle, and it is not a rescue, and it is not evidence that anybody is alive*. **Halloway Reed sells on the tram arch, in daylight, in front of nine hundred people, and does not come into the yard**: a claim on the **next** closing, eleven coppers, the toll on page one, the fifteen-day labour term on page three, and the sentence *your public road is going to fail and on the morning it fails the only thing anybody can buy is speed, and I sell speed with the toll printed on the front*. He is better, he is right, and **forty-one places sell**. Adrian reads the sentence about a thing that does not exist and admits in public that he has no answer to the rest of it. The remedy is called and **calling it opens the sealed page, and what is behind it is a man and not a cause** — and the board enters that it is performing the closing with the condition unmet, in four reasons, the third of which is Rell Bode's sentence that **a condition is a condition and not a consent.** The witnesses are the tenth hand and **Ferrin Usk**, who accepts as a witness and not as a believer and who says he has not bought a place and will not buy one off a man whose first page he has not read. Adrian reads the token — an hour, the third reading this month — and the token's own reading contains *if it opens twice, the route may be stranded and nothing on this page says it cannot.* **The bus is the one that came out of a service yard on its own with nobody at the wheel, and the two facts are entered in two lines, on the tenth hand's demand, and the registrar has agreed to keep them in two lines and the book contains no document in which they are joined.** One charter, one bearer, one activation, one term with an end, closed at twenty to five. The vehicle goes out at five minutes past five with twenty-four people in it. The far-side holder writes **twenty-four** on a wall in chalk.
- **Chapter 47 — The Term (Adrian Vale).** Adrian stands on the apron with nothing to do for eleven minutes, which is the job. **A mother — Rosalind Hesk, whose daughter of eight is on the far bank and not on the return party — asks him, in the rain, to keep it open four minutes, because somebody told her in this yard at two o'clock that if he held it, the bus could go up and take more.** She did not know that holding it past the term closes the case, and he tells her, in four minutes, in front of nine hundred people, and apologises for the man who cost her the hope without finding him. **The sentence the chapter rests on is his: the limit is the only thing in this city that anybody can check.** **A chandler, Amos Treft, states in public that his cart of a hundred and seventy pounds of coal and eleven sacks of seed is not going over, and that he would have made a very good thing out of four minutes and is not calling anybody a robber.** Three columns, **212 / 186 / 240**, and under them the price of eleven households without coal on Monday, **uncomputed and not nil.** At about a quarter past five the object reports on the open page of the book, off the record and not off the brass: **it is failing before the term ends, the plate is parted at the rim, and it does not know whether the route will close at twelve minutes or before it.** The bus comes down onto the stamped plate. **Bett Ruhl reads the release condition at the closing, and cannot read, and says so first, and does not pretend to have understood the ninth line.** The count: **twenty-four outward and twenty-four tolls; - **Chapter 48 — What Is Left of the Hearth Line (Tarin Kest).** A holding, not a hearing, because a hearing is when you want to be proved right about something. **Three people try to treat a crossing that has been performed as a right, inside an hour** — a trader reselling a place for ninety coppers, a broker's clerk offering to broker a St. Orra season, and a man who got on the bus and came back and wants it written down that he did it — and the fifth of the five things stops all three in four minutes each, and the third one is the best of them: a man asking for something to be written down about himself who does not want to be a fool. Then **the river stairs' own arithmetic**, because the footbridge's numbers — five on the deck, twenty-two minutes, four usable hours — belong to a hundred and forty feet of plank that is in the service cut, and Tarin strikes them off the slate before he will use one. **One person at a time, about three hours down and three hours up, in daylight: seventeen is about two and a half days, and the last person on the stair at dusk is not accounted for until the morning of the third.** **The crew of three is a count and not a rescue.** And there is no marshal, because a Warden's man with a lamp on a stair is a manifest, and a manifest is what makes a queue — and the light is held by **Nessa Pell**, a clerk, who can see nine feet up a stair and cannot see the turn, and who says the count is published in the morning whether it closes or not because the only way anybody can check a number at the top of a three-hour stair is to walk up it themselves. **Joss Amery is paid**: three days, a barrow-load, three columns of **268 / 241 / 310**, the barrow-load uncomputed and not nil, the altered coal board entered as a fact joined to nothing at the request of two men who did not ask each other — and the reason the payment can be made at all is that **he can be found**, which is the only qualification he has. Tarin finds that the timetable he warned the district about has **got a committee**, and says out loud that he does not know whether that is better. And at ten past three, in nine minutes, six hundred people build the honest answer to *can it be sold*: **the thing can be used and cannot be owned; the use can be sold and cannot be kept; the end can be checked and cannot be argued with.** And then **the custody term has expired at midnight and nobody noticed for twelve hours**, and the board renews it in public in about four seconds, and Nessa Pell asks for a rotation the board has no power to create, and the minute says so, and the request stands.
- **Chapter 49 — The Damaged Token (Mara Quill).** **Ossie Prent stands up at eleven and makes the best objection anybody has made to anything in this book, and he is right**: a case is where a thing is, a sheet travels, and **the crack is the product, and a shape is the easiest thing in this city to print** — and Mara agrees with all of it and answers with one thing. **A damaged object in a public case is not evidence; it is a souvenir with a lamp on it.** Evidence is a thing connected to a claim, and Lot 17's claims are unstated and uncomputed, and a child is dead with no name in any book. So the object is entered as **a lot with a condition and not a relic**, and the condition is the whole of the entry: the crack through the plate, the chip out of the reverse, **two hundred and eleven grains before and two hundred and four after**, five openings, and *it is not known whether it will open again*. **Rell Bode's objection is about the drawer** and not about the remedy, and Mara's answer is that a drawer in a public room with a lamp in it and a rotation of keys and a reader at a counter is a shelf, and that **Rell Bode does not convert and says so.** The board publishes a result: no expiry in the entry and no end in it, a seven-day term renewed in public with the hour published, inspection on stated days by anybody, a rotation the board has been asked for and cannot create, and **the ward market's reader describing the object to anybody who turns up, at eleven coppers a quarter, which is what the market voted on the thirteenth and not what a day of it is worth, and that difference is the lie in her own line.** **Mara signs the custody page with the hand that is in the spill and says so before anybody asks, and about four hundred people do not look at her hand**, and the book records that this is the fifth time and the first time it has cost anybody anything.
- **Chapter 50 — The Book, Left Open (Adrian Vale, and the room).** The last read-back, in a kitchen under the Ward Nine tram line, with two people who are not clerks, two hours and six minutes, the eighteenth read twice, the toll read three times at Bett Ruhl's request, and Lina booking the autumn. Rutt reads the entry of the crossing at the counter and in the yard. Mara enters the foot of the page: the crossing, the tolls, the seventeen, the sealed page opened and the man **asked on the second of this month, told what the job was on the fourteenth, and not gone for on the day**, Hester Vane's key and the third of the new month, **the reading ledger closed** at five in the month of the crossing and four in this month, **the counterfeit still winning on speed**, the span still shut, and a fifth line in chalk under three lines and four lines in charcoal on a kitchen wall: ***THE ANSWER IS NO. ## New canon, written into the bible before use

Added to `bible/world.md` and `bible/terminology.md`:

- **A lot with a condition, and a custody that can be inspected.** Not evidence, not a relic — a lot, whose condition is the whole of the entry and may be measured and argued with. No expiry and no end in the entry; a short term renewed in public with the hour published; a key that rotates and a holder named before the key is held; inspectable on stated days; describable by a reader at a counter.
- **A question put to the object.** One question, once, by a person with no claim, with the limit written into the book first, the object not asked to agree to anything, the asker stating in advance what he expected. **A reply given without a question is not a promise.**
- **A count published with a decline in it.** A decline is a decline, not a hole; not asked is not asked; the offered number and the travelling number both stay; a count with a no in it is a different instrument from a count read aloud.
- **A cash-side reading of the market.** The rate voted on the thirteenth, in columns, with three unassigned coppers and a reason; the nine coppers refused with the reason *the thing has no day on it*; **a rate buys a service and does not buy a seat.**
- **A custody term that expires quietly.** The hour of a renewal is published in advance; a clerk's memory is not an instrument.
- **A reading that arrives on an open page mid-use.** A closing in progress writes on the open page of the public book, and a reader who has handled that page reads it as he reads any other record. Bounded, entered in the ledger in the ordinary way, and it told nobody anything they could act on except that the term was in danger.

## Review-pass flags

- **Length.** 3,903–5,661 words, **47,989 in the batch**, against 4,000–5,400 and 45,200 in Batch 0003 and 3,840–6,092 and 48,588 in Batch 0004. Chapter 49 is the short one; Chapter 46 is the long one and cannot lose the tram arch or the sealed page.
- **Panels, counted properly: there are four blocks, not three, and the earlier count was wrong.** Chapter 44's answer; **Chapter 46 twice** — the reading of the object at about half past eleven, and the three lines Adrian's hand put on the open page at about twenty past four; Chapter 47's failure report. **RULING on the Chapter 46 pair, which the review pass left open.** The rule in `AGENTS.md` is at most one *System panel* per chapter unless the story depends on more, and the two Chapter 46 blocks are not two System panels. **The first is the brass object speaking and is the only System panel in the chapter. The second is an entry in a public book, written by a man's hand on the page in front of nine hundred people, and it is set in the same block form because it is a page and a page should look like a page.** The two are separated by about five hours of story time and neither depends on the other. The prose has been left as it is. **The batch therefore carries three System panels in ten chapters** — one in Chapter 44, one in Chapter 46, one in Chapter 47 — against two in Batch 0004 and one or more in most of Batch 0003, and that frequency is defensible in a climax batch with the object in play for the first time in forty chapters. **It is still flagged**: none of the three solves a scene, one is a refusal, one is a warning the reader is told they cannot act on, and one recasts five weeks of evidence without naming anybody.
- **Institution density.** Chapters 41, 43, 45, 48, 49 and 50 are all rooms in which documents are argued about. **This is the batch's real risk and it is worse than Batch 0004's, and it is the first thing Volume 02 should vary.** What the batch did about it: Chapter 42 and Chapter 47 are physical, Chapter 45 is a walk of four hundred yards in the rain, and Chapter 50 opens in a kitchen.
