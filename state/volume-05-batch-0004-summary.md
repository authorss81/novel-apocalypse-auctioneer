## 1. What the block is

Ten chapters in ten days, the third to the twelfth of the fifth month, one chapter to a day, and no chapter carries two dates, which is the first block in this volume where that is true.

**A sheet came down that nobody put on the trestle table, and it carries a toll on a thing the Registry calls a gate, and this district spent five days in the first week of the fourth month getting that word back off a stranger's schedule and has not said it in a yard since.** The sheet sends one toll to each of four places by the roads below the water, paid by **the party of each of the four and by nobody else**, and the four are numbered and not named, and it prints the rule that **a place is the fourth of the four by itself until somebody says it is not.** It names four parties and not one person, and the finding of the block is that **a toll is a thing a named person pays under a term**, and that a party is not a person, and that a toll which cannot be fastened to a person cannot be collected and cannot be refused either, and that the record on all four is that nobody was asked. **This yard decided it was the fourth of the four without being asked, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud in front of about nineteen people that he was not going to be the man who decided it, so that if somebody did decide it there would be a line in the book saying a man noticed.**

**A lock came out of a channel at the sluice end of the loams on a barrow, about a hundred and ninety pounds, and up a bank in about eleven minutes, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against read it in front of about nineteen people and was wrong.** He said that what a lock holds with is the pin, and that the pin takes the load, and that a lock is a safe thing exactly as long as a man is near it. **The man of fifty-six put his own hand on the frame next to his and said the pin takes nothing, because he has been putting a stick through it to turn the screw with both hands in the water for nine years, about four thousand times, and if the pin took the load his hands would have gone under the water with the screw.** Four days later the same man read the notch in a sill on a bank four miles out past the loams and was wrong again, and this time the man who was right was a man of about forty-one who keeps a road house nine miles up, standing about four feet away with his thumb in the notch: **that is where the board goes, there is one notch in that sill and there has been one since before his father walked the road, and a mark that says where a board goes is not a mark that says how high the water got.** **Both errors were said out loud by the man who made them, in his own words, in front of witnesses, and a clerk entered each time that neither saying is a figure and neither is a column.**

**And a notice went under the leg of the trestle table, folded once, pressed into soft ground, and it names no place and no person and it identifies this district's records office by what it does and where its books are.** The registrar of that office was asked in the six things in front of about nineteen people and answered in a hundred and ten words, and the only line in the notice that has anything to do with her is her own rule of the twenty-first of the fourth month, and she wrote it before she knew there was anything on it, and it is the only rule anybody in this district has ever written down and enforced, **and it is also the only reason that notice could be written.** She said she cannot stand in a yard and use this yard's own rule about a name put on a figure, because nobody has put her name on anything, **and that what is on the notice is her room and her books and the fact that she is the only one who may look in them, and all three of those are true and she wrote two of them herself.** The notice takes effect on the first of the next month.
