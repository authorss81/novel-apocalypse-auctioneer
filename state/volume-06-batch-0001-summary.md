## 1. The position the block opened on

**The twenty-second of the fifth month of the eighteenth year after the Long Fracture is the last day of Volume 05's prose, and the first of the sixth month is ten days after it, and on that day a record set against four places four hundred miles away became IN FORCE.** The district had read that record out seven times in seventeen days in a yard a stranger can walk into, had made it its own twice over without anybody deciding to, and had not answered it and was not going to answer it by carrying a page out of a registrar's own office.

**The starting figures, all derived and none carried: board 159. Train 465 days, arrived on the eleventh of the second month of the seventeenth year and counted across the year boundary. Unentered days 179 since the twenty-fourth of November. A hundred and forty days from the second of January, which is twenty weeks and no days.** The chair at the end of the trestle table had stood twenty-nine days. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally had not moved for thirty-one days.

**The calendar was about to turn its meaning. Until the first of the sixth month, *last month* in this district meant the fourth month and *the month before last* meant the third. From the first of the sixth month, *last month* means the fifth, *the month before last* means the fourth, and the third month is two months back and must be named that way or by its own name.** The pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month and became two months back on the morning this block opens.

**And on that same morning, about a quarter to twelve, about nine people standing about nine feet off a gatepost of the yard of Lot Seventeen were looking at the back of a sheet of paper about the size of a sheet of paper with a fresh nail in it and no mud on the nail.**

**Spent by the plan this phase wrote before it wrote a word:** the first day a rival record was in force and unanswered; the change in the meaning of a relative month; a printed sheet carrying one number and one day; a woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route coming back down four miles and refusing a public instrument in a doorway; a second book in the room where the books are; a building with two doors at the near end of the lane behind the bank; a room about two miles the other way with nothing over the door; a line of print naming a keeper whose column is empty; a bell in an oak frame four miles out past the loams; a building eleven miles off seen once from a rise; the difference between a number made by knocking and a number made by walking.
