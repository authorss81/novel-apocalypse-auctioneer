# Volume 01 Summary — The First Hammer (Chapters 1–50, complete)

## The volume in one paragraph

A brass transit token recovered from a stranded bus can open one route across the St. Orra Fault for twelve minutes and takes the memory of why each traveller is travelling. Adrian Vale, a practical auctioneer who has spent seventeen years turning uncertainty into a figure, tries to sell that use as a public service instead of a private road. It takes forty chapters, a dead child with no name in any public book, a man with a turned ankle who cannot be entered in a public record, a bus that came out of a Fault with nobody at the wheel, a counterfeit form that prints nine honest-looking lines with no fold in it, and a good administrator's nine-year-old rule about a piece of ballast. On the eighteenth of the last month a public yard of about nine hundred people watch him close one charter with one bearer, one activation and one term of twelve minutes, and the road opens and comes down and seventeen people are on the far bank who do not know why they went, and the term is held at the limit and a chandler's coal does not go, and the token comes home without the bus with a chip out of its rim, and the last thing anybody in Alder Reach does about it is put it in a case in public view, describe it at a counter, and leave the book open.

## What Alder Reach has at the end of the volume

- **A public crossing happened once**, on the eighteenth of the last month: one activation, twelve minutes, one bearer, no extension, no automatic renewal. **Twenty-six offered, twenty-four travelling, two declines entered and neither struck. Twenty-four outward tolls, seven return tolls, thirty-one in all, seventeen people on the far bank.** The shelter group is a count and not a household, the toll is charged to a person and not to a thing, and a returning traveller pays again and the object has still never said what a second payment takes.
- **The Hearth Line is administratively possible, finite, and answerable to more than one steward. It is not dependable.** The official span is shut to all carriage with its certificate suspended by two people who are not Selik Marne, and the permanent-office proposal that would have made the crossing a service instead of a claim is tabled and not adopted and not dead. The middle plank of the only remaining footway is at the bottom of the service cut. **Every further crossing is a new proposal, a new closing, two fresh witnesses, a public yard, and a day somebody has to stand in front of.**
- **Lot 17 has the first public lot book in the district**, in nine copies on eleven boards, read back in full twice in a kitchen with two people in it who are not clerks. It records use, toll, term, release condition and appeal path in one place in that order; the release condition read aloud at the opening and at the closing by a different person each time; a public refusal with no reason owed; no successful crossing treated as a transferable right; **failures entered as failures of the instrument with no entry struck**; **no goods, no freight, and no carriage except the person who is carried as a person**; a remedy that is known to fail and is recorded as not discharged; a held asset of 1,536 coppers that is neither a payment nor a security; a question asked of the brass; a count with a refusal in it; and a custody with a condition in it. **It is also the first thing in Alder Reach a hostile stranger can take cold and use**, and that is not a defect anybody has solved.
- **A registrar's sealed-page register still has one changed cell** and she has said in public, and will keep saying, that she cannot certify her own instrument. **A child is dead with no name in any public record.** A man of a protected household has an ankle that turns on wet ground. A woman at the far receiving ramp has said no to being asked and must not be asked again. **The remedy for all three was called and not discharged.**
- **The counterfeit is still winning on speed** and forty-one people bought an honest queue off a man who printed the toll on the first page, and the news of the crossing reached the terraces two hours before the closing was entered in the book.
- **A reading of a line on a certified gauge housing at twenty-three minutes to eight on the morning of the nineteenth, and a bus that came out of a Fault with nobody in the driver's seat and is now the district's only vehicle.** Three unexplained things, none joined to any other, and the volume's method is that nobody joins them.

## Adrian's position

**Still a Stage 1 Reader, and the volume did not promote him, and the book says so in the same line as every figure he has ever entered.** He can close one charter with one bearer, a short term and a visible toll, and on the eighteenth he did exactly that in front of about nine hundred people. He cannot split a use, transfer it, renew it, or post security. He has paid two hours of reading on the crossing day alone. **The volume's change in him is not a stage and is not a reading: it is that he handed a thousand five hundred and thirty-six coppers of his own inventory into a steward's hands in daylight on the twelfth, refused the steward's key back on the twenty-sixth in eleven seconds, held a limit in the rain in front of nine hundred people and was then told by a Warden administrator that he held it for the wrong reason, and did not defend himself.** He is told to his face that a limit checked by one man is a man and not a limit, and he agrees it is fair.

## The institutions the district built for itself

- **The proposal schedule** of seven items, and an **eighth line** that no carriage is permitted except a person carried as a person, moved by Marda Osk out of a counterfeit's second page and paid for by six clinic hands carrying cold boxes they cannot put down.
- **The public board**: four seats and no owner, which can refuse to record a closing, publish a result, call a review, uphold a challenge in part, and set or renew a short public custody term, and which cannot grant a use, waive a toll, audit a security, or strike a line off a notice board. It built a terms-closing out of its own powers, refused a sentence about a place, performed a closing with a condition unmet and entered the fact, published that a damaged object is a lot with a condition and not evidence, and refused to let an answer be a revelation.
- **The no-name remedy** and **the split-use remedy**, entered known to fail, called twice, and not discharged either time.
- **A mark is a refusal, not a person, and may not carry a job.** Nessa Pell's index of every use of a mark since the fourteenth found the double use in an hour and ten minutes and is still the only piece of administration anybody asked for. **A mark was retired in this volume**, filed off a pin, and the retirement is in the index with its date.
- **A person is accounted for by a count, a destination and a remedy, and not by a household.** A steward may not count the people in a group who are not in a household unless they ask; anybody may say no; the count is published with the decline in it; **a wrong count is not struck.** And this volume's last count is the first one in the district that has ever contained a refusal.
- **A payment on an unstated claim** at a figure the yard is invited to check, with the shortfall entered as uncomputed and not nil. **A held asset**, which is neither. **Joss Amery paid**, for the worst possible reason, which is that he can be found.
- **The claim-comparison table** and **disclosure that travels to the person**, built so that no courier is ever in a room where a reading happened.
- **The ward market's paper levy**, four coppers a quarter for eleven years for paper that does not exist, voted on the thirteenth at **forty-one** in columns with three unassigned coppers and a reason, funding a published inspection and a public reader at a counter, and **refusing nine coppers more for a standing crew at St. Orra on the ground that the thing has no day on it.**
- **A question put to the object**, with its limits written into the book before it was asked, and the standing finding that **a reply given without a question is not a promise, and six of them were given to this lot.**
- **A lot with a condition**, inspectable on stated days, describable at a counter, on a short term renewed in public with the hour published, and **a key that rotates**, held from the twenty-sixth by a cooper's wife who cannot say why she got on a bus and who asked what she is to do if the term expires and nobody comes.

## The relationships, as the volume leaves them

- **Adrian and Mara Quill.** Further apart in law than at any point in the volume and closer in practice, and **not one word said about it, in public or otherwise, in either direction.** She holds a book he cannot post into; he asked a question she built the instrument for; they pass a slate in a kitchen at a quarter to seven in the morning. **Neither of them is the other's instrument and neither of them has become one.**
- **Adrian and Tarin Kest.** Operational partners with a real argument that is not resolved. Tarin keeps the terms; Adrian keeps the limit; Selik says the limit was checked by one man; Tarin says the timetable has got a committee and he does not know whether that is better.
- **Adrian and Orren Pike.** An uneasy apprenticeship in inspection and record repair. Orren brings the eleven lines and Adrian does not hire him and does not make him safe.
- **Adrian and Lina Vale.** A negotiated public participation that preserves her right to remain unrecorded. She books the autumn, writes a line on her wall in chalk, and has not been thanked and will not be.
- **Adrian, Selik and Halloway.** Central control, concealed sale, and public limitation. **None of the three is defeated.** Selik's span is the correct alternative and his safety argument is respected in the last chapter. Halloway is honest on an arch and right about everything and has forty-one customers.
- **Rell Bode, Fen Ambrose, Nessa Pell, Bett Ruhl, Rutt, Ivo Stenn, Teo, Hester Vane.** The people who did the actual work of the last three weeks and are not a single one of them a protagonist.

## The three unexplained things, and the ending that is not the ending

1. **A bus came out of a Fault with nobody in the driver's seat** after eleven hours and forty minutes. It is now the district's only vehicle and has carried twenty-four people through a route and come back with its engine running, and **its use is ordinary and its arrival is still unexplained**, and nobody has joined it to anything.
2. **A certified gauge housing drew a mark at twenty-three minutes to eight on the morning of the nineteenth**, on a shut and empty span, on a trace with no instrument on it, and two fitters who are not Selik Marne suspended the certificate under the water office's own clause, and the span is shut, and nobody can say why.
3. **A foot of the crown of a service cut went down at four minutes past eleven** on the seventh with a man standing on it — **and that one is accounted for, by a clause Adrian wrote at two in the morning, and the accounting is a fact about paperwork and not about the ground.**

**The volume ends on a custody term about to expire and a key in a cooper's wife's coat, and on four words.** The next opening of the route is on somebody else's word and not on Adrian's, and the book says so, and nobody is going to be told a date by a man with a token.

## What Volume 02 inherits

- A term that expires on the **third of the new month** and a holder who must be named before the key is held or it is not held.
- Seventeen people on a far bank and a passage that takes two and a half days.
- A public span that is shut and correct, an order that is right, and a permanent office that is tabled and alive.
- A counterfeit's best argument, which is that on the morning a public road fails a book does not open a door.
- A remedy that fails on a stated day and a woman at a river stairs who says a book with a drawer is a book with a drawer.
- A limit checked by one man, and a Warden administrator who has said in public that it should be replaced by a room.
