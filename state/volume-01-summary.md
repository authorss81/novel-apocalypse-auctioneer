# Volume 01 Rolling Summary — The First Hammer

## Current position

Chapters 1–30 are complete. Alder Reach has crossed St. Orra three times with the Last Fare, failed on the fourth, found the nine, buried a child with no name in a public book, and published the whole of it. **The official bridge is shut to all carriage with its instrument certificate suspended by the water office over Selik's own written instruction to leave the pilot attached. The public road is suspended. The middle plank of the only remaining footway is at the bottom of the service cut.** Nineteen people were walked home over that footbridge in two days by a steward who put her own name in a public book, and the way they came is gone. Lot 17 is poorer, emptier, and considerably more honest, and the counterfeit is now a service race that Halloway Reed says out loud he is winning on speed.

## Established pressure

- The Last Fare opens one route across St. Orra for twelve minutes and takes the memory of why each traveler is traveling, **per traveler per leg**. It is **cracked through the plate after four activations** — one bare and failed in the flood, three witnessed — and **it is not known whether it opens again.** It returns to the public case at the closing whether or not the vehicle does, and it has now answered five unasked questions in short plain lines without ever saying what a second payment of the toll takes.
- **Tolls are counted by leg, and travelers and tolls separately.** Ch 9 outward: 16 travelers, 18 tolls. Ch 16 return: 16 distinct people, 18 tolls. Ch 19 outward: **29 offered, 28 travelled, 28 tolls, no return leg at all.** **19 stranded + 9 unaccounted for = 28. All 9 were found on the twentieth; 8 lived and 1 child died. All 19 were walked home over the footbridge on the eleventh and twelfth. Nobody came home before that.**
- Geography: Lot 17, the St. Orra staging yard, the marked spur, the old Ward Nine platform and its bell post are on the eastern bank; the Ward Nine district, its fever ward, the receiving stations, the far receiving ramp and the far school house are on the older central bank. **Every charter and book entry carries the bank in the text.**
- The four places stay distinct: the **loading spur / marked spur** is the near-side track behind the old school shelter; the **maintenance spur** is the near-side place the bus was found, a few streets west of the depot, behind a service gate; the pre-Fracture **maintenance bay** is beyond the river wall on Tarin's working card; the **service approach** is Selik's span, shut.
- **The cause of the failed return is the movement of the marking.** A hand was in the bell-post socket, inside a day or two of the crossing. **Who, how, and why are not known.** One nailed-heel print going toward the river wall belongs to nobody. The nine-line return term now in the book answers it procedurally: *if the marking is not where the pair says it is, the joint is the return and the arm is not.*
- **The pilot line.** A certified gauge housing on a shut, empty span drew a mark at 7:37 on the morning of the nineteenth on a trace with no instrument attached to it. The certificate is suspended, the span is shut, and nobody can say why. **Two unexplained things within three hundred yards of each other in one week, joined in no document.**
