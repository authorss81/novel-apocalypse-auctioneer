**486 — Two Answers In One Yard.** A man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up asks, in front of about nineteen people, whether a figure nobody has to put down and a figure three columns of a wall are carrying a day out can stand in one yard at once; a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale answers that they can and that she would not build a yard that could not hold both, and says she had argued the other way for six weeks and had been wrong by a thumb; a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally says he is not going to tell nine people which of the two is worth more.
