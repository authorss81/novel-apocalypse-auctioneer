## Chapter 0201 — The First Of April

**The month turns, and it is said once, in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about eight in the morning, and the board outside the room has carried the twenty-fourth of December for ninety-eight days and the train on the siding has stood four hundred and fourteen days.** The eighteenth year after the Long Fracture, and the derivation of the train's day-count is restated with the year named, because **a count taken inside the eighteenth year alone gives forty-eight and is wrong.** A clerk of nineteen years entered the turn and entered that the month before it was the third and the one before that the second, and that nobody asked. **The word steward is asked out loud: it is a word, a date of the thirty-first of March, and a name in the ward market's own book, and the date has passed, and the second half of the word is a season and the season started this morning and nobody has stood in a frame since the nineteenth of October, and a clerk entered that this is not a delay for the third time in eleven weeks and was not asked to.** The woman of fifty-eight is asked in the six things, in front of about nine people, for the third time and it is not a new question, **and she says in sixty-four words that she cannot say what a day of it is worth and is not going to take a thing she cannot say the worth of, and a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said on the thirty-first of the third month that she has offered her four houses and cannot weigh a day.** **The fifth of the five things this district does not have comes out of the mouth of a person who weighs sacks for a living and the count did not move.** The six households at the sluice end stand at one signed, two refused, three not asked again, and **a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally for six households says a man who is not asking has not been refused, and both halves went into the minute in their own words, and the record says not asked.** A man of about thirty-one goes out at first light with a stub about the size of a coin. **A route keeper walks nine miles and says in forty-five words that somebody opened his road on the night of the twenty-second of the second month, that it was not him, that he has not seen who it was, and that he is not going to say a name.** The tank four miles down a lane is at nothing for the first time since the first of the second month and the crack is dry and a man of about fifty-five refuses a fifth mend for the third time in four months with no reason. **A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing says a man who is proud of a door is a man who has not counted what comes through it, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam says a person who opens a road and is not asked who opened it is not the same as a person who is not asked, and this district has spent four months finding that out about a name on a page and is now finding it out about a road.** The road is nine miles from the fourth holding on the delivery sheet and the two are a coincidence until somebody says otherwise. **AND ON THE FIRST OF THE FOURTH MONTH THE MONTH TURNED AND WAS SAID ONCE, THE BOARD HAD CARRIED THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF DECEMBER FOR NINETY-EIGHT DAYS, THE TRAIN HAD STOOD FOUR HUNDRED AND FOURTEEN DAYS, A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SIX OFFERED A WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT FOUR HOUSES OF A ROUND AND COULD NOT SAY WHAT A DAY OF IT WAS WORTH, AND A ROUTE KEEPER SAID IN FORTY-FIVE WORDS THAT SOMEBODY HAD OPENED HIS ROAD ON A NIGHT IN THE SECOND MONTH AND IT WAS NOT HIM.**

## Chapter 0202 — A Man With A Portfolio

**A man of about forty-four comes up the lower terrace road at about half past eleven with a flat case the size of a hymn book, and walks past a yard with nine people in it to go to a door with a fanlight over it.** **A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing follows him at about eleven yards' distance without being asked to and without being told not to, and nobody stops him.** The name on the case is the name of a Registry, and a Registry is a body and not a house and not a person. **A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing says in the lane that a book a stranger can walk up to is a door, that we built it, that we told nineteen people it was the best instrument in this district, and that it is the best instrument in this district and it is a door. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam says eighteen months, four hundred yards, four hundred yards and a stranger, and that nobody has come through it who was not a neighbour or a creditor, and that the good half ran out this morning.** The man comes back into the yard at about twenty past twelve on his own account, puts the case on the trestle table, does not open it, and then opens it. **Eleven sheets. He turns one round so that the writing is the right way up for the people in the yard, and a man of fifty-six entered that a man who turns a paper round so that four people can read it has done one thing for free.** He says in thirty-nine words that he holds a renewal, that the lease it renews wants a bearer named, and that a Registry does not keep a book a stranger can walk up to and he has come to the one place in this basin that does. **The registrar of this district's records office comes into the yard on her own account, was not sent for, and says in sixty-four words that she will tell him what this office prints and not what is in it, and that an office may print a house and may not print a person, and that a name off a sheet is not a person who agreed to be there.** He does not say where he got the name. **She refuses him a page in about four seconds and gives the reason in thirty-seven words: you may look at anything in this office, you may not carry it out and you may not have a copy of a page, and that is not about you and is about the hand on it.** A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing says a man who will not say where he got a name is not the same as a woman who will not give a page, and the man who digs loam says they are the same, and **he says the two sentences out loud on purpose and the clerk entered that he did and that it was not asked for.** He is put in the six things: he does not answer the third, which is the year, and gives no reason. **He says in forty-one words that the lease names the place, that this is not the lease, that he is not going to say it today, that he is not refusing to say it, and that both of those are true — and a clerk entered that this district has a column for a person who will not read a thing and that the count in it is nine, and that it does not have a column for a person who will not name a place and never had one, and that the second is a hole and not a blank.** The sixth of the six is who he came for and he answers it before it is asked: **there is a man in this basin whose name I have and I did not get it here and I am not going to say where I got it and that is not a refusal, it is a door on my side.** He is sleeping at the second house up the lane with a sign over the door that is not a sign. **AND ON THE SECOND OF THE FOURTH MONTH A MAN CAME UP THE LOWER TERRACE ROAD WITH A PORTFOLIO AND WENT PAST A YARD WITH NINE PEOPLE IN IT TO A DOOR WITH A FANLIGHT OVER IT, THE REGISTRAR CAME INTO THE YARD ON HER OWN ACCOUNT AND REFUSED HIM A PAGE IN FOUR SECONDS AND GAVE THE REASON, AND HE SAID THAT HE HAD A NAME AND DID NOT SAY WHERE HE HAD GOT IT.**

## Chapter 0203 — The Renewal

**A clerk of nineteen years reads a stranger's sheet out in a yard in front of about nineteen people at about ten in the morning, and a man of fifty-six says a document is read out and not discussed, and that a sheet brought by a stranger is read the same way a sheet brought by a neighbour is read, and that the two being read the same is the only protection anybody in that yard has.** The sheet has three lines: **a lease is renewed; the term ended on the last day of the second month and this renewal is dated the first of the fourth month; the use is a road, the toll is paid at the ninth place, the holder is the Registrar-General, Cael Orin; the place is named on the lease, this is not the lease.** **A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and the man who digs loam say two halves of the same worry at the same time: the renewal is late, and nobody knows it is late because nobody has ever seen the day it was due on.** The third line is read again and he says in thirty-one words that he is not going to say it today and is not saying it today either and has not refused and is not going to refuse. **A clerk entered that this is the second time in two days that a person has said both halves of a refusal in the same breath, and that the second of those refusals has no count at all and that is a hole and not a blank.** There are ten other sheets: eight about places nobody here has heard of, a schedule, and a lease, and **the man says a stranger carried ten documents into a yard and one was read and nine were not, and a document a stranger will not read out in a yard is a document the stranger owns and is not a fourth of the three nobody owns.** The man who digs loam says the ninth place on the delivery sheet is the one nobody can account for and that he has been to that counter eleven times in eight weeks and been refused five times with no reason, and that he is not saying those are the same. **A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said in the lane at about half past three that a name said once in a yard is a name four hundred people have, and that this district has had one of those since the twentieth of December and did not know it was the good kind. The name was said once and not written down, and the minute says a holder and does not carry the name.** In the lane at the sluice end, the six households stand where they stood, and **the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat, one of the two that refused, says in eighteen words that she would rather be in the water with her name not written on anything than pay for it.** **The man of about thirty-one comes home at about half past two with a strip of paper about the length of his hand with a line ruled across it and a word at the top and nothing under the line, puts it on the table unasked, and says in fifty-one words that the counter took his name off the sheet in about nine seconds, cost nothing, asked nothing, and that he has not been released and has been found.** A clerk entered that a man has been found and not released and that the two are not joined, and that **a name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on, and what came off here is not the figure and is the name, and the figure of twenty-one years came off this district's page on the thirty-first of March in the same line as the date and was not asked for.** He says in thirty-four words that it can be taken off where they put it on and not by him, and that he is not glad, and that he is not going to be asked what he is going to do with it. **And the Registry man, at about four in the afternoon, asked what a Registry is for, says in thirty-one words that a Registry holds records of holdings, that that is all it does, that it is not a small thing to do, and that it is not a thing you can do in a yard.** **AND ON THE THIRD OF THE FOURTH MONTH A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS READ OUT A SHEET OF THREE LINES AND THE THIRD OF THEM SAID THAT THE PLACE IS NAMED ON THE LEASE AND THAT THIS IS NOT THE LEASE, THE HOLDER ON THE SECOND LINE IS THE REGISTRAR-GENERAL, CAEL ORIN, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE CAME HOME WITH A STRIP OF PAPER THAT SAYS HIS OWN NAME CAME OFF A SHEET AND SAID IN FIFTY-ONE WORDS THAT HE HAD BEEN FOUND AND NOT RELEASED.**

## Chapter 0204 — The Bearer

**A Registry asks a yard for a person, in the six things, at about ten in the morning in front of about nineteen people, and the man with the portfolio is not sent for and comes.** He says in thirty-seven words that a Registry does not name a bearer, that a lease names a bearer, and that a lease is a page in a book you may look at and may not carry out, and that that is the same rule yours has. **A man of about thirty-four who digs loam says the answer out loud before the man with the portfolio can get to it: the man at the back of the book, there is one of him in this district and he is standing in this yard and everybody in it knows it and two people thirty-four miles east and four hundred yards from here know it and neither of them was in this yard when it happened.** A clerk entered that he said the whole of a thing in a yard that had taken four months and eleven pages to arrive at and that she is not going to put a number on it. **Adrian says no himself, unasked, in sixty-seven words: he stood at the back of a book about water because he could not list what he had posted, he has listed it since, this is a different thing, and a man who cannot say no to a stranger with a portfolio is not a man who is standing at the back of a book, he is a person holding a piece of paper.** A man of fifty-six says that is the second time a man has refused a bearing in eleven days and that the first was a route keeper standing on a bank saying nothing. **The man who digs loam says in twenty-two words that he has said no and said why and that the why is the only reason anybody in this yard has given for anything in four months that was not about a figure.** A man of fifty-six says a man who brought a lease into a yard and was asked for a bearer by a yard and did not need one is a man who came here to be asked, **and a body which sends a man to be asked is a body that expects a no and has a paper for the no.** The Registry man then says, unasked and uncounted, **that the bearer does not have to be from this district, and that it is on the second line, and that it is a fact about the page and not a rule, and that he has not read the page and the yard cannot read it either.** **The registrar refuses in four seconds, in seventy words, not to have this district entered in a Registry, not to have a man in the yard entered under a holder, and not to have the ninth place entered under a toll.** Then she says one more thing to him and not to the yard and nobody counts it: **you walked past a yard with nine people in it this morning to go to a door with a fanlight over it, there are two doors in this district with a fanlight over them, you went to the wrong one for what you came for, and I am not going to tell you that in a yard.** **A man of about thirty at the counter comes down the eleven mile road for the tenth time in eight weeks with four sheets, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing says in thirty-nine words that a man carrying sheets for a house and a man carrying a case for a Registry are two functionaries and that neither of them is a party and that a yard is a room and a room is not a party and is not a seventh of the six instruments.** He is asked about the ninth holding for the sixth time in eleven weeks and refuses with no reason, **making six refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and six are six and are not a column and are not added to the thirteen.** He is asked a seventh, new thing — where the four sheets go when he has put them down — and does not answer, and he looks at the man who digs loam and then at the man with the portfolio and **the two men did not look at each other at any point in the afternoon.** The nine holdings are read out at about three in the afternoon: **one, two and three hold and take, four is the top field four miles out and has not taken a drop since the first of the second month, five and six hold and take, seven was given up on the twenty-fourth of the second month and has a bar down, eight holds, nine is the one nobody can account for.** The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale says in thirty-nine words that she has weighed nine sacks a day for nine years and has never weighed a day. **The man of about thirty-one says in thirty-eight words that the strip of paper is in his coat, that he is not going to pin it up, not going to burn it, and not going to have it read out in a yard again, and that he has had it read out in a yard and it did not help.** **AND ON THE FOURTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM SAID IN A YARD THAT THE BEARER OF A LEASE ANYBODY WANTS IS THE ONLY PERSON IN THIS DISTRICT WHO STANDS AT THE BACK OF A BOOK, AND THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST REFUSED IN SIXTY-SEVEN WORDS AND GAVE A REASON, AND THE REGISTRAR REFUSED IN FOUR SECONDS TO HAVE THE NINTH PLACE ENTERED UNDER A TOLL.**

## Chapter 0205 — The Ninth Row

**The fourth of the eleven sheets is read and it is a schedule and not a lease, at about ten in the morning, in front of about nineteen people.** A sheet with nine rows ruled across it, a column on the left with a number in each row, and a column on the right with a heading over it. **The heading is one word and it says OPEN.** The schedule reads: **the schedule of nine gates, the first is at the first place and the ninth is at the ninth place; eight of the nine carry a day in the column headed OPEN, the ninth carries a word; a word in a column headed OPEN is not a day and is not a figure and the schedule does not say what it is.** A man of about thirty-four who digs loam says a row that says it is not a road is a thing that rhymes with a holding nobody can account for and **a rhyme is not a join and he is not going to make it one at ten in the morning.** The man with the portfolio says in five words that it is not a road. **The panel at about eleven says that a schedule that names eight roads names eight things and not nine, that the ninth is not unnamed but named as something that is not a road, and that naming a thing as what it is not is a finding and not a blank; and that a person may be asked for a bearing once in the same six things and not twice, and that a second asking is not a new question and is not added to any count in this district.** A man of about thirty-four who digs loam **disagrees with the last line out loud, in twenty-three words: a second asking on the next day is a new question if the answer changed, and mine changed, and I gave a reason.** The board has carried the twenty-fourth of December for **a hundred and two days** and has four dates on it and no fifth, **and the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and has nothing on it and has been ruled since the second of January.** Nobody put a line on a board on any of the five days from the first of April. **The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale asks what is opening, having heard the word OPEN read out four times, and the man with the portfolio says in twenty-three words that his Registry calls them gates, that the word on his sheet is gates, and that the word on the yard's sheet is not on his sheet.** A man of fifty-six says this district has been using the Registry's word for four days without knowing it was the Registry's word, **and that a word a stranger lends you is a word a stranger can take back, and that nobody in this yard had asked where the word came from and that the asking was done by a man who keeps a scale at a weighbridge because a column said OPEN.** A man of fifty-six says a man who keeps a road is nine miles off and would be the man to ask and **nobody sent for him and the record says not asked.** The man of about thirty-one is asked what a person at the back of a book is for and says in forty-four words: **I do not know. I said on the thirtieth of the third month that it stops at me and I said the same thing to nine people and I have had four days to find out whether I meant it and I did not.** The man who digs loam says the fifth of the five is a man and was never a bearer and that he is going to stop saying that, **because a man who is a security and is not a bearer is exactly the thing the ninth row is, and I am not joining them and I am not letting go of that.** The man of about thirty-six says he has been in this yard three days, said OPEN four times, and been asked what was opening by himself, and that he is not going to be made a finding. **The man of about fifty-five, asked whether the water that came down a channel on the twenty-ninth of the third month came from above him or from below him, says in forty-six words that he has watched that tank for nine years and has never once been able to say where the water came from, and that a channel is a channel and the road beside it is a road and the two of them meet about a mile up.** He then says in thirty-five words that he said no to a fifth mend in January and is going to say no to a fifth mend in April, and that a tank with a mark on it is a tank he can see and a tank with a mend in it is a tank that comes out in three pieces. **At about half past four the Registry man is asked, as the sixth of the six, when the lease has to be answered, and gives the last day of the fourth month, and says in twenty-five words that he has to be at the place on it and there is nobody at the place but me.** **A man of fifty-six says a stranger has now given this district a day for the first time in four months and that a day is a figure and a figure is not a schedule, and that the man has been in this yard four days and has cost this district a page, a name, a word it had been using and a morning, and that the district is not going to count any of that because a clerk of nineteen years would have to have a column and there is not one.** **AND ON THE FIFTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS READ OUT A SCHEDULE OF NINE ROWS UNDER A COLUMN HEADED OPEN, EIGHT OF WHICH CARRY A DAY AND THE NINTH OF WHICH CARRIES THE WORDS IT IS NOT A ROAD, A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM DISAGREED WITH THE LAST LINE OF THE PANEL THAT CAME AT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING IN TWENTY-THREE WORDS, A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SIX WHO KEEPS A SCALE ASKED WHAT WAS OPENING, AND THE MAN FROM THE REGISTRY SAID THAT THE LAST DAY OF THE FOURTH MONTH WAS THE DAY HE HAD TO BE AT A PLACE WHERE THERE WAS NOBODY BUT HIMSELF.**

## Chapter 0206 — What A No Does

**A man says no for the second time and nothing happens, and the man with the portfolio is on his fourth day with a bench outside the door with the fanlight over it that nobody gave him.** A clerk entered that a stranger has slept three nights at his own cost and has not been charged and has not asked. **The list of the five is read out again: a charter of four sheets closed on the eighteenth of December with a page of it in the ward market's own book and no word certified on any of the four; a third form of a withdrawal notice, twenty-nine words on its face, printed and fixed to a counter and not kept by a person; a food agreement of six sheets on seasonal terms of the seventh and eighth of December, witnessed by two people who were not in the room when the reason was read out, and the sheet says so on its face; a receipt in a building thirty-four miles east that a stranger may read and may not walk off with; and a man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row.** **The man with the portfolio says in fifteen words that it is a good list and that his is nineteen pages and none of it is that, and a man of fifty-six entered that this is the first time in four months a man from outside this basin has said anything about this district that was not an ask.** The man who digs loam says **a Registry does not want a list, a Registry wants a person, this yard has spent four months getting to the end of a list, and a stranger walked in and asked for the one thing the list was never going to be.** They ask him again, on a new day, what a bearer is, and he says no again in fifty-eight words: **I said no on Thursday in sixty-seven words and I am saying no again in fewer and I am not going to be asked a third time this week, and a man who says no twice in a yard has not stopped anything and I would like that written down in a line where somebody will find it.** A man of fifty-six says in seventy-two words, at a counter's voice, that **a no in a yard is a thing that happened, it is not a thing that holds, the lease is on a page a stranger may look at and may not carry out and it does not know this yard happened, and a man who says no in front of nineteen people believes he has done something and he has done one thing and it is not the thing.** The man who digs loam says the part that had not been said: **he is the only person in this district who can be fastened to a toll, he has said no in a yard twice, a house thirty-four miles east already knows his name, and the only reason that has not cost anything this week is that the house has not asked for it. That is not a defence. That is a week.** The man of about twenty-three who copies for nothing says in twenty-three words that he has asked for eleven coppers a week three times and been refused three times for the same reason, that there is no money in this district that anybody can pay anybody with, and **that he is not asking today and is telling the yard the number so that it knows he knows the number.** Then he does the thing nobody asked for: **he copies the schedule of nine rows in about fifty minutes, in his own hand, on the back of a sheet of his own buying, column heading and eight days and the ninth row with its two words, and gives it to a clerk of nineteen years and says twenty-one words: I gave it up, nobody took it, put both of those down and put down which one of them is true.** A clerk read the copy against the sheet and got nothing wrong. **The man who digs loam says a man who copies a stranger's schedule for nothing is the fourth instrument this district has built and not named and that he is not going to count him, and the count is six.** The registrar says in fifty-three words: **I will not put a holder in this book. I will not put a toll at the ninth place in this book. I will put a day and a sheet and a person who came and asked, and the fourth of those three things is the only one of them that is mine.** **She did not look at him and he did not look at her, and a clerk entered that this was not one of the nine times they had been in the same yard on purpose and is not going to be entered as one.** The man of about thirty at the counter comes for the eleventh time in eight weeks, is asked one question and not asked about the ninth holding, and says in **four seconds** that his house had never heard of a Registry. **A clerk entered that he said it in four seconds and that the registrar said no in four seconds on the fifteenth of the second month and that a clerk is not going to put a number on the two, and that the two bodies are not joined and the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve and neither of them moved.** A man of fifty-six says two bodies that have never heard of each other are going to meet over a place with no name on a sheet, **and that this district has four documents nobody owns and none of them is a prediction.** **AND ON THE SIXTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH THE LIST OF THE FIVE WAS READ OUT AGAIN IN A YARD AND A MAN FROM A REGISTRY SAID IN FIFTEEN WORDS THAT IT WAS A GOOD LIST AND THAT HIS OWN WAS NINETEEN PAGES, THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST SAID NO FOR THE SECOND TIME IN FIFTY-EIGHT WORDS AND ASKED FOR IT TO BE WRITTEN DOWN, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM SAID THAT A NO IN A YARD IS A THING THAT HAPPENED AND IS NOT A THING THAT HOLDS.**

## Chapter 0207 — The Fifth Day

**The seventh of April, the fifth day of the man with the portfolio, about fourteen people in the yard. The board has carried the twenty-fourth of December for a hundred and four days, the train has stood four hundred and twenty days, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and thirty-four.** The man of about thirty-one says in twenty-one words that the man on the bench has been there since eight, has not asked anybody for anything, and that he has asked him for nothing. **The panel at about eleven says: a lease names a holder and a lease does not name a bearer; a bearer is a person who owns a toll under a stated term; a holder is a name a body has printed, and a name a body has printed is not a person who agreed to be there.** The man of about thirty-one disagrees with the whole of it before he is asked, in forty-four words: **I have been written on four times and I did not ask for any of them and I am not a holder because I do not pay a toll and I am not a bearer because nobody has asked me, and I would like somebody to write down that I do not know the difference and that the four of them do not know it either.** **The registrar puts the first question she has ever put to the stranger, in thirteen words: does a lease say who keeps the thing it is a lease of.** He says no in four seconds and gives the reason in fifty-six words: **a lease says what the holder may do with it; a keeper is not in a lease; a keeper is a person who is on a hill and is not on a page, and there are four generations of them where this lease is and none of them has ever been on a page of mine.** **That four generations is the first stranger's figure entered in this yard, and it came with no date, no year and no place, and it is a rhyme and not a join.** Then a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asks, in the six things, what a Registry does with a no, and the man on the bench comes into the yard for the first time in five days and says in thirty-four words: **we enter it; that is what a Registry does with a no; it writes the no down in the place the asking came from and it dates it and the asking is the answer.** And then he says the rest of it, unasked, and it is the turn of the block: **I did not come for a bearer. ## Chapter 0260 — The Difference Between The Two Numbers

**The tenth of the sixth month, hot by nine, about nineteen people in the yard at ten, and the chair forty-eight days old and unsat in on any of the forty-eight.
