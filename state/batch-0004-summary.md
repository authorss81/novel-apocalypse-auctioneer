# Batch 0004 Summary — The First Hammer

## Batch outcome

Chapters 31–40 close out Batch 0003's payment, put Selik's permanent-route argument in front of a yard instead of a chamber, build a claim-comparison service that discovers it cannot reach people, convene the people the Hearth Line touches, write eight existing terms into one place, split the route, get the count wrong, injure a man who cannot be named, and reopen the public lot book in front of him.

**There is no fifth activation in this batch and no chapter reaches for the token.** The token is in a locked case throughout, on a seven-day custody term that lapses, is renewed, and is renewed again. The batch's only reading of the object is Chapter 40's three lines off an open page, and the last of them is `THE OBJECT WAS NOT ASKED.`

The batch's cost is a real body: a man of a protected household in the service-cut yards, standing on ballast past a Warden's rule, because a schedule in a public book said where a man would be standing. He is not in any public book by name. The remedy for him is a hearing that is called and not discharged, and the registrar entered her expectation of that failure in advance, in her own hand, on the eighth.

## Plan deviations

The prose is canon. Where it departs from `workspace/volume-01/batch-0004/PROMPT.md`, it departs like this.

- **The multi-bearer failure does not happen at a closing of a real charter, because no charter can be closed.** The prompt asked for a first multi-bearer public auction that fails. With no token, no vehicle, and a Stage 1 reader who cannot close a split, the batch instead builds a **terms-closing** — a record published by a board that confers no use, seats no token, and is made entirely of powers the board already has (publish a result, take two fresh witnesses, call a review). The card's own legal innovation is preserved and built over two chapters, and the card's warning that it must be built by a board in public and not announced is obeyed: **the instrument is built on the morning of the sixth by the sitting steward in a yard, and Adrian arrives with the problem rather than the answer.** The failure happens in the first hour of the closing, at a ward market counter, and the board suspends it. Nothing is concealed and no toll is concealed, because nothing is performed.
- **Adrian's Chapter 40 posting is the lease, the salvage and a two-season named repair-crew term, and the reason the lease still exists is stated in the chapter.** The prompt noted that the inspection press has already been given away, so the posting had to be a different asset. It is, and the batch found the mechanism: **a payment conveys nothing, because the claim it was offered against was never stated and there is no body to buy from it.** So the lease Adrian entered in his payment of the twenty-ninth was never sold, is still in his name, and is the only thing of value he has. The press is gone, the payment was uncomputed, and the difference between a payment, a security and a held asset is the batch's technical spine.
- **The four bearers are not four drivers.** The prompt's Ch 35 beat required four named bearers and a rotating return crew, and required the standing sentence to be the trouble. It is: Tarin is the person half of the named pair and is entered **over his own refusal**; Renn Fisk is the near abutment; **Dessa Rill is entered and is expressly not a bearer** (she asked for that in Ch 34 and it is the canon rule that a remembering passenger is a condition on the use); and the fourth is a **mark**. Adrian's schedule therefore names three people and a mark, and the fourth of the four is the mark, which is what makes the closing unperformable and what makes Chapter 39's wrong bearer the same household's other member. The rotating return crew is Adrian's fifth entry and the reason Mara says "that is five."
- **The wrong bearer and the injured man are one household and two men, and neither is the man Fen asked on the far bank.** The prompt's Ch 36 beat needs a person nobody counted who chose not to be counted (the woman at the kettle, unnamed, who asked that the only thing entered because she said nothing be that she said nothing) and Ch 39 needs a wrong bearer found and a person hurt. The prose separates them cleanly: the *uncounted* person is the woman at the ramp; the *wrong bearer* is the line-holder of the service-cut yards, who is of the same protected household as the fourth entry, so **one mark did two jobs six days apart**, which is the mechanism that makes the whole failure a governance failure rather than an error.
- **Selik's review of his own order is set on the day of the paper-levy vote, and neither event has happened yet.** The prompt asked for a stated date; the prose gives one and then leaves both the review and the rate meeting as a live hook for Chapters 41–50, which is where Teo's question from Chapter 34 — *who tells four thousand households, and for how long, if the crossing never opens* — has to be answered or refused.
- **Halloway does not appear.** The prompt required him to be better again and not humiliated, not to be in this batch. He said it in Chapter 28 and it stands; his technique and his argument are used against him by proxy in Chapters 31, 32 and 38 and he is not named in any of them, which is the correct handling and leaves Volume 02 free to put him on a rail.
- **Hask Vell is exposed by a rent receipt, not by a reading.** The prompt asked for a disclosure that travels to the person and a claim that is entirely legal and still costs seven days of a household's labour.
